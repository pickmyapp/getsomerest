How to choose earplugs for sleeping next to a snoring partner

In this guide, earplugs for sleeping next to a snoring partner means reusable earplugs designed to reduce the sound of a snoring partner enough that you can sleep comfortably through the night. Most buying mistakes come from skipping the same few checks, so run through the list below before you compare prices.

  • Price: compare the real checkout price, including shipping, not just the list price. Loop's official price is $24.95, but the Quiet 2 is frequently discounted to around $20 — check both Loop's site and Amazon before you buy.
  • Materials: look for clear details about fabrics and fill instead of vague marketing terms. For earplugs, this means the plug material (soft silicone, moldable putty, thermoplastic), whether filters are included, and whether the product is silicone-free if you have sensitivities.
  • Trial period: a longer home trial makes it easier to return something that doesn’t suit you. Loop offers a 100-night sleep trial when you buy direct — unusually generous for a $25 product — while drugstore plugs rely on standard retailer returns.
  • Warranty and returns: read the terms, including who pays for return shipping. Reusable plugs from Loop and Alpine carry a two-year warranty against defects, which matters when a plug is a nightly-use item.

How earplugs actually handle snoring — and their limits

To choose the right pair, it helps to know what you are up against. A quiet snore sits around 30 to 40 decibels — roughly a whisper. A loud, habitual snore reaches 50 to 70 decibels, about the volume of a conversation at the table or a running dishwasher. Severe snoring can spike to 80 decibels and above, which is closer to a vacuum cleaner running in the bedroom. The best sleep earplugs reduce sound by 22 to 28 decibels, which is why they turn most snoring from a sleep-breaking event into a background murmur — but they do not make it disappear.

The Noise Reduction Rating (NRR) or SNR number on the box tells you the laboratory maximum, not what you will get in bed. Real-world performance depends almost entirely on the seal. A moldable putty plug with a 22-decibel rating that fully seals your ear canal will outperform a 28-decibel filtered plug that loosens when you roll onto your side. That is why our top pick, the Loop Quiet 2, ships with four tip sizes: fit is the feature. Try every included size, wear them for at least an hour on the first night, and expect the first two or three nights to feel strange. Most people stop noticing earplugs by night four.

There is one honest limit to set expectations against. Snoring is low-frequency sound — the rumble travels partly through the air and partly through bone conduction, vibrating through the mattress and pillow into your skull. No earplug blocks bone-conducted sound, which is why the heaviest snoring may still register as a faint vibration even with a perfect seal. If your partner's snoring regularly wakes you despite good earplugs, the next step is not a better plug — it is addressing the snoring itself. Loud, persistent snoring with gasping or choking can be a sign of sleep apnea, and a doctor's visit matters far more than any accessory.

On the comfort question, side sleepers have the hardest time. Anything that protrudes from the ear canal gets pushed by the pillow, and pushing is what breaks the seal and causes soreness. Low-profile designs — the Loop's flush-fitting ring, moldable putty that sits inside the canal, Alpine's short thermoplastic body — are all engineered around this problem. If you sleep on your side and your current plugs ache by morning, the shape of the plug is almost certainly the issue, not your ears.

Earplug hygiene for nightly use

Something that lives in your ear canal eight hours a night needs a cleaning routine, or it becomes a small petri dish. Reusable silicone plugs are the easiest to maintain: rinse them with warm water and mild soap after every few nights, pat them dry with a clean towel, and let them air-dry completely before they go back in the case. A damp case breeds bacteria, so the case itself deserves a wash now and then. Alpine includes a cleaning accessory with the SleepSoft for exactly this reason, and Loop's one-piece silicone design rinses clean in seconds.

Moldable putty plugs like Mack's need different handling. The putty is meant to be reused for weeks, not months: replace a pair when it stops feeling tacky, looks visibly dirty, or loses its seal. Never try to wash putty plugs aggressively — you will just embed the debris deeper. And never use putty plugs if the packaging is damaged or the pair has picked up lint and dust it will not shed; the cost of a fresh pair is pennies per night.

Watch for the warning signs that mean a night off or a doctor's visit. Itching, soreness in the morning, unusual wax buildup, a feeling of fullness in the ear, or any discharge mean you should stop using earplugs until your ears settle. People with ear tubes, recent ear surgery or recurring infections should talk to a doctor before nightly use — this is the one category of earplug question where professional advice genuinely matters. Keep your pillowcases clean too; a fresh pillowcase does more for ear health than most people expect. With basic care, reusable plugs stay safe and effective for months, which is part of what makes them such a good value.

Frequently asked questions

Can earplugs actually block out snoring?

Earplugs reduce snoring significantly but rarely silence it completely. A loud snore can reach 50 to 70 decibels, and even the best sleep earplugs cut 20 to 28 decibels. That usually turns a jarring snore into a distant murmur most people can sleep through, especially combined with a white noise machine.

What NRR do I need for a snoring partner?

Look for a Noise Reduction Rating of at least 20 decibels for moderate snoring, and 24 decibels or more if your partner snores loudly. Just as important as the number is the seal: a 22-decibel plug with a perfect seal outperforms a 28-decibel plug that slips out at 3 a.m.

Are reusable earplugs better than disposable foam plugs for sleep?

For nightly use, yes. Foam plugs offer the highest raw noise reduction but dry out, get dirty and need replacing every few nights. Reusable silicone plugs like the Loop Quiet 2 or Alpine SleepSoft are washable, last for months and are far more comfortable for side sleepers.

Will I still hear my alarm with earplugs in?

Usually, yes — an alarm next to your bed is loud and close enough to come through most earplugs. Filtered plugs like the Alpine SleepSoft are specifically designed to let alarms stay audible while muffling background noise. To be safe, test any new pair on a night when you have a backup wake-up plan.

Is it safe to wear earplugs every night?

For most people, yes, as long as you keep them clean. Wash reusable plugs regularly, let your ears air out during the day, and stop using them if you notice pain, itching or unusual wax buildup. If you have ear tubes, recent ear surgery or recurring ear infections, ask your doctor before nightly use.
