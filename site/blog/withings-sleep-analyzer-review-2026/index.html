When an under-mattress tracker beats a wearable

The Sleep Analyzer's pitch is not better sensors — it is zero friction. Think about the failure modes of wearables: the ring you forgot to charge, the watch that dies at 11 p.m., the band that irritates your wrist. The mat has none of them: it slides under the mattress once (a ~10-minute setup with calibration), stays plugged in forever, and measures every night without you thinking about it.

What you gain: adherence. A tracker you never touch is a tracker you never abandon, and sleep data only matters in trends across weeks. The mat tracks duration, sleep onset and wake time, cycles (deep, light, REM), continuous and average heart rate, snoring duration, interruptions, regularity, and a nightly Sleep Score — a full dashboard, synced automatically over 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi (which switches off while you are in bed and syncs on waking).

What you lose: precision. Measuring through the mattress is inherently less direct than measuring at the skin, so stage-level accuracy trails a good wearable. It assumes stillness means sleep — reading in bed can register as light sleep — and it is single-user: a partner's movements can confuse the readings. One mat, one sleeper, one bed.

Who it's for: anyone who has bought a wearable and stopped wearing it; couples where one partner hates gadgets in bed; and data-curious sleepers who want trends, snore tracking, and breathing-disturbance awareness signals without a nightly charging ritual or a subscription.

Our verdict: who should buy the Sleep Analyzer

Buy it if you want sleep data with zero nightly effort. Nothing to wear, nothing to charge, no subscription for any core feature, free unlimited data storage, and Apple Health / Google Health Connect integration make it the lowest-friction tracker you can buy. At ~$129–$150 on sale, it costs less than a year of most wearable subscriptions — and it works every night, forever.

Skip it if you want lab-grade stage accuracy or share the bed. The through-the-mattress measurement trades precision for convenience, stillness gets mislabeled as light sleep, and a partner's movements can confuse it. Wearables (rings, watches) track stages better if you will actually wear them nightly — our subscription-free tracker roundup compares those.

The honest summary: the Sleep Analyzer's competition is not the Oura Ring — it is the drawer where abandoned wearables go. If that drawer sounds familiar, the mat wins.

Frequently asked questions

How does the Withings Sleep Analyzer work without wearing anything?

It is a thin pneumatic sensor that slides under your mattress at chest level and measures your body's movement through the mattress via ballistography: heart rate, respiratory rate, and movement patterns from which it derives sleep duration, sleep cycles (deep, light, REM), a nightly Sleep Score, snoring duration, and breathing-disturbance signals. A built-in sound sensor listens for snoring. It stays plugged in and syncs over Wi-Fi — you never charge it and never wear it.

Does the Withings Sleep Analyzer need a subscription?

No — and that is one of its strongest selling points. All core sleep-tracking features are free in the Withings Health Mate app (iOS/Android) with a free Withings account, which also includes unlimited online data storage. Withings+ is an optional paid add-on for extra guidance and insights, but the tracker works fully without it.

Can the Withings Sleep Analyzer detect sleep apnea?

Careful framing needed here. The device flags signs of sleep-disordered breathing (breathing disturbances and snoring) as awareness signals — reviewers describe it as clinically validated for moderate-to-severe OSA screening — but it is not a diagnosis and does not replace a professional sleep study. Its regulatory status varies by region (older listings noted US FDA status as pending). If you have concerns about your breathing at night, talk to a clinician.

What mattresses work with the Withings Sleep Analyzer?

Withings tested it on mattresses 4–15 inches (10–40 cm) thick — spring, latex, foam, and memory foam all work. It is not recommended for waterbeds or airbeds, and slatted frames need a flat rigid board under the sensor. One mat tracks one sleeper; a partner's movements can confuse the readings, so it is a single-user device.

Withings Sleep Analyzer vs a smart ring or watch: which tracks sleep better?

Wearables generally track sleep stages more precisely because they measure pulse directly from your skin; the Sleep Analyzer measures through the mattress, which trades some precision for zero-effort adherence — Withings cites high long-term use because there is nothing to charge or wear. If you will actually wear a ring or watch every night, a wearable wins on detail. If you won't, the mat's data beats the wearable you left on the nightstand.
