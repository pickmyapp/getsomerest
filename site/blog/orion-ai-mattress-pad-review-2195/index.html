Orion AI Mattress Pad Review: The $2,195 Sleep System TechCrunch Couldn't Hate (2026)

Orion is a $2,195 AI-powered mattress pad with a $250/year plan that learns your sleep and adjusts bed temperature all night. We reviewed the specs, the TechCrunch test, and the five-year cost math against Eight Sleep.

When TechCrunch's Amanda Silberling agreed to test Orion's $2,195 AI mattress pad, she expected a punchline. As her headline put it on September 18, 2026: she was mad that she loved it. Over a few months of testing, the phone-controlled pad — which cools the bed around bedtime, gets colder as you fall asleep and eases back toward room temperature at wake-up — delivered what she described as sleeping like a baby. This is the honest review: what Orion is, what the AI actually does, and whether the $2,195 price plus $250 a year makes sense against Eight Sleep's new Pod 6.

Quick answer

The Orion Sleep System ($2,195 + $250/year) is a legitimate rival to Eight Sleep: a phone-controlled dual-zone mattress pad with built-in sleep tracking, a 50–113°F range and an AI that adapts bed temperature through the night. It costs less up front than a Pod 6 Standard ($2,699) but more over five years than some rivals once the subscription compounds. Best for hot sleepers who want active cooling and like the AI-adaptive pitch; skeptics of the subscription can get most of the temperature benefit from our best cooling mattress toppers for a fraction of the price. The quick verdict

Orion is the real thing: not a gadget gimmick, but a full sleep system built around the single most controllable factor in sleep quality — bed temperature. The hardware is a cover that fits your existing mattress, paired with a control unit that heats and cools it. The software is the Orion Intelligence app, which tracks your sleep and shifts the night's temperature curve based on your data. TechCrunch's months-long test ended with the reviewer sleeping better and genuinely annoyed about it.

The catch is the price stack: $2,195 up front (launch pricing started at $2,295), plus $250 a year for the full system features. That's cheaper to enter than Eight Sleep's Pod 6 Standard at $2,699 — but the subscription math deserves a hard look, which we do below.

What the Orion Sleep System actually is

Three parts — hardware, AI, and onboarding — define what you're actually buying.

The hardware: a cover, not a mattress

Orion is not a mattress and doesn't pretend to be. It's a smart cover designed to fit any standard mattress, connected to a compact control unit that provides both heating and cooling. Dual-zone control means each side of the bed can run its own temperature — the headline spec is a 50°F to 113°F range, one of the widest we've seen. Setup is about 30 minutes according to hands-on testing. Built-in sensors track heart rate, breath rate and sleep patterns from the cover itself, so there's nothing to wear.

The AI: a temperature curve that learns

Here's what "AI-enabled" actually means on the Orion. The default program is schedule-based: around the bedtime you enter manually, the pad starts cooling; it gets colder as you fall asleep; then it drifts back toward room temperature before your wake time, which the reviewer found made getting out of bed easier. Where the AI comes in is adaptation: the app collects your tracking data night after night and shifts the cooling curve instead of running a fixed program — learning your thermal profile rather than following your initial settings forever. The reviewer was skeptical about how much the AI contributes versus plain scheduled cooling, and honestly, that's a fair skepticism we share.

The onboarding: a sleep optimization test

Before the system ships, you can complete an at-home Sleep Optimization Test that analyzes your natural temperature patterns, so the device arrives pre-programmed for your comfort rather than starting from a generic default. It's a genuinely thoughtful touch — most sleep tech asks you to endure weeks of mediocre defaults while it "learns." Orion also leans on accessibility positioning: lower EMF emissions, a compact footprint and financing options, from a company founded by 26-year-old Harry Gestetner, who previously sold creator platform Fanfix for an estimated $65 million and raised an $18 million seed round for Orion.

Sleeping on the pad feels like flipping your pillow to find "the cool side," except that now your whole bed is the cool side of the pillow.

The real cost: $2,195 plus $250 a year

Let's be blunt about the money, because this is where sleep tech purchases go wrong. Orion's year one runs $2,445 ($2,195 + $250), and five years lands around $3,445. That's meaningfully cheaper than the Pod 5 Plus system's five-year ~$5,095, and slightly under the Pod 6 Standard's ~$3,694 at base Autopilot pricing — but it's $1,250 of subscription over five years for features the hardware can't fully use without it.

Who should (and shouldn't) buy it

Buy the Orion if you're a genuinely hot sleeper — night sweats, a partner with a different temperature, a bedroom that never cools down — and you like the idea of the temperature curve adapting itself. It's also the right pick if Eight Sleep's $2,699+ entry point made you wince but you still want active, phone-controlled cooling.

Skip it if you just sleep "a bit warm." The honest hierarchy is: fix the room first (cool bedroom, right bedding), then a passive cooling topper, and only then a $2,195-plus-subscription system. Also skip it if a recurring fee for a mattress accessory bothers you on principle — our best sleep trackers without a subscription is worth a read for the zero-fee philosophy, and the same logic applies to temperature tech.

What about the AI — is it real or marketing?

Both, in the way all consumer AI is right now. The adaptive curve is a real feature: the app genuinely adjusts your nightly temperatures based on accumulated sleep data, and the pre-shipment Sleep Optimization Test means it starts from your profile rather than a generic default. But the scheduled cooling — cool at bedtime, colder in deep sleep, warmer toward wake — is doing most of the heavy lifting, and a fixed schedule would get you most of the benefit. We're rating the hardware and the temperature range more than the AI mystique.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the Orion AI mattress pad cost?

The Orion Sleep System costs $2,195 up front (launch pricing started at $2,295), plus $250/year for the full system features. Year one runs about $2,445 and five years about $3,445. Prices last checked October 15, 2026.

Is Orion better than Eight Sleep?

They're different answers to the same problem. Orion is cheaper up front ($2,195 vs $2,699+), publishes a wider temperature range (50–113°F) and fits your existing mattress. Eight Sleep's Pod 6 counters with 18 biometric sensors vs Orion's built-in tracking, a 50%-smaller under-bed hub and a longer track record. Hot sleepers win either way; pick Orion on price and range, Eight Sleep on sensing and maturity.

What does the Orion AI actually do?

The Orion Intelligence app collects your sleep and temperature data night after night and shifts the cooling curve — when it cools, how cold it gets, when it warms for wake-up — instead of running a fixed program. It also starts from your profile thanks to the pre-shipment Sleep Optimization Test. The scheduled cooling does most of the work; the AI refines it.

Do I need a new mattress for the Orion?

No. Orion is a smart cover designed to fit any standard mattress, paired with a compact control unit. That's one of its real advantages over full mattress replacements — and setup takes about 30 minutes.

Is the Orion a medical device?

No. Like Eight Sleep, Orion presents its sleep and biometric tracking for wellness and personal insight. It doesn't diagnose, treat or prevent any sleep disorder, and nothing here is medical advice — if you suspect a sleep condition, talk to a clinician.
