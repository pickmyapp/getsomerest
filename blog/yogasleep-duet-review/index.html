Yogasleep Duet Review: Is the Upgrade Over the Dohm Worth It?

The Duet packs 30 sounds, a Bluetooth speaker and an amber night light for $44.99 — five dollars less than the Dohm Classic. But it cannot fake a real fan. Our honest, side-by-side verdict on which Yogasleep belongs on your nightstand.

Share
10 min read
By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Oct 13, 2026 · Last updated Oct 13, 2026

The Yogasleep Dohm is the white noise machine — a real fan inside a plastic shell, making the same rushing-air sound since 1962. The Yogasleep Duet is the company's modern answer: a digital machine with 30 sounds, a Bluetooth speaker and a dimmable amber night light, sold for five dollars less than the Dohm. The upgrade looks obvious on paper. In the bedroom, it comes down to one question: do you love the sound of a real fan?

Quick answer

The Duet is the better buy for most people: $44.99 gets you 30 sounds, a Bluetooth speaker, a dimmable amber night light and sleep timers — features the $49.99 Dohm Classic simply does not have. But the Duet is digital, and it cannot reproduce the Dohm's real mechanical fan texture. If that fan whoosh is what puts you to sleep, keep the Dohm; if you have never bonded with a fan sound, buy the Duet.

The verdict up front

We will skip the suspense. For most sleepers buying today, the Yogasleep Duet is the smarter purchase: it costs less than the Dohm Classic, does more, and its digital sound library is genuinely good. The Dohm Classic survives this verdict on one honest, defensible ground — the physical texture of its fan sound — plus the kind of simplicity some people genuinely want from a nightstand device: two speeds, a tone dial, no menus, nothing to configure in the dark.

Here is the shape of the tradeoff. The Duet is a multi-tool: white, pink and brown noise, fan sounds (including two digital recreations of the Dohm itself), nature tracks and lullabies, a night light, a Bluetooth speaker and timers. The Dohm is a single perfect tool: one sound, made by a real fan, endlessly adjustable with a twist of the collar. Neither machine is trying to be the other, and the right choice is almost entirely a question of which kind of sleeper you are.

Sound quality: real fan vs digital

This is the entire decision, so it deserves the detail. The Dohm Classic's sound is produced by an actual asymmetric fan spinning inside the housing — air moving, not a recording of air moving. Turn the outer shell and you physically change how the sound escapes, which is why the tone adjustment feels so natural. There is no loop, no digital seam, no speaker. Reviewers describe the sound as a "whoosh" with a depth that recordings struggle to capture, and the machine measures a modest 41.5 to 52.5 dB at about six and a half feet — gentle, but limited. If your room is very noisy or the sound needs to carry across a large bedroom, the Dohm's modest volume is its real ceiling.

The Duet takes the opposite approach: a small speaker playing 30 digitally generated, non-looping sounds. The library is genuinely broad — two white noise options, two pink, three brown, deep fan and standard fan sounds, two digital recreations of the Dohm's signature tone, nine nature sounds (rain, surf, thunderstorm, campfire, birds and crickets) and eight lullaby or relaxation tracks. If you have never decided which noise color suits you, the Duet is a laboratory: our brown noise vs white noise comparison argues that personal comfort matters more than the color, and the Duet lets you run that experiment without buying three machines.

The honest criticism is one digital machines always face: the Dohm recreations sound close to the Dohm the way a photo of a fire looks close to a fire — recognizably similar, physically different. Fan purists consistently say the Duet's fan sounds are good but not the same thing, and we agree. The Duet compensates with raw output: its amplifier plays noticeably louder than the Dohm's real fan, which makes it the better masker in genuinely noisy environments — a snoring partner across the room, street traffic, thin apartment walls. For light sleepers whose main problem is intrusive noise rather than falling asleep, that extra headroom matters more than analog purity; see our guide to the best white noise machine for light sleepers for how masking power ranks against comfort.

Features: night light, Bluetooth and timers

The Dohm has two speeds and a tone dial. The Duet has a feature list, and most of it is genuinely useful at night — not gimmick territory.

The night light is the standout. It glows in warm amber tones with gradient brightness you adjust by holding the button down, and it operates independently of the sound — light without noise for nighttime feedings, noise without light for everyone else. Amber light is far less disruptive to sleep than white or blue light, which makes this a thoughtful choice for a nursery. It is the single feature most likely to pull a Dohm owner into an upgrade, because the Dohm has no light at all and adding one means a second device on the nightstand.

The Bluetooth speaker lets you stream anything from your phone — a podcast to fall asleep to, your own curated brown-noise playlist, a sleep-story app — through a speaker that lives permanently by the bed. There is no Wi-Fi and no app, which we count as a plus: no accounts, no updates, nothing to break at 2am. The sleep timers (45 minutes, 90 minutes or 8 hours, plus continuous play) are the Duet's answer to the Dohm's "on until you turn it off" simplicity, and they are genuinely handy if you only need sound to fall asleep.

Two honest caveats. First, the Duet runs on an included USB cord but the wall adapter is not included — you supply your own USB plug, and the unit must stay plugged in while running. It is not rechargeable, so it is bedside-bound, not a travel machine. Second, digital machines age: a speaker's sound can degrade over years, while the Dohm's fan is a famously long-lived, simple mechanism. The Duet is the modern convenience pick; the Dohm is the buy-it-for-a-decade pick.

Price and value

Here is the pricing surprise: the Duet costs $44.99 at major US retailers (Amazon, Target, Walmart), while the Dohm Classic lists at $49.99 direct from Yogasleep. The "upgrade" is five dollars cheaper than the classic it replaces. Both carry a 1-year limited warranty, and Yogasleep offers a trial period on both (30 nights direct from Yogasleep; some retailers advertise longer trials).

On pure value — features per dollar — the Duet wins in a rout: 30 sounds, Bluetooth, a night light and timers for less money. But value is not just feature count. If you already own a Dohm and love it, $44.99 buys you nothing your nights are missing. And if the specific thing you want is the simplest possible machine with one perfect sound, paying $5 more for the Dohm is money well spent. Prices checked October 2026.

Who should upgrade to the Duet

Upgrade — or buy the Duet as your first Yogasleep — if any of these describe you:

  • You want a night light and a sound machine in one device. Nighttime feedings, bathroom trips, a partner who reads in bed — the amber light handles all of it without a second gadget.
  • You want brown or pink noise, nature sounds, or lullabies. The Dohm plays one sound. The Duet plays thirty. Nursery owners especially should start here.
  • You want a bedside Bluetooth speaker. Streaming your own sleep audio through the nightstand speaker is genuinely convenient, no app or Wi-Fi required.
  • Your room is genuinely noisy. The Duet's louder digital output masks more than the Dohm's modest fan can, and timers let you run it just for falling asleep.
  • You have never owned a Dohm and just want the best value. Five dollars cheaper, far more capable — this is the default recommendation.

Who should stick with the Dohm

Keep the Dohm — or buy it over the Duet — if any of these describe you:

  • The Dohm's fan texture is what puts you to sleep. If you have slept to a real fan for years, no digital recreation will feel right, and the Duet's extras will not tempt you. This is the most common reason Dohm owners stay put, and it is completely valid.
  • You want the simplest device possible. Two speeds, a tone dial, one switch. No Bluetooth pairing, no sound menus, nothing to scroll through in the dark. Some people — including plenty of light sleepers — find fewer decisions genuinely relaxing.
  • You distrust digital sound. Some sleepers notice loops and compression artifacts that others never hear. The Dohm has no digital audio at all, so there is nothing to notice.
  • You want a decade-long device. A spinning fan is a simpler mechanism than a speaker and amplifier. Dohms famously run for years; the Duet is newer and its electronics are the wear item.

If your main sleep problem is not noise at all — a racing mind at bedtime, or a bedroom that runs too warm — no sound machine solves that alone. Our research on ideal bedroom temperature covers the environmental half of the equation.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Yogasleep Duet sound as good as the Dohm?

It depends on what you value. The Duet offers 30 digital sounds including two Dohm-style fan sounds, brown and pink noise, and nature tracks — far more variety. But it cannot physically reproduce the Dohm's real mechanical fan texture, which purists say has a depth no recording matches. For variety, the Duet wins; for authentic fan sound, the Dohm wins.

Is the Yogasleep Duet louder than the Dohm Classic?

Yes. The Dohm Classic is a modest-volume machine — Yogasleep measures it at about 41.5 to 52.5 dB at 6.5 feet, with a real fan that cannot be pushed further. The Duet's digital amplifier plays noticeably louder, which is an advantage in very noisy rooms and for blocking a snoring partner across the room.

Can the Duet's night light be turned off completely?

Yes. The sound and the night light run independently, so you can use either one alone. The light itself is a warm amber tone with gradient brightness you adjust by holding the button down — amber light is far less disruptive to sleep than white or blue light, and you can dim it to barely there.

Does the Yogasleep Duet need Wi-Fi, an app, or batteries?

None of the three. The Duet connects over Bluetooth only — no Wi-Fi and no app required — and it is powered by an included USB cord rather than batteries. Note that the wall adapter is not included, so you will need your own USB wall plug. The unit must stay plugged in while running; it is not rechargeable.

Which should I buy: the Yogasleep Duet or the Dohm Classic?

Buy the Duet if you want more than one sound, a night light, Bluetooth streaming, or sleep timers — it costs $44.99, five dollars less than the Dohm. Buy the Dohm Classic if a real mechanical fan's texture is what puts you to sleep and you want the simplest machine possible with nothing to configure, at $49.99.

Key takeaways

  • The Yogasleep Duet ($44.99) beats the Dohm Classic ($49.99) on features and price: 30 sounds, Bluetooth, an amber night light and timers for five dollars less.
  • The one thing the Duet cannot do is reproduce the Dohm's real mechanical fan texture — if that sound is what puts you to sleep, the Dohm remains the right machine.
  • The Duet plays louder and masks more noise; the Dohm is simpler, with no menus, apps or digital audio to notice.
