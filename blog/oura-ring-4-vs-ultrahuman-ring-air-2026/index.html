Oura Ring 4 vs Ultrahuman Ring Air (2026)

Both rings cost $349, but only one keeps charging you — and only one is still sold in the US. Sleep accuracy, battery and 3-year cost compared, Nov 27, 2026.

Share
10 min read
By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Nov 27, 2026 · Last updated Nov 27, 2026

On paper, this is a tie. The Oura Ring 4 and the Ultrahuman Ring Air both cost $349, both track sleep, heart-rate variability and temperature, and both work with iOS and Android. The difference shows up after the box is open: Oura charges $5.99 a month for the membership, while Ultrahuman charges nothing for its core app. And you cannot buy a new Ring Air in the United States any more — a patent ruling took it off the US market in October 2025. Prices checked November 27, 2026.

Quick answer

Both rings list at $349, but the Oura Ring 4 adds a $5.99-a-month membership for full features, while the Ultrahuman Ring Air charges nothing after purchase — $558.97 against $349 over three years at list. The Ring 4 is $249 on Amazon right now, which cuts its three-year cost to about $459.

Oura Ring 4 vs Ultrahuman Ring Air at a Glance

Two rings, one price, opposite philosophies. Oura sells you hardware plus a membership; Ultrahuman sells you hardware once and hands over the data. Here is the comparison in one paragraph each.

Oura Ring 4 — from $349 plus $5.99 a month: the most accurate sleep staging in a ring, the most polished app, and the best validation record — with a membership that gates the features you bought the ring for, and a three-year cost of $558.97.

Ultrahuman Ring Air — $349, no subscription: the lightest ring here at 2.4 grams, with sleep, heart-rate variability, temperature and recovery data included for life — but not sold new in the United States since October 2025, so American buyers need the Ring Pro at $479 or a different ring.

What Matters When a Ring Tracks Your Sleep

A smart ring has one job while you sleep: measure accurately without waking you. Everything else is secondary, and the two rings make opposite trade-offs. Four things decide which fits you.

1. Staging accuracy: the only number that matters

Both rings report sleep stages — light, deep and REM — and a morning sleep score. The question is how often the stages are right. Oura has the most published validation of any smart ring, with independent studies against polysomnography, the lab gold standard — much of it Oura-funded, but it exists and is public. For the Ultrahuman Ring Air, we found no published sleep-tracking validation study as of October 2026.

2. The subscription: the real price

Both rings cost $349 on the shelf. Oura then charges $5.99 a month or $69.99 a year, and without it the app falls back to three basic daily scores with the detailed insights locked. Ultrahuman charges nothing for the core app — sleep stages, heart-rate variability, temperature and recovery scoring are included in the purchase for as long as you own the ring. Over three years at list price: Oura $558.97, Ultrahuman $349 — at the Ring 4's $249 Amazon street price, Oura's three-year cost is about $459. Ultrahuman's optional PowerPlugs, such as Cycle and Ovulation Pro at about $39 a year, are extras you choose — not a gate on the basic product. The subscription is the whole argument for most buyers — and it is the reason this comparison exists.

3. Comfort and battery: the nightly reality

A ring you notice at night is a ring you stop wearing. The Ring Air is the lightest here at 2.4 grams and 2.4 mm thick, with a titanium shell, a tungsten-carbide coating and a hypoallergenic epoxy inner lining. The Oura Ring 4 is heavier and slightly thicker, with smooth titanium inside and out and sensors that sit flush. Battery life runs the other way: Oura claims 5 to 8 days, Ultrahuman 4 to 6 days in independent checks, though Ultrahuman's own comparison table lists 8 to 10. Both last a working week between charges.

4. Availability: the fact most comparisons miss

In April 2024 Oura complained to the US International Trade Commission that Ultrahuman and RingConn infringed its patent; in September 2025 the Commission ruled for Oura, and the exclusion order took effect on October 21, 2025. RingConn settled and licensed the patent; Ultrahuman did not, so the Ring Air left its US store. Ultrahuman redesigned its hardware, and on March 6, 2026 US Customs ruled the new Ring Pro does not infringe, so it is now sold in the US at $479. Oura sued again in Texas on October 22, 2026, over two different patents. For a buyer the position is simple: outside the US the Ring Air is a $349 no-subscription ring; inside the US it is not for sale new.

Our Winner — and Who Should Buy Which

For sleep tracking alone, the Oura Ring 4 wins. It has the best-published sleep-staging accuracy of any ring, the most polished app, and the deepest insights — readiness, recovery and cycle tracking the Ultrahuman app does not match. The cost is real: $558.97 over three years at list price — about $459 at the Ring 4's current $249 Amazon price — and a membership you cannot cancel without losing the features you bought the ring for. Buy the Oura if you want the most accurate sleep data available in a ring and you accept the subscription as part of the price.

Buy the Ultrahuman Ring Air if you live outside the United States and refuse a subscription: $349 once, no recurring fee, the lightest ring here, and solid sleep and recovery tracking with no paywall on your data. If you are in the US, do not hunt for grey-market Ring Air stock — buy the Ultrahuman Ring Pro at $479 list instead, which is the redesigned ring cleared for US sale and adds up to 15 days of battery and a charging case, or switch to a rival that is actually sold here: the RingConn Gen 2 at $299, no subscription, or the Samsung Galaxy Ring from $299.99, also no subscription.

The Rings in Detail

Oura Ring 4 — from $349 plus $5.99 a month

The Ring 4 is the ring that made the category. It is a titanium ring with eight LED sensors, sized 4 to 15, with a 5-to-8-day battery depending on size. The hardware is the benchmark: comfortable enough to forget, and the only ring here with published validation against lab sleep studies. The app is the other half: Sleep, Readiness and Activity scores each morning, heart-rate variability and temperature trends, cycle insights, and guidance that improves the longer you wear it. The membership is the catch: without one, the ring works only with limited functionality. One month is included, then $5.99 a month or $69.99 a year. The Ring 5, launched in May 2026 from $399 ($299 on Amazon during Black Friday week), is smaller and lasts 6 to 9 days — but it carries the same membership, so the choice is about $50 of hardware, not the subscription.

Ultrahuman Ring Air — $349, no subscription

The Ring Air is the minimalist's ring: 2.4 grams, 2.4 mm thick, a titanium shell with a tungsten-carbide coating, and infrared PPG, non-contact skin temperature and 6-axis motion sensors inside. It tracks sleep stages, heart-rate variability, temperature, movement and recovery, plus circadian-rhythm and metabolic-health features Oura does not emphasise. The app gives you everything the ring measures with no paid tier gating the data — Ultrahuman promises lifelong access to the ring and your data in writing. The gaps: blood oxygen (SpO2) is still in development, atrial-fibrillation detection is a paid PowerPlug, and — decisive for American buyers — the Ring Air is not sold new in the US. Ultrahuman's US store lists the Ring Pro and Ring Rare instead; the Ring Air survives only as leftover retail stock. Outside the US, at $349 with no subscription, it is the best-value sleep ring on the market. Inside the US, it is a ring you cannot buy.

The alternatives if the Ring Air is off your shelf

American buyers who want the no-subscription deal have two options. The Ultrahuman Ring Pro, launched in February 2026 and cleared by US Customs in March 2026, is $479 list (about $378 during Black Friday week), with up to 15 days of battery, 250 days of on-ring storage and a charging case holding up to 45 days of charge. The cheaper route is to leave Ultrahuman entirely: the RingConn Gen 2 at $299 has no subscription and settled Oura's patent claim with a licence, so it sells freely in the US, and the Samsung Galaxy Ring from $299.99 also charges no subscription. Both track sleep well, neither has Oura's validation record, and both cost less over three years than the Oura Ring 4 with membership. Our RingConn Gen 2 vs Ultrahuman Ring Air comparison covers the RingConn in detail.

Frequently asked questions

Can I still buy the Ultrahuman Ring Air in the US?

Not new from Ultrahuman. A US International Trade Commission exclusion order took effect on October 21, 2025, and Ultrahuman's US store no longer lists the Ring Air — it points buyers to the Ring Pro at $479 list. Leftover retail stock may exist, and owners who bought before the order keep full warranty support — but no new Ring Air stock can be imported.

Which ring tracks sleep more accurately, Oura Ring 4 or Ultrahuman Ring Air?

The Oura Ring 4, on the published evidence. Oura has the most published sleep-lab validation of any smart ring, tested against polysomnography, the lab gold standard — though much of it was funded by Oura itself. We found no published sleep-tracking validation study for the Ultrahuman Ring Air as of October 2026. Both rings measure the same signals, but only Oura's staging accuracy has been tested in print.

Does the Oura Ring 4 work without a membership?

Partly. Without a membership the ring still collects data, but the app falls back to three basic daily scores — Sleep, Readiness and Activity — and locks the detailed insights that make the ring worth buying. A membership costs $5.99 a month or $69.99 a year. Ultrahuman charges nothing for its core app — sleep stages, heart-rate variability, temperature and recovery scoring are included in the $349 purchase for life.

Which ring is better for side sleepers?

The Ultrahuman Ring Air, on weight and thickness: it is 2.4 grams in its smallest size and 2.4 mm thick, the lightest ring in this comparison, and its smooth titanium shell with a tungsten-carbide coating has no protruding sensor bumps. The Oura Ring 4 is also slim with flush sensors, but it is heavier — and neither ring bothers a side sleeper the way a watch does.

Should I buy the Oura Ring 4 or the newer Oura Ring 5?

The Ring 5 launched in May 2026 from $399 ($299 on Amazon during Black Friday week); Oura still sells the Ring 4 at $349 list ($249 on Amazon) with the same membership. The Ring 5 is smaller — about 40% smaller, sizes 6–13 — with longer battery, 6 to 9 days against 5 to 8, and the same terms. If you want the latest hardware and can pay $50 more, buy the Ring 5. If the subscription bothers you, the Ring 4 saves money on hardware but not on the membership — and a subscription-free rival like the RingConn Gen 2 at $299 or the Samsung Galaxy Ring from $299.99 may be the better buy.

Key Takeaways

  • Same sticker, different cost: both $349 list, but the Oura adds $5.99 a month — $558.97 over three years against $349 flat (about $459 at the Ring 4's $249 street price).
  • Oura tracks sleep more accurately on the published evidence: the most lab validation of any ring; no published validation study exists for the Ring Air.
  • The Ring Air is not sold new in the US: an October 2025 patent ruling removed it from the American market — US buyers should look at the Ring Pro at $479 or the RingConn Gen 2 at $299.
  • Ultrahuman is the lighter ring: 2.4 grams against a heavier Oura, with a no-paywall app that gives you all your data for the life of the ring.
  • The Ring 5 changes the Oura side: May 2026, from $399 ($299 on Amazon this week), smaller with longer battery — but the same membership, so it does not fix the subscription question.
  • Buy the Oura for accuracy, the Ultrahuman for ownership — and check availability in your country before you decide.
