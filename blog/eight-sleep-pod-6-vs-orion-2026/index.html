Eight Sleep Pod 6 vs Orion: AI Cooling Mattress Pads, Head to Head

The Pod 6 suits data-obsessed optimizers who want dual-zone heating and cooling, biometric tracking, and automatic temperature and elevation adjustments — starting at $1,999 plus a $199–$399/year membership. Orion is the simpler, TechCrunch-tested cooling pad: $2,195 plus $250/year, a 50°F–113°F range, and built-in sleep tracking. Single-side sleepers should look at the Pod 6 Solo.

How to choose an AI cooling mattress pad

These are active thermal systems, not passive toppers: a bedside unit circulates water or air through the cover all night. You are really buying a price model, a temperature range, and a piece of software — settle these before spending $2,000-plus.

Start with the room itself: if your bedroom runs hot all summer, read our guide to sleeping cool without AC first — no pad can rescue an 85°F bedroom at midnight.

  • Subscription or one-and-done? Both systems here charge yearly on top of the hardware. Compare the five-year total, not the sticker price — a cheaper pad with a pricey plan costs more by year three.
  • Dual-zone vs single-side: Couples who run hot and cold need independent zones; a solo sleeper on one side may only need half the coverage — and can pay for half.
  • What the "AI" actually does: In practice, both are data-driven temperature schedules: they learn your sleep timing and shift heating or cooling to match. Ask whether that automation is worth a recurring fee.
  • Temperature range: A published absolute range (like Orion's ~50°F–113°F) tells you how low it can actually go; "dual-zone" alone leaves you guessing.

The subscription trap: do the five-year math

This is where comparisons go wrong: the hardware price is only the entry ticket. Every Pod 6 requires an Autopilot membership — Standard $199/yr ($17/mo), Enhanced $299/yr ($25/mo), Elite $399/yr ($33/mo) — and the warranty rides on it (2 years with Standard, 5 years with Enhanced and Elite). Orion charges a single $250/yr Sleep System plan. The five-year totals:

  • Pod 6 Solo + Standard: $1,999 + 5 × $199 = $2,994
  • Pod 6 Queen + Standard: $2,899 + 5 × $199 = $3,894
  • Pod 6 Queen + Enhanced: $2,899 + 5 × $299 = $4,394
  • Orion: $2,195 + 5 × $250 = $3,445

The cheapest way into the category is a one-side Pod 6 on the Standard plan; a full-bed Pod 6 with a mid-tier membership sails past Orion by year five. Our Pod 6 price and subscription breakdown walks through every size and tier if you are deciding between memberships.

AI claims vs schedule-based behavior

Strip away the marketing and both do the same thing: collect your sleep data and run a temperature schedule around it. Pod 6's Autopilot adjusts temperature — and, notably, bed elevation — automatically through the night using biometric tracking. Orion's phone-controlled app learns your sleep data and shifts its cooling curve to match. Neither company publishes enough detail to independently judge the "AI" — and even friendly reviewers raise an eyebrow: TechCrunch paired genuine love for Orion's cooling with open skepticism about the AI claims. Treat the software as convenient automation, not magic, and weigh it against the recurring fee it demands.

Temperature range and dual-zone cooling

Orion publishes an absolute range of roughly 50°F to 113°F — unusually transparent, and genuinely cold at the low end. Eight Sleep advertises dual-zone heating and cooling but does not publish an absolute °F range the way Orion does, so you are trusting the brand's reputation rather than a number. Both cover the two-zones-for-couples use case, and Pod 6's Solo is the only option built for covering a single side. Our verdict: which $2,000+ sleep system wins

The data-obsessed optimizer buys the Pod 6. Dual-zone heating and cooling, biometric tracking, and Autopilot software that adjusts temperature and elevation through the night make it the most complete sleep system here — if you want every variable measured and managed, nothing else in this comparison does more. Budget for the membership, though: a Queen on the Standard plan is $3,894 over five years, and the hardware is only half the story. Our Pod 6 price explainer has the full tier math.

The cooling-first buyer who hates fine print buys the Orion. One pad, one $250/yr plan, a published 50°F–113°F range, and cooling that won over a skeptical TechCrunch reviewer — a simpler proposition to say yes to. Built-in heart-rate and breath-rate tracking plus a guided Sleep Optimization Test mean you still get data, just with less menu-diving. Read our full Orion review before deciding.

The one-side sleeper buys the Pod 6 Solo. If you sleep alone on one side of the bed, there is no reason to pay to cool empty mattress: the Solo at $1,999 plus the Standard membership is the cheapest five-year path in this comparison at $2,994.

One honest caveat: neither treats, diagnoses, or improves a medical condition — they're comfort products for the sleep environment. If overheating at night is new, severe, or paired with other symptoms, talk to a clinician.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need the subscription with the Eight Sleep Pod 6, or can I skip it?

You need it. Per Eight Sleep's launch materials, every Pod 6 requires an Autopilot membership — Standard $199/yr ($17/mo), Enhanced $299/yr ($25/mo), or Elite $399/yr ($33/mo). The membership powers the AI temperature and elevation features and carries the warranty (2 years with Standard, 5 years with Enhanced and Elite). Treat it as part of the price: a Queen on the Standard plan costs $3,894 over five years.

Which is cheaper over five years: Pod 6 or Orion?

It depends on the configuration. The Pod 6 Solo with the Standard membership totals $2,994 over five years — cheaper than Orion's $3,445 ($2,195 plus 5 × $250/yr). But a full-bed Pod 6 Queen with the Standard membership reaches $3,894, and the Enhanced tier pushes it to $4,394. Solo sleeper: the Pod 6 wins on cost. Couple on a full bed: Orion is the cheaper five-year buy.

How cold does the Orion actually get?

Orion publishes a range of roughly 50°F to 113°F with dual-zone cooling and heating — unusually transparent for the category, and genuinely cold at the low end per TechCrunch's testing. Eight Sleep, by contrast, advertises dual-zone heating and cooling without publishing an absolute °F range. If a hard number matters to you, Orion is the only one here that gives you one.

What did TechCrunch say about the Orion sleep pad?

Amanda Silberling reviewed it on September 18, 2026, and the verdict was split: open skepticism toward the AI claims, genuine love for the cooling — she said she was “sleeping like a baby.” The review also notes built-in heart-rate and breath-rate tracking and a guided Sleep Optimization Test during onboarding. It's the most credible third-party data point either product has right now.

Will an AI cooling pad fix my night sweats or insomnia?

We don't make that claim. These are comfort products for the sleep environment — they manage temperature comfort through the night, which many people find more pleasant. They are not medical devices, and nothing here is medical advice. If overheating or poor sleep is new, severe, or comes with other symptoms, talk to a clinician rather than shopping for hardware.
