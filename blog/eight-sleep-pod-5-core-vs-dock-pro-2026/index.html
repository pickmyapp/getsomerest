Eight Sleep Pod 5 Core vs ChiliSleep Dock Pro: Is the $199/Year Subscription Actually Worth It?

Pod 5 Core ($2,849 + $199/yr) vs ChiliSleep Dock Pro (from $999, no subscription): real five-year cost math, what Autopilot buys, and who should skip Eight Sleep. 2026.

By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Oct 16, 2026 · Last updated Oct 16, 2026

This is the most expensive question in bed cooling: is Eight Sleep's Pod 5 Core worth nearly triple the price of the ChiliSleep Dock Pro — and then a $199 annual subscription on top? Both circulate water through a mattress cover to cool your bed. The difference is everything around the water: sleep tracking, autopilot temperature changes, vibration alarms — and the fee that keeps them all switched on. Here is the honest math.

Quick answer

The Eight Sleep Pod 5 Core costs $2,849 plus a mandatory $199/year Autopilot subscription (required for the first 12 months; canceling later disables tracking, autopilot cooling and vibration alarms, leaving manual temperature only), while the ChiliSleep Dock Pro starts at $999 with no subscription ever. Over five years that's roughly $3,844 vs $999-$1,499. The Pod 5 wins for hands-off tracking and adaptive cooling; the Dock Pro wins for pure cooling value.

The subscription is the product — here's what it actually buys

Both systems move water through a mattress cover. Everything else is software and business model. The Pod 5 Core's hardware is excellent — a quilted cover with per-side water channels, a hub, and biometric sensors — but the features that make it an "Eight Sleep" rather than a "nice cooling pad" all live behind Autopilot.

Here's what the money gets you, and what happens if you stop paying.

  • What Autopilot unlocks. Sleep tracking (heart rate, HRV, sleep stages, breathing rate), autopilot temperature adjustments through the night, vibration and thermal alarms, scheduled heating and cooling, and the Health Check reports. One membership covers two sleeper profiles.
  • What canceling removes. After the mandatory first year, you can cancel — and the Pod 5 reverts to manual temperature control. No tracking, no autopilot, no smart alarms, no schedules. The hardware still cools; the intelligence is gone.
  • The tier ladder. Standard ($199/yr) carries a 2-year warranty. Enhanced ($299/yr) extends it to 5 years — the warranty only stays valid while the plan is paid. Elite ($399/yr) adds cardiovascular and respiratory monitoring. Over five years, the Enhanced tier adds $500 to the hardware cost.
  • What the Dock Pro gives you instead. 55-115°F water cooling, dual-side control, Wi-Fi app schedules, buttons on the unit, and a 30-night trial — all with zero subscription. No tracking out of the box, but nothing to lose by canceling either.
  • The real comparison. Don't compare $2,849 to $999. Compare $3,844 (five-year Pod 5) to $999-$1,499 (five-year Dock Pro) — a roughly $2,500-$2,850 gap. The question is whether tracking and autopilot are worth ~$50/month for half a decade.

What Autopilot actually unlocks

The value proposition is real, not just marketing: the Pod 5's sensors watch your sleep in real time and nudge the water temperature up and down through the night — cooler when you're restless, warmer toward morning. Owners who keep the subscription tend to love exactly this: they set it once and never think about bed temperature again. If you're the kind of person who tracks workouts, HRV and recovery data, the Pod 5 folds your bed into that ecosystem. Our smart mattress tracking comparison puts it in context.

The five-year math

Run the numbers honestly. Pod 5 Core: $2,849 + 5 × $199 = $3,844 at Standard; $4,344 at Enhanced; $4,844 at Elite. Dock Pro ME: $999, done. Dock Pro WE: ~$1,299-$1,499, done. Even if the Dock Pro's pad wears out in year four and you replace the whole system, you're still thousands ahead. The Pod 5 only makes financial sense if you were going to buy a high-end sleep tracker and a premium cooling system separately — then the bundle math starts to look reasonable.

Who should skip Eight Sleep (and who shouldn't)

Skip Eight Sleep if you only want a cooler bed: the Dock Pro does that job for a third of the five-year cost with no strings. Skip it if subscriptions bother you on principle — because the subscription isn't optional garnish, it's the product. Don't skip it if you genuinely want passive, tracked, adaptive sleep cooling and you'd keep the membership: the Pod 5 (or the newer Pod 6) is the only system that does all of that in one box.

Our verdict: who should pay the subscription?

Buy the Eight Sleep Pod 5 Core if you want the most intelligent bed-cooling system money can buy and you're comfortable with the ongoing cost: passive biometric tracking, autopilot temperature that adapts through the night, and an ecosystem that treats your bed as part of your recovery routine. Go in eyes open — $2,849 is the down payment, $199/year is the mortgage, and canceling turns it into a very expensive cooling pad. If you're buying new today, also look at the Pod 6: it's the current model, starts cheaper, and the Solo option from $1,999 is built for single sleepers.

Buy the ChiliSleep Dock Pro if you want the cooling without the commitment. It holds any temperature from 55 to 115°F, runs both sides of the bed independently, needs no subscription ever, and costs $999-$1,499 once — roughly a third of the Pod 5's five-year cost. What you give up is the intelligence: no tracking, no autopilot, no smart alarms. For pure, honest, set-your-temperature cooling, it's the better financial decision by a wide margin — and it's the system we recommend to most people.

Frequently asked questions

Are heated mattress pads safe to leave on all night?

Yes, with a modern pad that has an auto shut-off timer and overheat protection, such as the ETL- or UL-certified models we compare here. Set the auto-off timer for the time you actually need — most sleepers use one or two hours after falling asleep rather than the full 12-hour window — and never use a pad that is damp or folded over itself.

Heated mattress pad vs electric blanket: which is better for sleep?

A heated mattress pad usually wins for sleep. It warms the bed from underneath so heat rises around you, it never slides off, and it can preheat the bed before you get in. Electric blankets trap heat on top and often make sleepers overheat; a pad plus your normal blanket lets you fine-tune warmth on each side.

How much does it cost to run a heated mattress pad?

Very little. A pad like the Bwarm HeatSync draws about 65 watts per side — roughly the same as an incandescent light bulb. Running one side for eight hours costs only a few cents of electricity, far less than running a 1,500-watt space heater to warm the whole bedroom.

Will a heated mattress pad fit my mattress?

Most fit mattresses up to 18 inches deep, which covers the majority of pillow-top and hybrid mattresses. If your mattress is thicker than 18 inches, pick a deep-pocket model — the Beautyrest Cotton pad here fits up to 21 inches, and all three pads use all-around elastic skirts so they stay put.

Can I wash a heated mattress pad?

Yes — all three pads we compare are machine washable. Detach the controller and cords first, wash on a gentle cycle, and make sure the pad is completely dry before plugging it back in. Never dry clean or bleach it, and never operate a pad that is still damp.
