GhostBed Climate Pro Explained (2026)

GhostBed's Climate Pro is a $999 dual-zone water cover that heats and cools your bed from 60°F to 105°F, with sleep tracking and no subscription.

What the Climate Pro Is

The Climate Pro is GhostBed's entry into active bed climate control: a thin cover that sits on your existing mattress, under your fitted sheet, with water circulating through a layer inside it. A bedside Control Hub heats or cools the water and pumps it through the cover, so the surface of your bed — not just the air in the room — holds the temperature you set. The circulating water temperature adjusts from 60°F to 105°F (about 15.6°C to 40.6°C), which covers cold sleepers, hot sleepers and most seasons in between.

Two things define it. The first is independent dual-zone control: each side of the bed runs its own temperature. If you sleep hot and your partner sleeps cold, neither of you has to compromise on one setting. The second is sleep monitoring built into the system: GhostBed says the Climate Pro records heart rate and sleep stages, and the basic temperature control, scheduling and Sleep Hygiene features need no subscription. That last point matters, because the category leader, Eight Sleep, gates its full tracking experience behind a $199-a-year Autopilot membership.

The campaign itself was a strong one. The Climate Pro launched on Kickstarter on October 6, 2026, passed $400,000 in its first week with an average pledge above $1,200, and closed on November 5 with nearly $691,000 pledged. GhostBed ran a $1 VIP reservation program in September, offering discounts of up to $700 and extended warranty benefits to people who signed up early. That pre-launch list is a big part of why the campaign funded fast — and a reminder that the $999 headline price was not what every backer paid.

How the Heating and Cooling Works

The Climate Pro works the same way as the other water-based covers: a bedside hub circulates temperature-controlled water through a Hydronic Layer inside the cover. Water carries heat far better than air, so the system can add or remove heat on demand instead of just blowing room-temperature air at you, the way a BedJet does. The practical result is a bed surface that holds a set temperature all night, rather than a room that slowly cools down.

The range is wide on both ends. Cooling down to 60°F covers hot sleepers and summer nights; heating up to 105°F covers cold sleepers and winter bedrooms. In practice most people live in the middle of that range — sleep research points to a cool bedroom, and the bed surface is usually set a few degrees warmer than the room — but the range means the cover can handle extremes that a fan or a passive cooling topper cannot.

Two details in the spec sheet deserve attention. The first is the Condensation Guard: GhostBed limits the circulating water temperature to a maximum of 14°F below room temperature, so the cover cannot chill far enough below the air to sweat condensation into your bedding. The second is the plumbing: 8-foot hoses that route out either the left or right side of the cover, so the hub can sit on a nightstand or on the floor, on whichever side of the bed works — a small thing that matters a lot in a real bedroom, where the outlet and the nightstand are rarely where the product photo puts them. The hub itself measures 11.7 by 10.3 by 6.3 inches and runs on 110–240V power.

The honest caveat is the same one that applies to every crowdfunded climate cover: no independent multi-night testing of production units existed as of late October 2026. Cooling speed, operating noise, power draw and how well the two zones stay separate when one side is set to 60°F and the other to 90°F are all unmeasured outside GhostBed's own testing. The sleep monitoring is in the same position — GhostBed says it records heart rate and sleep stages, but no independent accuracy testing is public, and there is no comparison against a clinical sleep monitor.

Price, Sizes and Kickstarter Dates

The Kickstarter price was $999, and that figure is the whole story of the product's positioning: half the price of an Eight Sleep Pod 5 Core, the same price as Moera's crowdfunded cover, and a fraction of the Orion system's $2,395. The $1 VIP reservation program in September offered discounts of up to $700, so the earliest backers paid well under $999. No retail price has been announced, and the first retail price — if the cover reaches GhostBed's store — will almost certainly be higher than the Kickstarter price. Crowdfunding prices are launch prices, not list prices.

Sizes are straightforward and couple-friendly: Twin XL (38 by 80 inches), Queen (60 by 80), King (76 by 80) and Split King (two Twin XLs). The Split King is the one to note if you share a bed: it is two separate covers, so each side is fully independent, with no heat exchange across the middle of the bed at all. On a standard Queen or King, the two zones share one cover, and GhostBed has not published independent data on how well the zones stay separate when the settings are far apart.

The timeline is short. The campaign launched October 6, 2026, closed November 5 with nearly $691,000 pledged, and launch coverage points to delivery in December 2026 or early 2027. That is fast for crowdfunded hardware, which is exactly why it is the date most likely to slip. First products rarely ship on their first estimate, and a cover that has to be manufactured, filled, tested and shipped in under two months leaves little slack for a problem found late.

What the launch materials do not say matters too. There is no published trial period, no published warranty and no return policy in the coverage we reviewed. Those are the terms that separate a retail purchase from a crowdfunding pledge, and they are the first things to check on GhostBed's site before you buy. GhostBed is an established mattress brand with a real customer-service operation, which is more reassurance than a first-time startup offers — but a Kickstarter pledge is still a pledge, not a store order.

The honest comparison is with the Moera Smart Climate Cover, our full explainer on the other $999 crowdfunded cover. Moera's range is wider — 50°F to 120°F against GhostBed's 60°F to 105°F — and it has Sleep Cycle's sleep analysis built into the topper, running locally on the device. GhostBed's counter is the brand behind it: GhostBed has sold mattresses for years, with a real support operation and a real warranty culture, while Moera is a first sleep product from a consumer tech company. If you are choosing between the two crowdfunded covers, Moera wins on specs and GhostBed wins on the company behind it. Neither has independent reviews yet.

Against the retail products, the trade is price against proof. Our Eight Sleep Pod 6 explainer covers the category leader: more than twice the price, a $199-a-year subscription for the full experience, but a shipping product with trial periods, warranties and years of independent reviews. The BedJet 3 vs ChiliSleep Dock Pro comparison covers the cheaper established alternatives: BedJet blows temperature-controlled air and is the cheapest way into active bed climate, while the Dock Pro is water-based like the Climate Pro but with no sleep tracking. If you only want a cooler bed and you want it this week, BedJet is the buy. If you want water-based heating and cooling with tracking, the Climate Pro is the cheapest way in — once it ships.

Who It Suits — and the Crowdfunding Risks

It suits you if: you sleep hot and your partner sleeps cold, you want active heating and cooling without paying $2,000 or more, you like the idea of sleep tracking without a subscription, and you are comfortable backing a first product on Kickstarter. At $999 it is the cheapest dual-zone water cover with tracking from an established mattress brand — if it ships as promised.

Skip it, or wait, if: you need a cover this month, you want a trial period and a warranty in writing, you are not comfortable with crowdfunding risk, or you want independently verified temperature and tracking accuracy. In those cases the Orion system ($2,395 for a Queen) and the Eight Sleep Pod 5 Core ($2,849 plus subscription) are the retail buys, and the BedJet 3 is the budget buy for cooling alone. Waiting for independent reviews of the Climate Pro is the lowest-risk move of all — the Kickstarter price is attractive, but it is only a bargain if the product arrives and works.

Where to buy: the Climate Pro was sold through its Kickstarter campaign, which closed November 5, 2026. Check GhostBed's site for the current price and availability — the cover may move to retail after the campaign, at a higher price. For the established alternatives, you can find the Eight Sleep Pod 5 Core on Amazon and the BedJet 3 on Amazon; Orion sells direct from its own store. Prices change often, so verify before you buy.

Everything in this article comes from GhostBed's Kickstarter campaign and launch coverage, PledgeRadar's campaign teardown of October 30, 2026, and HomeCrux's launch report of October 17, 2026.

  • Price: $999 was the Kickstarter special price, per HomeCrux and PledgeRadar. The $1 VIP reservation program and discounts of up to $700 come from PledgeRadar's teardown. No retail price has been announced.
  • Campaign total: nearly $691,000 pledged when the campaign closed November 5, 2026. PledgeRadar reported over $500,000 as of October 30, with over $400,000 in the first week.
  • Temperature range: 60–105°F is the circulating water temperature range from GhostBed's specifications, per PledgeRadar. The bed surface range is slightly narrower, around 59–104°F. Both are manufacturer figures.
  • Sleep tracking: GhostBed says the Climate Pro records heart rate and sleep stages, with no subscription for basic control, scheduling and Sleep Hygiene features. No independent accuracy testing is public.
  • Delivery: December 2026 or early 2027, per launch coverage. Crowdfunding dates are estimates and slip often.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the GhostBed Climate Pro cost?

The Kickstarter price was $999, and the campaign closed on November 5, 2026 with nearly $691,000 pledged. GhostBed ran a $1 VIP reservation program in September offering discounts of up to $700, so early backers paid less than the $999 headline price. No retail price has been announced yet, so check GhostBed's site for the current price before buying.

When will the Climate Pro ship?

GhostBed has pointed to delivery in December 2026 or early 2027, according to launch coverage. That is an estimate for a first-run crowdfunded product, and crowdfunding delivery dates slip often. Back it only if you are comfortable waiting past that window.

Does the Climate Pro need a subscription?

No. GhostBed says basic temperature control, scheduling and its Sleep Hygiene features do not require a subscription, and the sleep monitoring is included. That is a real difference from Eight Sleep, where the full tracking experience sits behind the $199-a-year Autopilot membership.

Does it work with any mattress?

It is a cover that fits over the mattress you already own, under your fitted sheet — the same idea as the Moera cover and the Eight Sleep Pod cover. It comes in Twin XL, Queen, King and Split King (two Twin XLs), with 8-foot hoses that route out either side, so the hub can sit on a nightstand or the floor on whichever side works.

Is the Climate Pro a mattress or a cover?

A cover. Some launch coverage called it a smart mattress, but the Climate Pro is a water-circulated cover that sits on your existing mattress, with a bedside Control Hub that heats and cools the water. You keep your mattress and add the cover.
