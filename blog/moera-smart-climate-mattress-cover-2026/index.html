Moera Climate Mattress Cover Explained (2026)

Moera's Smart Climate Mattress Cover heats and cools your bed from 50°F to 120°F, dual-zone, with Sleep Cycle built in. From $999 on Kickstarter.

What the Moera Cover Is

Moera is a consumer technology company, and its Smart Climate Mattress Cover is its first sleep product: a thin cover that sits on your existing mattress, under your fitted sheet, with water circulating through a layer inside it. A bedside unit heats or cools the water and pumps it through the cover, so the surface of your bed — not just the air in the room — holds the temperature you set. The tested bed surface range is 50°F to 120°F, which is the widest range of any bed climate product we have covered: wider than the Eight Sleep Pod 5's 55°F to 110°F and the Orion's 50°F to 113°F.

Two things set it apart from the covers already on sale. The first is dual-zone control: each side of the bed runs its own temperature, independently. If you sleep hot and your partner sleeps cold, you do not have to compromise on one setting — each side gets its own. The second is Sleep Cycle, built in. Moera is the global IoT and smart home partner behind the multi-year licensing agreement Sleep Cycle announced in December 2025, and these toppers are the first products under it. The sleep analysis is built into the bed itself: it measures sleep and breathing through the night and adjusts temperature on its own, so you only have to go to bed.

The project officially began in August 2025, alongside the Sleep Cycle collaboration, and the cover launched on Kickstarter on November 10, 2026. The Moera app also provides access to Sleep Cycle Premium features, while keeping core temperature control functions free if you back Moera during the Kickstarter campaign. The cover sheets can be machine-washed, while the mattress cover itself can be spot-cleaned — a practical detail that matters for something you sleep on every night.

How the Heating and Cooling Works

Moera actively regulates bed surface temperature through a water-circulated layer, which is the key difference from passive cooling bedding. A cooling mattress topper or a bamboo sheet can only move heat away slowly; a water loop can add or remove heat on demand. The company says you can feel a noticeable cooling or warming effect in as little as five minutes, which is meant to establish a comfortable sleep environment within the critical ten-minute window before bedtime. That pre-bed window is the product's real use case: most people do not need their bed at 50°F all night, they need it cool when they get in and stable while they sleep.

The temperature range is wide on both ends. Cooling down to 50°F covers the coldest sleepers and the hottest summer nights; heating up to 120°F covers people who sleep cold and winter bedrooms. In practice you will live in the middle of that range — most sleep research points to a cool bedroom, and the bed surface is usually set a few degrees warmer than the room — but the range means the cover can handle extremes that a fan or a cooling topper cannot.

The automatic adjustments are where Sleep Cycle comes in. The sleep analysis is powered by Sleep Cycle's patented AI sound model under an SDK licensing agreement, and the model runs locally inside the topper, independent of any smartphone or app. It measures sleep and breathing through the night and adjusts the temperature on its own as your sleep changes. That is the same closed loop Eight Sleep and Orion promise — measure, then adjust — but with the analysis running on the device rather than in the cloud. Whether it works as well as the established systems is untested: no independent review of Moera's sensing existed as of November 12, 2026.

One honest caveat about the numbers: the 50°F to 120°F range is a tested bed surface temperature range from the manufacturer, not an independent measurement. Eight Sleep's 55°F to 110°F and Orion's 50°F to 113°F are manufacturer figures too, and they are all measured the same way — at the surface, under lab conditions. Compare them as claims, not as verified facts, until someone tests them side by side.

Price, Sizes and Kickstarter Dates

The launch release states no price at all — which is why the queue note for this article flagged pricing as something to verify at writing time. The price we can report comes from Build My Longevity's check of the campaign: Moera is on Kickstarter from $999 for a Queen, with shipping from January 2027 at the earliest. That is the figure to use, with the caveat that Kickstarter reward tiers change as early-bird slots fill, and the campaign page is the only authoritative source for the current price and for other sizes.

At $999, Moera undercuts the established water-based covers by a wide margin. The Orion Sleep System lists at $2,395 for a Queen, and the Eight Sleep Pod 5 Core is $2,849 plus $199 a year for Autopilot. Even the GhostBed Climate Pro, the other crowdfunded dual-zone water cover on Kickstarter, is also $999 — so Moera is priced against the crowdfunding rival, not against the retail products. That is the right comparison: you are paying a crowdfunding price for a crowdfunding product, with crowdfunding risk attached.

The timeline is short and worth reading carefully. The project began in August 2025, the Sleep Cycle partnership was named on September 4, 2026, and the Kickstarter launched November 10, 2026. Shipping from January 2027 at the earliest means the first backers could have a cover within about two months of launch — fast for crowdfunded hardware, and therefore the date most likely to slip. First products rarely ship on their first estimate.

What the release does not say matters too. There is no published trial period, no published warranty, no list of sizes and no return policy in the launch materials. Those are the terms that separate a retail purchase from a crowdfunding pledge, and they are the first things to check on the campaign page before you back it. If the campaign does not state them, treat that as information.

The honest comparison is with the Eight Sleep Pod 6 and the Orion AI mattress pad, our two full explainers on water-based covers. Against the cheaper air-based options, the trade is different. If you only want a cooler bed and you want it this week, BedJet is the buy. If you want water-based heating and cooling with tracking, Moera is the cheapest way in — once it ships.

GhostBed's Climate Pro deserves a mention because it is the closest thing to Moera on the market: also a dual-zone water cover, also on Kickstarter, also $999, with a narrower 59°F to 104°F range and no sleep tracking. If you are choosing between the two crowdfunded covers, Moera's wider range and Sleep Cycle integration are the differentiators; GhostBed's is an established mattress brand. Neither has independent reviews yet.

Who It Suits — and the Crowdfunding Risks

It suits you if: you sleep hot and your partner sleeps cold, you want active heating and cooling without paying $2,000 or more, you already use Sleep Cycle and like the idea of its analysis running in the bed, and you are comfortable backing a first product on Kickstarter. At $999 it is the cheapest dual-zone water cover with tracking on the market — if it ships as promised.

Skip it, or wait, if: you need a cover this month, you want a trial period and a warranty in writing, you are not comfortable with crowdfunding risk, or you want independently verified temperature and tracking accuracy. In those cases the Orion system ($2,395 for a Queen) and the Eight Sleep Pod 5 Core ($2,849 plus subscription) are the retail buys, and the BedJet 3 is the budget buy for cooling alone. Waiting for independent reviews of Moera is the lowest-risk move of all — the Kickstarter price is attractive, but it is only a bargain if the product arrives and works.

Where to buy: Moera is sold through its Kickstarter campaign, not through Amazon or a retail store. Check the campaign page for current reward tiers, sizes and the shipping estimate before backing. For the established alternatives, you can find the Eight Sleep Pod 5 Core on Amazon and the BedJet 3 on Amazon; Orion sells direct from its own store. Prices change often, so verify before you buy.

When will Moera ship?

From January 2027 at the earliest, according to third-party coverage of the campaign. That is an estimate for a first product on Kickstarter, and crowdfunding delivery dates slip often. Back it only if you are comfortable waiting past that date.
