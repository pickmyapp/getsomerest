Oura Ring 4 vs Samsung Galaxy Ring (2026): Subscription Flagship vs No-Subscription Challenger

Oura Ring 4 vs Samsung Galaxy Ring in 2026: prices, the two-year subscription math, battery, and which smart ring tracks your sleep better. Honest verdict.

Share
13 min read
By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Oct 21, 2026 · Last updated Oct 21, 2026

The two heavyweights of the smart ring world could not take more opposite approaches to your sleep: the Oura Ring 4 is the sleep-tracking flagship with deep, clinically validated data behind a $5.99-a-month membership, while the Samsung Galaxy Ring is the titanium no-subscription challenger that folds everything into Samsung Health for a one-time $399.99. If you are buying a ring to understand — or fix — your nights, here is how the two actually compare on the things that matter: sleep accuracy, comfort, battery, phone compatibility, and what each one really costs you over time.

Quick answer

The short answer: For pure sleep tracking, the Oura Ring 4 still wins — its staging is validated against polysomnography and its Readiness and Sleep scores go deeper than anyone else's. For value, the Samsung Galaxy Ring wins: $399.99 with no subscription ever, a lighter ring, and an FDA-cleared sleep apnea risk feature landing in fall 2026 that Oura lacks. Over two years, Oura ($349 hardware + ~$144 in membership) costs roughly $93 more than Samsung's flat $400, and the gap keeps growing. iPhone owners should get the Oura; Galaxy phone owners who hate subscriptions should get the Samsung.

How to Choose a Sleep-Tracking Ring (What Actually Matters)

A smart ring is the ideal sleep tracker on paper: nothing on your wrist, nothing buzzing, nothing glowing at 3 a.m. But rings differ in ways that matter a lot more at night than a spec sheet suggests. Weigh these before you pay.

Your phone decides half the matchup. This is the bluntest difference between these two rings. The Galaxy Ring works only with Android phones, and its best features — the Energy Score, Galaxy AI sleep summaries, gesture controls — need a Samsung Galaxy phone. The Oura Ring 4 works with both iOS and Android through its own app. If you carry an iPhone, the Samsung ring is simply not an option.

Subscription tolerance. Oura’s membership is $5.99 a month or $69.99 a year, and it is not optional if you want the scores and insights that make the ring worth owning. Over two years that is roughly $144 on top of the $349 hardware. Samsung charges nothing, ever. Ask yourself honestly: would a monthly fee you forgot about annoy you, or is it fine for a service you use every morning?

How deep the sleep data goes. Both rings track light, deep, and REM sleep, heart rate, overnight blood oxygen, and skin temperature. Oura goes deeper: validated staging, nightly HRV trends, breathing-disturbance tracking, cardiovascular age, and a Readiness Score that folds it all together. Samsung gives you a Sleep Score, an Energy Score, and sleep coaching — solid, but the research pedigree is thinner.

Fit, because you wear it 24/7. A ring that is half a size off will either slide around and wreck sensor accuracy or wake you up pinching. Both brands ship free sizing kits, and you should wear the test ring for at least a full day and night before ordering — fingers swell while you sleep. The Samsung ring is lighter (2.3–3.0g vs Oura’s 3.3–5.2g) and thinner, which some sleepers notice on their index finger.

Battery and charging. Both rings last most of a week, which means one short charge session weekly, not nightly like a smartwatch. Samsung’s AirPods-style charging case holds about 1.5 extra charges, so you can travel for nearly three weeks without a cable. Oura charges on a puck in about 80 minutes.

The app you will open every morning. You will spend more time in the companion app than touching the ring. Oura’s app is a sleep journal with coaching (Oura Advisor) and deep trend views. Samsung Health is a general wellness hub where the ring’s data merges with your phone and watch. Neither is wrong — but try screenshots of both before you commit.

Our Winner: Oura for Sleep Science, Samsung for Everything Else About the Purchase

There is no single winner here — the right ring depends on your phone and your feelings about subscriptions. But there are clear winners for each scenario.

Best for sleep-data nerds: Oura Ring 4. If you want the deepest, most validated picture of your nights — staging you can trust, HRV trends, temperature deviations, breathing disturbances, a Readiness Score that actually predicts how you feel — Oura is still the ring to beat. No other ring has its research pedigree, with 100+ peer-reviewed studies behind the platform.

Best for Samsung phone owners: Galaxy Ring. The Energy Score pulling ring, watch, and phone data together is genuinely greater than the sum of its parts, and every Galaxy-only perk (gesture controls, on-device AI summaries) only applies here. At $399.99 with no subscription, it is also the cheapest premium ring to own long-term.

Best value over two years: Galaxy Ring, easily. The math is not close. Oura costs $349 plus roughly $144 in membership over two years — about $493 — versus Samsung’s $399.99 flat. That $93+ gap keeps widening every year you keep the ring. If two rings track your sleep about equally well, paying a forever-tax for the privilege is a real consideration.

Best for iPhone owners: Oura Ring 4, by default. The Galaxy Ring does not work with iPhones at all. Oura’s iOS app is a first-class citizen, so this pick makes itself.

The 2026 wildcard: sleep apnea screening. Samsung announced in July 2026 that the Galaxy Ring will get FDA-cleared sleep apnea risk detection via a fall 2026 software update — the first smart ring with that clearance. Oura tracks breathing disturbances but carries no such clearance. This is a risk screening tool, not a diagnosis, and anyone with real symptoms should still see a clinician. But as a differentiator, it is meaningful.

The bottom line: buy the Oura if you want the best sleep science and do not mind renting it monthly. Buy the Samsung if you are on Android, hate subscriptions, and want a lighter ring that still covers every sleep metric that matters. Both will get you to the same place most mornings — the difference is how much you pay along the way, and how deep the rabbit hole goes when you tap your sleep score.

In-Depth Reviews

Oura Ring 4 — 9/10 for sleep science, 6/10 for the subscription pill. The Gen 4 hardware fixed the biggest complaint about earlier Oura rings: the interior sensor bumps are gone, replaced by recessed Smart Sensing that sits flush against the skin, which made the ring noticeably more comfortable overnight and cut blood-oxygen dropout gaps by about 30%. It is still the ring that takes sleep most seriously — four-stage tracking validated against polysomnography, nightly HRV with 98.4% accuracy versus ECG, skin-temperature deviation that flags illness before you feel it, and a Readiness Score that remains the best morning number in wearables. Daytime Stress and the 14-day Resilience score add a layer Oura did not have before: it is no longer just a sleep ring. Weaknesses: the membership is a real tax — $69.99 a year, forever, with the ring’s best features locked behind it — and at 3.3–5.2g it is the heavier of the two rings. Price: from $349 on Amazon (plus membership).

Samsung Galaxy Ring — 8/10 overall, 10/10 for no-subscription value. Samsung’s first ring is a remarkably polished debut: Grade 5 titanium, a slim concave profile that resists scratches, and at 2.3–3.0g it is one of the lightest smart rings you can buy. Sleep tracking is genuinely good — stages, heart rate, SpO2, temperature, a Sleep Score out of 100, and sleep coaching — and the late-2025 algorithm update closed much of the gap to Oura. The charging case is a small delight: 1.5 extra charges inside means nearly three weeks between cables. And everything is free after the $399.99 — no tier, no upsell, no “premium insights” lock. Weaknesses: Android only, full stop, and several of the best features (Energy Score depth, gesture controls, on-device AI summaries) need a Galaxy phone specifically. Raw sensor accuracy is competent rather than category-leading, and some reviews note occasional odd SpO2 dips. Price: $399.99 on Amazon, no subscription ever.

Worth knowing before you buy either: RingConn’s Gen 2 (from $299, no subscription) and the Ultrahuman Ring Air (from $349, no subscription) exist and are credible alternatives, with RingConn in particular offering superb battery life. But neither matches Oura’s sleep-science depth or Samsung’s ecosystem integration, which is why this comparison stays focused on the two flagships.

Frequently asked questions

What does the Oura Ring 4 actually cost over two years compared to the Galaxy Ring?

The Oura Ring 4 starts at $349, plus Oura Membership at $5.99 a month or $69.99 a year — roughly $144 over two years, or about $493 total including the first free month. The Samsung Galaxy Ring costs $399.99 once, with no subscription, so its two-year cost stays $399.99. That makes the Galaxy Ring about $93 cheaper to own over two years, and the gap widens every year after.

Which ring is more accurate for sleep stages, Oura or Samsung?

The Oura Ring 4 is more accurate and more proven: its sleep staging has been validated against polysomnography with 94.4% sensitivity, and its heart rate variability readings hit 98.4% accuracy against medical ECG. Samsung refreshed the Galaxy Ring's sleep algorithm in late 2025 and reviewers call it genuinely good — competitive for everyday use — but it has far fewer independent validation studies behind it.

Do I need a Samsung phone to use the Galaxy Ring?

You need an Android phone running Android 11 or later, but not necessarily a Samsung one. Basic tracking works on any compatible Android. However, the best features — the full Energy Score, Galaxy AI sleep summaries, and gesture controls — are gated to Samsung Galaxy phones. The ring does not work with iPhones at all.

Does the Oura Ring 4 work without the subscription?

The ring still functions, but you lose what makes it special: the Sleep, Readiness, and Activity scores, detailed sleep-stage data, trends, and history are locked behind Oura Membership ($5.99/month or $69.99/year). Without it you get basics like step counts, sleep duration, and simple heart rate data. The subscription is effectively required if you are buying it for sleep insights.

Which ring is more comfortable to sleep in, and how is battery life?

The Galaxy Ring is the lighter and thinner of the two (2.3–3.0g vs the Oura's 3.3–5.2g), which some sleepers notice on the finger overnight. The Oura's recessed sensor design is smoother than its predecessors but the ring is still a touch bulkier. On battery, Oura leads slightly at up to 8 days versus Samsung's up to 7 days (6 days on the smallest sizes) — but Samsung's charging case holds 1.5 extra charges, giving it nearly three weeks of cable-free use.

The Takeaway

  • Best sleep science: Oura Ring 4 — from $349 plus $5.99/month or $69.99/year membership. The deepest, most validated sleep data on a finger.
  • Best no-subscription pick: Samsung Galaxy Ring — $399.99 once, never billed again. Lighter, thinner, and excellent inside the Galaxy ecosystem.
  • The two-year math: Oura ~$493 vs Samsung $399.99. The subscription gap grows every year you own the ring.
  • Phone rules: iPhone owners must buy Oura; the Galaxy Ring is Android-only and best with a Samsung phone.
  • 2026 wildcard: the Galaxy Ring is slated to get FDA-cleared sleep apnea risk detection this fall — a first for smart rings.

Whichever ring you choose, order the free sizing kit first and wear it for a full day and night — fingers swell while you sleep, and fit decides sensor accuracy. And if your sleep problems are more than curiosity about your scores — loud snoring with pauses, gasping, or exhaustion despite full nights — skip the gadgets and talk to your doctor or a sleep clinic. A ring tracks; a clinician diagnoses.
