Therabody SleepMask Review: Does the Vibrating Blackout Mask Work?

Therabody put a gentle vibration motor inside a true blackout sleep mask. We looked at how the vibration works, how dark it really gets, battery life, and whether the $110 price makes sense.

Share
9 min read
By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Oct 13, 2026 · Last updated Oct 13, 2026

A sleep mask is the cheapest sleep upgrade there is, so adding a battery and a vibration motor to one is a bold move. Therabody — the company behind the Theragun — did exactly that: the SleepMask pairs a genuine 100% blackout design with three calming vibration patterns aimed at the brow, meant to help you unwind before sleep. It was even named one of the best sleep masks of 2026 by Men's Journal.

Quick answer

Yes, the Therabody SleepMask works — as a blackout mask first, with vibration as a bonus. At $110, it delivers true 100% light blocking with zero eye pressure, plus three adjustable vibration patterns (Pulse, Wave, Constant) at the brow with a 15-minute auto shut-off. The battery lasts about 4 hours (roughly 16 sessions). It's a great premium mask, but the vibration is a comfort feature, not proven sleep therapy.

How the vibration feature works

The headline feature is a small vibration module positioned at the glabella — the spot between your eyebrows — that delivers three patterns: Pulse, Wave and Constant. Each pattern has adjustable intensity, and the mask remembers the last pattern you used so you don't have to set it up every night. The idea is sensory distraction: by keeping your attention on a slow, rhythmic physical sensation, the mask gives a racing mind somewhere calmer to sit while you drift off.

This is the same relaxation logic behind weighted blankets and handheld massagers — steady, predictable input that competes with anxious thoughts. Men's Journal's 2026 best sleep mask roundup specifically praised the vibration for potentially helping users fall asleep faster and stay asleep, and Therabody's own product copy positions it as a way to settle the nervous system before bed. That said, be clear-eyed about what this is: a calming pre-sleep ritual, not clinically proven sleep therapy. There is no published evidence that brow vibration shortens sleep onset, and Therabody doesn't claim FDA clearance for any medical effect — the mask is FDA registered as a general device, which is a listing, not an endorsement of sleep benefits.

One thoughtful detail: the vibration fades out automatically after 15 minutes rather than cutting off abruptly. That's long enough to fall asleep to, but short enough that you won't be buzzed awake by it later in the night — and it's why the battery stretches as far as it does. Don't want the buzz at all? It works perfectly as a plain blackout mask with the vibration switched off, which makes the feature genuinely optional rather than a forced gimmick.

Blackout design and comfort

Vibration aside, this is first and foremost an excellent blackout mask. The design is a true 100% blackout: contoured, padded eye cups that sit off the eyes completely, creating a dark pocket you can blink inside. There is zero pressure on the eyes or the bridge of the nose, which is the thing cheap flat masks get wrong — they press on your eyelids, and you notice it the moment you try to fall asleep. The fabric is soft and breathable rather than plasticky, and the fit adapts to different face shapes.

The strap is adjustable and non-slip, and it doesn't tangle hair — a small but real comfort point if you've ever woken up with a mask strap knotted in the back of your head. Therabody says the contoured, flexible shape suits all sleeping positions, including side sleeping. That's an important claim to stress-test, because side sleepers press the mask against the pillow all night: the lightweight 2.3 oz build and flexible frame mean it compresses without digging in, and the strap holds position when you roll over.

Hygiene is handled better than on most tech masks: the internal padding layer is removable and machine washable. Sweat and skin oils build up on anything worn against the face nightly, so washable padding is the difference between a mask that lasts and one that gets gross within a month. For the full darkness story, pair any mask with the rest of the bedroom environment — our research on ideal bedroom temperature and our best smart sleep masks roundup both cover why a cool, dark room matters as much as the mask itself.

What it feels like night after night

In daily use, the SleepMask's best quality is that it disappears. The contoured cups mean your eyes never touch fabric, and the vibration — on the lowest intensity — reads as a faint hum at the brow rather than a massage-gun assault. Because it fades out after 15 minutes, you'll almost never notice when it stops. The most common friction point in reviews of vibration masks generally is heat: anything with electronics against the forehead runs slightly warmer than plain fabric, though the breathable build keeps it manageable. If you run hot at night, try it with the vibration off first and treat the mask as a pure blackout purchase.

Battery, charging and travel

The SleepMask runs on a rechargeable lithium-ion battery charged over USB-C. Therabody rates it at 4 hours of vibration per charge, which translates to roughly 16 fifteen-minute sessions — about two weeks of nightly use before you reach for a cable. That's generous for a device you only run for a quarter-hour a night, and it means the battery is essentially a non-issue in daily life as long as you charge it when you notice the vibration weakening.

Travel is clearly a design priority: a travel lock prevents the mask from buzzing itself awake inside your luggage, and it ships with a travel pouch. It's also TSA approved, so there's no security-line awkwardness about a vibrating device in your carry-on. At 2.3 oz it won't add meaningful weight to a travel kit, and the washable padding means it survives hotel life better than most. For frequent flyers, this is arguably the mask's strongest use case — a blackout mask plus a calming ritual in one pouch, no app required at 35,000 feet.

The Therabody app and sound content

Like all Therabody products, the SleepMask connects to the Therabody app, which includes free TheraMind sound content — a library of science-backed audio designed for relaxation and sleep. You don't need the app to use the mask (the vibration controls are on the device), so think of it as an optional extra rather than a requirement. That's the right call: sleep gadgets that demand an app just to function tend to get abandoned the first time the Bluetooth pairing acts up at midnight.

The app does add genuine value for the sound content alone, especially if you're the type who falls asleep to audio. If you prefer hardware for that, our guide to the best white noise machines for light sleepers covers dedicated options, and if a snoring partner is the actual problem, the best earplugs for sleeping with a snoring partner will do more for your sleep than any mask feature.

Honest limitations

The price is the first and biggest objection. At $110, the SleepMask costs roughly three times what an excellent non-electronic blackout mask costs — and ten times a good basic one. If all you want is darkness, a $35 Manta or even a $10 MZOO gets you 95% of the blackout experience. You're paying the premium entirely for the vibration, so be honest with yourself about whether a calming brow buzz is something you'd actually use or just a novelty you'd try twice.

The second limitation is evidence. Therabody markets the vibration as a way to settle the nervous system and support deeper sleep, but the company's materials don't cite clinical studies showing improved sleep onset or quality. The mechanism is plausible — rhythmic sensory input is relaxing — but plausible isn't proven, and you shouldn't buy this as a treatment for insomnia or any sleep problem. It's a wellness comfort feature, full stop.

Practically: it's heavier and warmer than a plain mask, it needs charging every couple of weeks, and the vibration won't be silent to a very light-sleeping partner (though at low intensity it's barely audible across the bed). There's also a one-year limited warranty, which is standard but short for a $110 electronic device — if the motor dies in month thirteen, you're buying a new mask. Finally, nothing here helps with sound: if noise is what wakes you, this mask doesn't address it at all.

Who should buy it — and who shouldn't

Buy it if: you want the best-feeling blackout mask in its class and the idea of a calming pre-sleep vibration ritual genuinely appeals to you. It's also a strong pick for frequent travelers (travel lock, pouch, TSA-approved, washable padding) and for side sleepers who've struggled with masks that shift or press on the eyes. If you've used Therabody products before and like the ecosystem, this slots right in.

Skip it if: you just want darkness — a Manta Sleep Mask at $35 is the smarter buy. Skip it too if you run very hot at night, if your partner is an extremely light sleeper who notices every hum, or if you're hoping a gadget will fix insomnia — that's a conversation with a sleep specialist, not a purchase. And if sound is the real issue, put your money toward earplugs or a white noise machine instead.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Therabody SleepMask's vibration actually help you fall asleep?

The three vibration patterns (Pulse, Wave, Constant) are designed as a calming pre-sleep ritual rather than a proven sleep treatment. Many reviewers find the gentle brow vibration helps them unwind and stop racing thoughts, and Men's Journal named it a best sleep mask of 2026. But there is no clinical evidence that it shortens sleep onset, so treat the vibration as a pleasant bonus on top of an excellent blackout mask.

How much does the Therabody SleepMask cost?

The Therabody SleepMask lists at $110 on Therabody's own site, and that is the price Men's Journal cited in its 2026 best sleep mask roundup. Retailers sometimes discount it — one US electronics retailer recently had it at $84.99 — so check current pricing before you buy. Prices checked October 2026.

How long does the Therabody SleepMask battery last?

The SleepMask offers about 4 hours of vibration per charge, which works out to roughly 16 fifteen-minute sessions — so around two weeks of nightly use before you need the USB-C charger. The vibration fades out automatically after 15 minutes each night, which is why the battery stretches further than the raw hours suggest.

Can you use the Therabody SleepMask without the vibration turned on?

Yes. The vibration is fully optional, and the mask works perfectly as a plain 100% blackout mask with it switched off. If you only want the vibration some nights — say, for naps or travel — just turn it on when you want it; it remembers your last-used pattern.

Is the Therabody SleepMask good for side sleepers?

Yes. The mask's contoured design applies zero pressure on the eyes or nose, and Therabody says it suits back, side and stomach sleeping. The flexible, lightweight 2.3 oz build and adjustable non-slip strap help it stay in place when you roll over.
