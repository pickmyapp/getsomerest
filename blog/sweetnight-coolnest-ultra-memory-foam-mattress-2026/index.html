SweetNight CoolNest Ultra Memory Foam Mattress Explained (2026)

SweetNight's CoolNest Ultra memory foam mattress pairs a 5-stage cooling system with 7-zone support foam, in Queen and King sizes from $729.99 to $1,029.99.

SweetNight's CoolNest series expanded on August 25, 2026, and the CoolNest Ultra sits at the top of it. It is an all-foam memory foam mattress built around two ideas: a five-stage cooling system meant to keep the sleep surface from warming up overnight, and a wavy-cut foam layer divided into seven support zones for the head, shoulders, back, waist, hips, legs, and feet. It comes in Queen and King only, in 12-inch, 14-inch, and 16-inch profiles, priced from $729.99 to $1,029.99.

Quick answer

The short answer: the CoolNest Ultra is SweetNight's flagship all-foam cooling mattress, launched in 2026. It pairs a 5-stage cooling system — Ice Silk cover, phase-change foam, breathable AirMesh foam, gel, and airflow channels — with 7-zone wavy-cut support foam, in Queen and King from $729.99 to $1,029.99, with a 100-night trial and 10-year warranty. Independent NapLab testing scored its cooling 8.0 and pressure relief 9.0, but noted the cool-touch feel fades after a couple of minutes in one spot.

At a Glance: The New Flagship

SweetNight has sold cooling mattresses for years, but the CoolNest Ultra is the most layered cooling design the brand has put into one all-foam bed. The series expanded on August 25, 2026, and the Ultra headlines it with five cooling stages stacked on top of a seven-zone support core — a combination aimed squarely at sleepers who like the conforming feel of memory foam but have always found it too warm.

  • The cooling stack: a cool-touch Ice Silk cover, PCMflux phase-change foam, AirMesh breathable foam, gel, and ventilation and airflow channels — five stages, working in sequence rather than relying on a single cool fabric.
  • The support core: wavy-cut 7-zone ergonomic foam covering head, shoulders, back, waist, hips, legs, and feet, over a high-density support foam foundation with an anti-slip base.
  • The headline claim: SweetNight's lab tests claim up to 11° cooler sleep — a manufacturer claim, and one that independent testing qualifies (see below).
  • The build credentials: OEKO-TEX certified fabric, CertiPUR-US certified foams, and a fiberglass-free construction.
  • The catch: Queen and King only, all-foam only, and the cool-touch sensation fades after a couple of minutes in one spot according to NapLab.

Priced from $729.99 for a 12-inch Queen to $1,029.99 for a 16-inch King, with a 100-night trial and 10-year warranty, the Ultra sits in the mid-range — well below premium cooling flagships, and close enough to sale prices (a 14-inch Queen listed at $679.98 on Amazon) that timing your purchase matters.

How to Choose Between the Three Profiles

The cover, the cooling stack, and the 7-zone support layer are shared across the line, so the choice comes down to height, feel, and budget.

Pick the 12-inch (Medium) if you like a firmer, more supportive memory foam feel, you sleep on your back or stomach, or you want the lowest price — the 12-inch Queen starts at $729.99. A thinner comfort stack means you sit closer to the support foam, which suits sleepers who dislike a deep, sinking feel.

Pick the 14-inch (Medium Plush) if you want a middle ground: more pressure-relieving comfort foam than the 12-inch, without the tallest price tag. This is the profile to look at first if you are a side sleeper or a combination sleeper who changes position during the night, and it is also the profile that shows up most often in retailer listings — one Amazon listing prices a 14-inch Queen at $679.98.

Pick the 16-inch (Medium Plush) if you want the plushest feel in the line and the most comfort foam, and you are happy paying up to $1,029.99 for the 16-inch King. Taller profiles also sit higher on a bed frame, which matters if you have a low platform base or a tall headboard to match.

One honest caveat before you buy: the cooling story is genuinely layered, but no all-foam mattress stays cool by itself all night. Independent NapLab testing found the cool-touch cover feel fades after a couple of minutes in one spot, with slight heat retention after that. Pairing the mattress with breathable sheets — see our linen vs cotton guide — will do more for overnight temperature than any single foam layer can.

Our Verdict: Who Should Buy It

For most buyers, the 14-inch Medium Plush Queen is the sensible default: it is the middle profile, it balances pressure relief with support, and it is the version most likely to appear at a sale price — the $679.98 Amazon listing for a 14-inch Queen undercuts the $729.99 list price of the smaller 12-inch Queen, which tells you how much sale timing matters here.

Who should skip it for now? Anyone who needs a Twin, Full, or California King — only Queen and King are offered. Anyone who wants a hybrid with coils should look at the separate CoolNest Hybrid instead, not this all-foam model. And anyone expecting the ‘up to 11° cooler’ claim to feel like air conditioning will be disappointed: the independent evidence points to a mattress that starts cool and manages heat reasonably, not one that actively refrigerates the bed.

For everyone else — especially hot sleepers who like memory foam but have been burned by heat-trapping foam before, and shoppers who want a fiberglass-free, CertiPUR-US certified build at a mid-range price — the CoolNest Ultra is a well-specified launch, and the 100-night trial makes it a low-risk one to try. What the Independent Testing Says

The most useful independent data point is NapLab's review. Its scores — cooling 8.0, pressure relief 9.0, edge support 8.1 — paint a mattress that relieves pressure very well, supports edges decently, and cools well but not exceptionally. That pressure relief score of 9.0 is the standout number, and it lines up with the 7-zone design brief: a wavy-cut foam that gives more at the shoulders and hips and holds firmer at the waist and back should, in principle, spread pressure evenly.

The cooling nuance matters more than the score. NapLab noted that the cool-touch cover feel fades after a couple of minutes in one spot, with slight heat retention after that. That is the normal behaviour of a passive cool-touch fabric: it feels cold on first contact because it pulls heat from your skin quickly, then equalises. It does not mean the mattress sleeps hot — the phase-change foam, AirMesh layer, gel, and airflow channels are there to slow the warm-up after that first touch — but it does mean the headline sensation is front-loaded.

The edge support split is worth noting too. SweetNight's own page calls edge support average, while NapLab scored it 8.1, a solid result. All-foam mattresses rarely match hybrids on edge support, and the high-density support foam foundation plus anti-slip base here are doing the structural work. If you regularly sit on the edge of the bed to get dressed, test that during the 100-night trial rather than trusting either number.

Frequently asked questions

What is the SweetNight CoolNest Ultra mattress?

It is the flagship of SweetNight's CoolNest series, an all-foam memory foam mattress launched in 2026. It combines a five-stage Advanced CoolNest Ultra cooling system with a wavy-cut 7-zone ergonomic support foam, and it is sold in Queen and King sizes only.

How much does the CoolNest Ultra cost?

List prices run from $729.99 for a 12-inch Queen to $1,029.99 for a 16-inch King. Sale prices run lower at times — the mattress featured in Labor Day sale coverage in 2026, and an Amazon listing shows a 14-inch Queen at $679.98. Prices vary by retailer and by sale period.

What are the three height and feel options?

The 12-inch profile has a Medium feel, while the 14-inch and 16-inch profiles are Medium Plush. The taller profiles add more comfort foam above the same high-density support foundation, which is why they feel softer even though the core design is shared.

How does the 5-stage cooling system work?

It layers five features: a cool-touch Ice Silk cover, PCMflux phase-change foam that absorbs heat, AirMesh breathable foam, gel, and ventilation and airflow channels. SweetNight's lab tests claim up to 11° cooler sleep — that is a manufacturer claim, and independent testing tells a more mixed story (see below).

What trial and warranty does it come with?

A 100-night trial and a 10-year warranty. Those are the standard terms SweetNight lists for this mattress; confirm the details with the retailer you buy from, since marketplace listings can carry different return terms.
