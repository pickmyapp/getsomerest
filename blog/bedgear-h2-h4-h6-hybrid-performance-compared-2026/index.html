BEDGEAR H2 vs H4 vs H6 Hybrid Performance Mattresses Compared: Firm vs Medium vs Plush (2026)

BEDGEAR's H2 is firm at $1,499.99, while the H4 and H6 are $1,999.99 in Queen. Firm vs medium vs plush compared: React pillowtop, cooling covers, and who each mattress suits.

BEDGEAR's Hybrid Performance line used to be a two-feel collection. The July 2026 expansion turns it into a three-feel lineup with a clear ladder: the new H2 is firm, the redesigned H4 is medium, and the updated H6 is plush. All three share the same hybrid build — adaptive foam over individually wrapped coils — and the same cooling platform. The differences are the feel, the pillowtop, and $500: the H2 costs $1,499.99 in Queen, while the H4 and H6 cost $1,999.99. Here is how to pick between them without lying on every mattress in the store.

Quick answer

The short answer: the H2 is firm ($1,499.99 Queen), the H4 is medium ($1,999.99 Queen), and the H6 is plush ($1,999.99 Queen). The H4 and H6 cost $500 more because they include the React Blend Pillowtop; the H2 skips it for a firmer, more streamlined surface. Side sleepers should lean H6, stomach sleepers H2, and combination sleepers H4.

At a Glance: The Three-Feels Lineup

BEDGEAR expanded its Hybrid Performance collection in July 2026 with a simple idea: one mattress line, three feels, no wrong answer if you know your sleep position. Here is the lineup in 30 seconds.

  • H2 — firm, $1,499.99 (Queen): the new model, built for sleepers who want firmer support. Streamlined comfort profile, no pillowtop, maximum airflow at the sleep surface.
  • H4 — medium, $1,999.99 (Queen): the redesigned middle model, now with the React Blend Pillowtop. The default pick for back sleepers and combination sleepers.
  • H6 — plush, $1,999.99 (Queen): the updated soft model, also with the React Blend Pillowtop. The pick for dedicated side sleepers and plush-feel lovers.
  • Shared across all three: hybrid construction (adaptive foam + individually wrapped coils), Ver-Tex cooling cover, Air-X mesh side panels, air vents, Split Head sizes, and CertiPUR-US certified foam.
  • The $500 question: the H4 and H6 cost $500 more than the H2, and the pillowtop is the reason. If you want medium or plush, it is worth it; if you want firm, the H2 saves you money for the feel you prefer.

BEDGEAR's own framing is worth repeating: sleep is personal because people are different, and the brand's job is to offer options rather than dictate one comfort level. The H2/H4/H6 ladder is that philosophy in product form.

How to Choose: Feel First, Price Second

Mattress shopping has one rule that matters more than any spec sheet: your sleep position decides your firmness, and your firmness decides your mattress. Everything else — cooling covers, coil counts, pillowtops — is secondary.

Side sleepers: start with the H6. Sleeping on your side puts your weight on two small points, the shoulder and the hip. A plush surface lets those points sink in, which keeps your spine straight instead of bowed. The H6's React Blend Pillowtop is the softest surface in the line, and it is the reason the H6 exists. If you wake up with a numb arm or a sore shoulder, the H2 will make it worse, not better.

Stomach sleepers: the H2, no contest. Stomach sleeping is the position most sensitive to a too-soft mattress, because hips sink first and the lower back arches. The H2's firm feel and streamlined comfort profile — no thick pillowtop to sink into — keep hips level. It is also the cheapest model, which is a rare alignment of best and cheapest.

Back sleepers: it depends on what you like. Back sleepers who prefer a firm, supportive, on-top-of-the-bed feel should pick the H2. Back sleepers who want gentle contouring under the lower back — the feeling of the mattress filling the small of the back — should pick the H4. If you switch positions during the night, the H4's medium feel is the safest default in the whole lineup.

Hot sleepers: all three are equal. This is the one category where the lineup does not differentiate. Every model uses the Ver-Tex cooling cover, Air-X mesh side panels, and air vents. The H2's slimmer comfort profile may sleep marginally cooler because there is less material to trap heat, but BEDGEAR markets the same cooling story across all three, and there is no independent test showing a meaningful gap.

Couples: look at Split Head sizes. All three models come in Split Head configurations, so each side of the bed can adjust independently on a compatible adjustable base. For couples with different firmness needs, BEDGEAR's other line — the Modular Hybrid Naked collection, with independent firmness per side — is worth comparing before you commit to a single-feel H model.

Our Verdict: The H4 Is the Default, the H2 Is the Value

If you forced us to pick one mattress for the most people, it would be the H4. Medium is the most forgiving firmness in any lineup: it suits back sleepers, combination sleepers, and side sleepers who do not want a very soft bed. The React Blend Pillowtop gives it the pressure relief the H2 lacks, and at $1,999.99 in Queen it sits in the normal range for a premium hybrid from a retail brand.

But the H2 is the smarter buy for two groups. Stomach sleepers and firm-bed lovers get the best mattress in the line for them at the lowest price — $500 less than its siblings — because the pillowtop they do not want is also the thing they are not paying for. And hot sleepers who sleep warm get the same cooling platform with a slimmer comfort stack, which is a plausible (if unproven) edge.

The H6 is the specialist: the right pick for dedicated side sleepers and anyone who loves a plush, pillowtop feel, and an unnecessary $500 for everyone else. Plush is the firmness most likely to be returned by back and stomach sleepers, so do not buy the H6 for its luxury feel unless you are sure plush is your feel — ideally by testing it in store first.

What the Launch Coverage Emphasized

Coverage of the July 2026 expansion focused on two things: personalization and the pillowtop. BEDGEAR's president, Eugene Alletto, framed the lineup with a line that every outlet quoted: sleep is personal because people are different — different body types, different comfort preferences, different ways of moving through the night — and the brand's job is to offer options rather than dictate one comfort level. The three-feel ladder is the product version of that philosophy.

The React Blend Pillowtop got the most technical attention. It is the first time BEDGEAR has built its Performance Pillow technology into a mattress, combining adaptive materials with silk-like fibers for pressure relief, support, and airflow. It is fully integrated into the H4 and H6 — not a removable topper — which is why those models cost more and why the H2, which skips it, can sit $500 lower.

Reviewers also noted what did not change: the cooling platform. Ver-Tex covers, Air-X mesh side panels, and air vents appear on every model, and the foam meets CertiPUR-US standards across the line. BEDGEAR's pitch is that cooling is table stakes, not an upsell — you do not pay extra for it by moving up the lineup, you pay extra for feel.

One gap in the coverage: no independent firmness testing yet. The H2/H4/H6 labels are BEDGEAR's, and no major review outlet had published instrumented or long-term testing of the expanded lineup as of October 2026. Treat the feel descriptions as a starting point for an in-store test, not a verdict.

How We Reported This Story

This article is a product comparison based on manufacturer specifications and launch reporting, not a hands-on test — we have not slept on the H2, H4, or H6. Firmness labels, prices, and construction details come from BEDGEAR's July 2026 collection announcement and Furniture World's July 14, 2026 report, which agree on all key facts: the three feels, the $1,499.99/$1,999.99 Queen pricing, the React Blend Pillowtop on the H4 and H6, and the shared cooling platform.

The position guidance in this article follows general mattress-fitting principles, not BEDGEAR's own claims, and the best test remains lying on the mattress in a store for at least 10 minutes in your usual sleep position.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between the BEDGEAR H2, H4, and H6?

Feel. The H2 Hybrid Performance mattress is firm, the redesigned H4 is medium, and the updated H6 is plush. All three are hybrid mattresses pairing adaptive foam with individually wrapped coils, and all three share the same cooling platform: a Ver-Tex cooling cover, Air-X mesh side panels, and air vents for cross-ventilation. The H4 and H6 add the React Blend Pillowtop, an integrated pillowtop that brings BEDGEAR's Performance Pillow materials — adaptive materials with silk-like fibers — into the mattress surface for extra pressure relief.

How much do the H2, H4, and H6 cost?

In Queen size, the H2 retails for $1,499.99, while the H4 and H6 both retail for $1,999.99 — a $500 gap. The H2's lower price reflects its streamlined comfort profile: it skips the React Blend Pillowtop that the H4 and H6 include. All three are available in Split Head sizes for couples who want different adjustability on each side, and the foam in every BEDGEAR Performance mattress meets CertiPUR-US standards for content, emissions, and durability.

Which BEDGEAR mattress is best for side sleepers?

The H6, most likely. Plush mattresses generally suit side sleepers best because the extra give at the shoulder and hip keeps the spine level instead of pushing the shoulder up. The H6's React Blend Pillowtop adds the softest surface in the line, which is exactly what side sleepers need for pressure relief. The H4 is the safer middle ground if you switch between side and back during the night, and the H2's firm surface will feel too hard for most dedicated side sleepers.

Which BEDGEAR mattress is best for back and stomach sleepers?

The H2 for stomach sleepers and most back sleepers who like firm support. A firm surface keeps hips from sinking, which keeps the lower back in a neutral position — the main thing stomach sleepers need from a mattress. Back sleepers are split: those who prefer a supportive, on-top-of-the-bed feel should pick the H2, while back sleepers who want some contouring around the lower back will be happier with the H4's medium feel and pillowtop.

Where can you buy BEDGEAR Hybrid Performance mattresses?

Direct from BEDGEAR.com and at select furniture and mattress retailers. BEDGEAR sells primarily through retail partners rather than a bed-in-a-box model, so trying the H2, H4, and H6 in a store is usually possible — and worth doing, since firm, medium, and plush are subjective labels. The expanded collection, including the new H2 and the React Blend Pillowtop versions of the H4 and H6, launched in July 2026.

Key Takeaways

  • BEDGEAR's expanded Hybrid Performance line runs firm (H2), medium (H4), and plush (H6) — all hybrids of adaptive foam and individually wrapped coils, all with the same Ver-Tex cooling cover and Air-X airflow system.
  • Queen pricing: H2 $1,499.99; H4 and H6 $1,999.99. The $500 gap is the React Blend Pillowtop, integrated into the H4 and H6 and absent from the H2.
  • Side sleepers should lean H6, stomach sleepers H2, and back or combination sleepers H4 — the safest default in the lineup.
  • Cooling does not differentiate the models: every one uses the same cover, mesh panels, and vents, and all foam is CertiPUR-US certified.
  • All three come in Split Head sizes; couples with very different firmness needs should also compare BEDGEAR's Modular Hybrid Naked collection, which personalizes firmness per side.

Reporting note: firmness labels are BEDGEAR's, not a standardized scale, and no independent long-term testing of the expanded lineup had been published as of October 2026. Test in store when possible.
