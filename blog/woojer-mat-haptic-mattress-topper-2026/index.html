Woojer Mat Haptic Mattress Topper Explained (2026)

Woojer's Mat, launched in December 2025 at $799 ($699 introductory), is a foldable 30-inch haptic mat with six transducers covering 1–250 Hz that turns music, movies and games into full-body vibration — an entertainment and wind-down device, not a medical sleep treatment.

By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Nov 6, 2026 · Last updated Nov 6, 2026

What if your bed could let you feel the soundtrack, not just hear it? That is the pitch behind the Woojer Mat, a foldable haptic mat from the company best known for its vibrating gaming vests and straps. Launched in December 2025 at $799, with a $699 introductory price, the Mat takes the low frequencies in whatever you are playing — a film score, a bass-heavy track, a guided relaxation session — and turns them into gentle vibration across your whole body through six built-in transducers. Woojer calls it a haptic wellness mat and mattress topper, but at about 30 inches wide it covers one person, not a whole bed, and it is a wellness and entertainment device, not a medical sleep treatment. Here is what it is, what it costs now, and who should buy — and should not — buy one.

Quick answer

The short answer: the Woojer Mat is a $799 foldable haptic mat ($699 at launch) with six transducers covering 1–250 Hz that turns audio into vibration you feel through your body. It connects by Bluetooth, USB-C or a 3.5 mm cable to any audio source and pairs with Woojer's free Wellness app. Buy it for movies, music and wind-down sessions; do not buy it expecting a proven sleep treatment or a full-bed mattress topper.

At a Glance: The Woojer Mat

Woojer built its name on wearable haptics — the Vest and Strap lines that let gamers and music fans feel bass through their torso. The Mat takes the same idea and lays it flat: instead of wearing the vibration, you lie on it. Six haptic transducers are spread along the mat, roughly beneath the shoulders, back, hips and legs, so the sensation moves through your body instead of buzzing in one spot.

  • Price: $799 list at launch in December 2025, with a $699 introductory price on Woojer's own store. Prices have moved since: when checked in October 2026 the official store showed the original Mat from $999, so confirm the current price before ordering. You can also check Woojer Mat on Amazon.
  • Size: one size only, 190 x 76 cm (about 74.8 x 29.9 inches) and 1.5 inches thick — roughly 30 inches wide, narrower than even a twin mattress. Unfold it on a bed or the floor for a session, then fold it away.
  • Transducers: six precision body zones using Woojer's patented Osci technology, reproducing frequencies from 1 to 250 Hz, including subsonic low end you feel rather than hear.
  • How it connects: Bluetooth 5.0, USB-C digital audio, or a 3.5 mm AUX cable, so it works with practically any audio source — a phone, TV, console or laptop. You can listen on headphones or speakers at the same time, with headphones connected wirelessly or plugged into the Mat's built-in amplifier.
  • Control: a free Woojer Wellness app with a library of vibroacoustic sessions for relaxation and wind-down, plus a glowing touch strip on the mat itself for volume and vibration intensity, so you can adjust it without reaching for your phone.
  • Power: mains powered through a 100 W adapter included in the box. No battery life is listed for the Mat itself, and Woojer sells a separate MAT Power Bank accessory for cord-free use.
  • In the box: the mat, 100 W power adapter, 3.5 mm cable, carrying bag, eye mask and user guide, according to Woojer's product page. Weight is listed by reviewers at about 6.7 kg (around 14.8 lb).
  • Guarantee: 30-day money-back guarantee and a 12-month warranty.

The important context is the size. This is not a topper you fit a sheet over and sleep on every night — it is a single-width session mat for one person, and that shapes whether $799 is worth it to you.

How to Choose: Mat vs Strap vs a Regular Topper

The Mat sits between two very different purchases: Woojer's own wearables, and an ordinary mattress topper. Choose by what you actually want to feel, and where.

Choose the Mat over the Woojer Strap 3 if you want full-body sensation while lying down. The Strap 3 is a single-transducer band worn across the chest or waist, listed at $199, and it is built for gaming, music and VR on the move. The Mat spreads six transducers along your whole body, which is a different experience entirely — closer to lying on a subwoofer than wearing one. If your use case is the sofa or the bed, the Mat wins; if you want haptics while walking around or playing VR standing up, the Strap is the right shape.

Choose the Woojer Vest 3 instead if immersion while seated matters most. The Vest 3, listed at $499 with six Osci TRX2 transducers around the torso, is Woojer's gaming-first product, with up to 8 hours of battery and a design meant for a chair, not a mattress. The Mat suits lying down and winding down; the Vest suits long seated gaming or VR sessions.

Choose a regular mattress topper if your problem is comfort or heat. A conventional topper changes how your mattress feels — softer, cooler, more pressure-relieving — every night, for both sleepers, and usually for well under $200. The Mat does none of that: it adds vibration, not comfort or cooling, and it covers one person only. If you sleep hot, start with our best cooling mattress topper for night sweats instead, and treat the Mat as an entertainment purchase, not a bedding upgrade.

Check the practicalities before you buy. The Mat needs mains power where you use it, unless you add the optional power bank. It is about 30 inches wide, so in a shared bed your partner gets nothing — and may hear or feel nothing either, since the vibration is localized to the mat. Woojer's FAQ also says not to use the Mat with a pacemaker or defibrillator, so check the safety guidance on the product page if that applies to you. The original Mat's cover is not listed as removable or washable — only the newer MAT Plus is described as having a removable, washable cover — so use a light sheet you can launder.

Our Verdict: Who Should Buy It

Buy the Mat if you want movies, music and games to feel physical — and you have a wind-down ritual to pair it with. This is the strongest case for it: a film night in bed, a bass-heavy album with your eyes closed, or a guided relaxation session where the low vibration acts as a sensory anchor. If that sounds like a regular part of your evenings, and $799 (or the current store price) feels fair for dedicated entertainment gear, the Mat is unlike anything else at this price — a home-theatre subwoofer you lie on.

Buy it if you already love Woojer's wearables. If you own a Strap or Vest and wish the sensation covered your whole body while you lie down, the Mat is the natural next step, and the app and audio setup will already be familiar.

Skip it if you want better sleep, less pain, or a better mattress. There is no controlled trial showing this specific mat improves sleep, and Woojer itself describes it as a wellness product, not a medical device. If your budget is $799 and your goal is sleeping better, a cooling topper, better bedding, blackout blinds and a consistent wind-down routine will do more — see our best ambient sounds for deep sleep for a free place to start. Skip it too for a shared bed, or if you need a device that runs all night without a power cable — no battery life is listed.

What Early Hands-On Reviews Say

Because the Mat launched in December 2025, the first hands-on impressions are now almost a year old, and they are consistent on two points: the sensation is real and pleasant, and the sleep claims are unproven.

T3's Lucy Miller tried the Mat in a short hands-on trial published in December 2025 and described setup as straightforward and the experience as soothing during relaxation sessions — but she also found that the single-width size made overnight use in a shared bed impractical, and the trial did not establish any lasting benefit. The Mat is 30 inches wide where even a twin mattress is 38, so it is a one-person device by construction.

Other early coverage struck a similar note. Reviewers praised the build — breathable airmesh and soft-touch fabric, a foldable design with a carrying bag, and touch controls — while warning that vibroacoustic therapy rests on small studies and pilot trials with clinical hardware very different from a consumer mat at home. The evidence is mixed, protocols vary widely, and none of it is a controlled trial of the Woojer Mat itself improving sleep.

Woojer's own framing is careful, and it should be repeated here: the company states the Mat is a wellness product, not a medical device, and is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent disease. Its published research summaries describe studies involving Woojer hardware in other contexts — including a 2025 study on vibration synchronized to breathing — not a trial of the Mat for sleep. Treat the Mat as a comfort and entertainment device with a plausible relaxation use, not as therapy. If you have a sleep disorder or chronic pain, talk to a clinician rather than buying hardware for it.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Woojer Mat a real mattress topper?

Not in the usual sense. It is a foldable mat about 30 inches wide (190 x 76 cm) that you lay on top of your mattress for a session — a movie, music, or a relaxation track — and then fold away. It does not cover a whole bed, takes no fitted sheet, and adds vibration rather than softness or cooling. If you want a conventional topper, see our guide to the best cooling mattress topper for night sweats.

How much does the Woojer Mat cost?

At launch in December 2025 it was listed at $799, with a $699 introductory price on Woojer's store. Prices have moved since: the official store listed the original Mat from $999 when checked in late 2026, and the newer MAT Plus and MAT Pro are on pre-order with shipping from December 1, 2026. Always check the current product page before buying, as discounts change.

Does the Woojer Mat help you sleep?

There is no controlled trial showing that the Mat itself improves sleep, and Woojer describes it as a wellness product, not a medical device. Low-frequency vibration can feel relaxing during a wind-down session — similar in spirit to ambient sound or a weighted blanket — but that is not the same as a proven sleep treatment. Use it as a comfort device, keep the intensity low before bed, and talk to a clinician about any sleep disorder.

What can you connect the Woojer Mat to?

Almost any audio source: it connects by Bluetooth 5.0, USB-C digital audio, or a 3.5 mm AUX cable to a phone, TV, console, or laptop. You can listen on headphones or speakers at the same time, and the free Woojer Wellness app adds a library of vibroacoustic sessions composed for relaxation and wind-down. A touch strip on the mat adjusts volume and vibration intensity without your phone.

Can two people use the Woojer Mat, or sleep on it all night?

No — it is built for one person at a time. At about 30 inches wide it is narrower than a twin mattress, so a partner gets no vibration, and it is designed for sessions, not eight hours of overnight use. It also runs on mains power through a 100 W adapter, with no battery life listed, so plan on using it near an outlet unless you buy the separate power bank accessory.
