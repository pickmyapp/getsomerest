BedJet 3 vs ChiliSleep Dock Pro: Air or Water — Which Active Cooling System Fits Your Bed?

BedJet 3 (from ~$417) vs ChiliSleep Dock Pro (from $999): forced-air vs water bed cooling compared on cooling power, noise, heating and value. October 2026.

Share
10 min read
By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Oct 16, 2026 · Last updated Oct 16, 2026

Passive cooling — breathable sheets, cooling toppers — only goes so far. The BedJet 3 and ChiliSleep's Dock Pro are active systems: they mechanically move heat out of your bed all night. But they do it in completely different ways. The BedJet 3 blows temperature-controlled air under your sheets, like a personal ventilation system. The Dock Pro circulates chilled water through a mattress pad, like a radiator in reverse. Here's how forced air stacks up against water.

Quick answer

The BedJet 3 (from ~$417 single-zone, $1,279 dual-zone) is the cheaper, simpler option: it blows air under your sheets, dries moisture fast, and heats as well as cools — but it can't cool below room temperature. The ChiliSleep Dock Pro (from $999 for the ME half-bed setup, ~$1,299 for the WE full-bed) circulates chilled water from 55°F to 115°F, so it genuinely chills the mattress surface. Both are subscription-free.

Air vs water: the physics that matters

The BedJet 3 is a ventilation system. Its unit pulls in room air, adjusts the temperature of that air, and pushes it under your sheets through a soft hose. Moving air strips heat and moisture away from your body — the same reason a fan feels cooler than still air. It's excellent at drying out night sweats and at taking the stuffy edge off warm bedding. But it is fundamentally bounded by the room: it cannot make your bed colder than the air it pulls in.

The Dock Pro is a conduction system. Its control unit actively chills (or heats) water, then circulates it through a tubeless hydro-membrane mattress pad you sleep on. Water in direct contact with the mattress surface transfers heat far more efficiently than air, so the pad can genuinely drop to 55°F even when the room is warm. It's the difference between a breeze and an ice pack.

  • Cooling power. The Dock Pro's 95 watts of cooling power (roughly double the older Ooler) holds its temperature even in warm rooms. The BedJet's airflow cools you below your skin temperature through evaporation and convection, but never below room temperature.
  • Heating. The BedJet's warm air mode is its secret weapon — it warms the bed in seconds, faster than any water system can heat its thermal mass. The Dock Pro heats to 115°F too, but more gradually.
  • Noise. The BedJet runs 30-46 dB(A) depending on fan speed — a soft whoosh at the top end. The Dock Pro's pump is quieter, a low hum plus faint pad crinkling. Both are white-noise-adjacent; neither is silent.
  • Maintenance. The BedJet asks for nothing — no water, no filters. The Dock Pro needs distilled water refills and periodic cleaning of the hydro system. If "appliance that just works" matters, BedJet wins.
  • Feel in bed. The BedJet needs no mattress pad — air flows under your normal sheets. The Dock Pro's pad sits between you and the mattress; the tubeless hydro-membrane is thinner and softer than older tube designs, but it's still an extra layer.

The airflow is different from water in a way some people genuinely prefer. You feel it more like a warm or cool breeze than direct cold or heat against your body.

Why the BedJet can't beat a warm room

This is the single most important buying fact in the article. If your bedroom sits at 78°F with no AC, the BedJet cannot give you a 65°F bed — it can only give you moving 78°F air, which feels better than still 78°F air but is still 78°F. In a moderately cool room (68-72°F), the BedJet is genuinely excellent. In a hot apartment with no cooling, the Dock Pro's chilled water is the only one of the two that actually solves the problem. Our guide to sleeping cool without AC covers the low-tech side of the same battle.

Why water wins for deep cooling

Water conducts heat roughly 25 times better than air, and the Dock Pro exploits that fully: the tubeless ChiliPad Pro membrane spreads water across five times the surface area of older tube designs, with no tubes to feel under you. Set it to 60°F and the mattress surface actually gets to 60°F. That's why hot sleepers who tried fans and breathable toppers and still woke up sweating tend to land on water systems — they're the first thing that changes the temperature of the surface itself rather than just the air above it.

The no-subscription angle

Both systems are buy-it-once products: every feature, every schedule, every temperature works with no monthly fee. That sounds unremarkable until you look at the smart-cooling market — Eight Sleep's Autopilot is mandatory for the first year at $199, and most of the intelligence disappears if you cancel. If the idea of a sleep subscription bothers you, the BedJet and Dock Pro are the two serious systems that never ask for one.

Our verdict: air or water?

Buy the BedJet 3 if you want the most affordable entry into active sleep cooling, you love the feeling of airflow, you deal with night sweats (the BedJet's moisture removal is genuinely excellent), or you want serious heating power too — its warm-air mode is the fastest bed-warmer in the category. It's also the right pick if you refuse all maintenance: no water, no filters, no priming. Just know its hard limit: in a hot room with no AC, it can't give you a cold bed.

Buy the ChiliSleep Dock Pro if you need the deepest cooling available without a subscription — real, sub-room-temperature chilling that holds even when the room is warm. It's the pick for hot sleepers who've exhausted passive options (breathable sheets, cooling toppers) and still wake up warm, and for anyone who wants heating and cooling in one tubeless pad. The price is the obvious trade-off — roughly double the BedJet — plus the water refills. But for raw cooling performance per dollar with no monthly fee, it's the strongest system in its class.

Frequently asked questions

Can the BedJet 3 cool as well as the ChiliSleep Dock Pro?

No — and that's the honest answer. The BedJet blows room-temperature air (slightly cooled by airflow), so it can't drop your bed below the ambient room temperature. The Dock Pro circulates actively chilled water, so it can bring the mattress surface down to 55°F even in a warm room. The BedJet is a powerful ventilation system; the Dock Pro is a genuine cooling system.

Is the Dock Pro noisy?

Mostly no. The control unit's pump is quiet — owners typically describe a soft hum or white-noise-like sound that fades into the background. The mattress pad itself can make a faint crinkling sound when you move on it. It's quieter than a window fan and far quieter than a portable AC, but extremely noise-sensitive sleepers should know the pump is audible in a silent room.

Does the Dock Pro need water refills?

Yes — it runs on distilled water and you'll top it off every few weeks to a couple of months depending on use, plus occasional filter swaps. It's the main maintenance the system asks for, and the main reason the BedJet's zero-maintenance air system appeals to some buyers.

Does the BedJet need special sheets?

Not required, but recommended. The BedJet works under your existing bedding, and its optional Cloud Sheet ($139+) distributes air more evenly and can split a bed into dual zones. Owners who skip the Cloud Sheet still get most of the benefit — the sheet mainly improves consistency and couples' zoning.

Do either of these require a subscription?

No — and that's a real selling point for both. All features on the BedJet 3 and the Dock Pro work with no monthly fee. (If you're cross-shopping Eight Sleep, know that its Autopilot subscription is mandatory for the first year — see our Pod 6 subscription explainer.)

Key takeaways

  • The BedJet 3 (from ~$417) blows temperature-controlled air under your sheets: great for airflow lovers, night sweats and fast heating — but it can't cool below room temperature.
  • The ChiliSleep Dock Pro (from $999) circulates chilled water for genuine sub-room-temperature cooling down to 55°F — the strongest subscription-free cooler in its class.
  • BedJet setup takes ~15 minutes with zero maintenance; the Dock Pro needs distilled water refills and occasional cleaning.
  • Both are subscription-free — a real advantage over systems that put features behind a monthly fee.
  • In a hot room with no AC, only the Dock Pro can actually deliver a cold bed.
