Manta Sleep Mask vs Alaska Bear: Contoured vs Silk Sleep Masks Compared (2026)

Light leaking into your bedroom costs you deep sleep — and the cheapest fix is a good mask. Two dominate the conversation: the Manta Sleep Mask (~$35), with contoured memory-foam cups that seal out nearly everything, and the Alaska Bear (~$16), a pure mulberry-silk mask with 50,000+ Amazon reviews that costs less than a pizza. We compared them on blackout, comfort, side-sleeping, durability, and value.

Quick answer

The short answer: the Manta Sleep Mask (~$35) is the blackout champion — contoured eye cups, near-zero light leak, excellent for side sleepers, machine washable. The Alaska Bear (~$16) is the comfort-and-value pick — featherweight mulberry silk, gentle on skin and lashes, good enough for rooms that are already mostly dark. Buy the Manta for total darkness; buy the Alaska Bear for cheap, comfortable light blocking.

How to Choose Between Contoured and Silk

The Manta and Alaska Bear are not really competing for the same buyer. The right question is which problem you are solving.

How dark is your room? If daylight floods the bedroom — shift workers, thin curtains, city windows — you need the Manta’s sealed blackout. Flat silk masks leak around the nose bridge, and in a bright room that leak matters. If the room is already dim and you only need to knock down stray glow, the Alaska Bear’s silk is enough and costs less than half.

How do you sleep? Side sleepers punish masks: pillow friction drags straps, cheek compression bunches flat fabric. The Manta’s cups compress without losing their seal, and its wide micro-Velcro strap stays put. The Alaska Bear’s single thin strap migrates on active nights. Back sleepers do fine with either.

Pressure vs. featherweight. Some people cannot stand anything pressing on their eyes; others cannot stand feeling anything at all. The Manta is structured — you feel it, but nothing touches your eyes. The Alaska Bear disappears on your face. Neither is objectively better; pick your sensation.

Pairs well with: a mask handles light, not noise. If a partner snores or the street is loud, pair either mask with earplugs or white noise — see our white noise machine guide and our blackout curtain guide for the full light-and-sound stack.

Frequently asked questions

Can you open your eyes inside the Manta Sleep Mask?

Yes — that is the whole point of the contoured design. The memory-foam eye cups create a cavity over each eye, so your eyelids never touch fabric and you can blink or even open your eyes fully inside the mask. Flat silk masks like the Alaska Bear lie directly against your eyelids, which most people tolerate fine, but the Manta’s zero-pressure design is a genuine comfort upgrade, especially for long sleepers and anyone sensitive to pressure on the eyes.

Does the Alaska Bear silk mask block enough light?

For most bedrooms, yes. The mulberry silk Alaska Bear blocks most ambient light — streetlight, a partner’s reading lamp, early dawn — and its tens of thousands of reviews confirm it works for ordinary use. It will not match the Manta’s near-total blackout: around the nose bridge, some light can seep in. If your room is already reasonably dark and you just need to take the edge off, the silk mask is enough. If you sleep days or need pitch black, the contoured Manta wins.

Which sleep mask is better for side sleepers?

The Manta, by a clear margin. Its eye cups compress slightly under pillow pressure without breaking their seal, and the wide micro-Velcro strap does not ride up the way thin elastic does. Testers report zero light leak in all sleep positions with the Manta. The Alaska Bear’s single thin strap tends to migrate over an active night, and flat silk can bunch when your cheek hits the pillow. If you are a dedicated side sleeper, the Manta’s $35 pays for itself in uninterrupted nights.

How do you wash a sleep mask without ruining it?

The Manta is machine washable — toss the whole thing in on gentle and it survives, which matters for a product sitting on your face eight hours a night. Silk masks like the Alaska Bear should be hand-washed with a gentle detergent and air-dried to protect the silk fibers; machine washing degrades them faster. Plan to wash any mask weekly: a mask collects face oils over roughly 2,500 hours of wear a year, and dirty fabric breaks down faster and can irritate skin.

Is the Manta Sleep Mask worth twice the price of the Alaska Bear?

It depends on what wakes you up. If stray light leaks around your current mask, you sleep on your side, or you have tried flat masks and found them uncomfortable, the Manta’s contoured blackout is worth every dollar of the difference. If your room is already dark, you sleep on your back, and you want the lightest thing possible on your face, the Alaska Bear gives you 80% of the benefit for under $16 — and at that price you can buy two and rotate.
