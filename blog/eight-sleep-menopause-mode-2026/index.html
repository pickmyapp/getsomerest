Eight Sleep Menopause Mode Explained (2026)

Eight Sleep's Menopause Mode, rolling out October 29, 2026, adds tailored Pod cooling for perimenopause through postmenopause, on-demand cooling from a bedside button, and sleep insights — but it requires an Autopilot membership and a Pod.

Share
9 min read
By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Nov 6, 2026 · Last updated Nov 6, 2026

Eight Sleep has turned its temperature-controlled Pod toward one of the most common reasons women wake up at night. Menopause Mode, announced October 28, 2026 and rolling out globally from October 29, 2026, is a new setting in the Eight Sleep app for women in perimenopause through postmenopause. Instead of reacting to overheating after it wakes you, the Pod cools your side of the bed ahead of the hours when hot flashes are most common, learns your preferences over time, and adds a bedside button for instant cooling when one arrives anyway. It also adds sleep insights that show how disrupted nights are affecting your rest and how your patterns compare with other women at the same stage. The catch is familiar to anyone who has priced an Eight Sleep system: Menopause Mode requires an active Autopilot membership on top of the Pod itself, so this is a comfort feature inside a premium, subscription-priced system, not a standalone fix.

Quick answer

The short answer: Menopause Mode is a free software setting for Eight Sleep Pod owners with an active Autopilot membership, rolling out from October 29, 2026. It pre-cools your side of the bed ahead of the hours when hot flashes are most common, cools further when light sleep is detected, and lets you trigger instant cooling from a button on the side of the bed. You still need a Pod — Pod 6 Solo starts at $1,999, standard two-zone Pod 6 from $2,699 — plus Autopilot at $199 to $399 a year.

At a Glance: Menopause Mode

Menopause Mode is not new hardware. It is a software experience inside the Eight Sleep app that changes how an existing Pod behaves at night. Eight Sleep says it was built from more than 57,000 nights of sleep data from 450 peri- and postmenopausal women, and it follows the company's Pregnancy Mode, which launched earlier in 2026 as part of its women's sleep research.

  • Rollout: begins globally October 29, 2026, through the Eight Sleep app on iOS and Android, for every member with an Autopilot membership.
  • Tailored cooling: the Pod lowers the temperature on your side of the bed ahead of the hours when hot flashes are most common, then adjusts that timing as it learns your preferences, bedroom conditions, and seasonal changes.
  • Sleep-stage aware cooling: most hot flashes occur during light sleep, so once light sleep is detected, Autopilot cools your side further to help you stay asleep.
  • On-demand cooling: press a button on the side of the bed and the Pod cools immediately, with no phone needed. You choose how long it runs, and it returns to your normal temperature on its own. Eight Sleep says 8 in 10 women report feeling cooler within 10 minutes of using it.
  • Sleep insights: the app shows how disrupted nights are affecting your sleep and how your patterns compare with other women at the same stage of menopause.
  • Dual-zone comfort: only your side changes temperature, so a partner who runs cold is not left shivering.

Eight Sleep reports that, in its own research, Pod users experienced 56% fewer nighttime hot flashes on average. That is a company-reported figure, not an independently verified result, and it should be read as a comfort claim about cooling, not a medical outcome. This article is about a temperature-comfort feature, not medical advice. If hot flashes or sleep disruption are affecting your health, talk to your clinician.

How to Choose: Is Menopause Mode Right for You?

Start with what you already own. If you already sleep on a Pod with an active Autopilot membership, Menopause Mode costs nothing extra as far as Eight Sleep has disclosed — it arrives as an app update, and turning it on is the obvious move if nighttime overheating is disrupting your sleep. The value question only gets hard if you are buying a Pod to get it.

Choose the Pod for Menopause Mode if you already want active bed cooling. The Pod's real strength is water-based cooling that can hold your side of the bed at a steady temperature all night, from 55°F to 110°F per side, and adjust it automatically as your sleep stage changes. If you sleep hot every night, share a bed with someone who wants a different temperature, and like the idea of contactless sleep tracking without a wearable, Menopause Mode is a genuine reason to pick the Pod over a simpler cooling pad. Read our Pod 6 price and subscription breakdown before you buy, because the membership is part of the real cost.

Choose a cheaper cooling system if cooling alone is the goal. A water-cooled pad such as the ChiliSleep Dock Pro or an air-based BedJet 3 can cool your side of the bed for far less money and without a yearly membership, though neither offers Menopause Mode's automatic pre-cooling schedule, sleep-stage aware adjustments, or menopause-specific insights. Our BedJet 3 vs ChiliSleep Dock Pro comparison walks through that trade-off in detail.

Check the room first. No bed system replaces a cool bedroom. Research consistently points to a bedroom around 65°F as a good starting point for sleep comfort. See our guide to the ideal bedroom temperature for sleep and, if air conditioning is not an option, how to sleep cool without AC for low-cost steps that help any cooling system work better.

Think about your stage and your setup. Menopause Mode is designed for the whole span from perimenopause through postmenopause, and its insights compare your patterns with women at the same stage. If you sleep alone, the Pod 6 Solo is worth a look — see our Pod 6 Solo sizes guide.

The hardware behind Menopause Mode is the Pod itself: a water-circulating cover that fits over your existing mattress, a Hub that heats and chills the water, and sensors in the cover that track heart rate, breathing rate, and sleep stages without anything worn on the body. The current model is the Pod 6, launched September 23, 2026, with 18 sensors in the cover — nine per side, up from one per side on the Pod 5 — and a Hub about half the size of the old one. The Pod 6 Solo costs $1,999 in every size, while the standard two-zone Pod 6 costs $2,699 for a Full, $2,899 for a Queen, and $2,999 for a King or California King. Autopilot is required for the first 12 months at $199 a year for Standard, $299 for Enhanced, or $399 for Elite, and the 5-year warranty needs Enhanced or Elite. You can find the Pod via Amazon search. For the cheaper alternatives, see BedJet 3 on Amazon and ChiliSleep Dock Pro on Amazon. Eight Sleep has not named a specific Pod model cutoff for Menopause Mode; its announcement describes the rollout as going to every member with an Autopilot membership and a Pod, and reporting at launch described it as working on any Pod through the app.

Our Verdict: Who Should Buy a Pod for It

Turn it on if you already own a Pod. For existing Autopilot members, Menopause Mode is a no-cost software update that makes the Pod noticeably smarter about nighttime overheating: pre-cooling ahead of the hours when hot flashes are most common, extra cooling in light sleep, and a bedside button for the nights when one arrives anyway. There is no reason not to try it.

Buy a Pod for it only if the whole system appeals to you. At $1,999 for a Solo plus at least $199 a year for Autopilot — $2,198 in year one before tax, and $2,899 plus membership for a standard Queen — Menopause Mode alone does not justify the purchase. It justifies it as part of a package: dual-zone cooling, contactless sleep tracking, and automatic temperature schedules, for someone who sleeps hot every night and shares a bed. If you are choosing between Pod generations, our Pod 6 vs Pod 5 upgrade guide explains what the newer hardware adds.

Skip the Pod if the budget is the deciding factor. A ChiliSleep Dock Pro from $799 or a BedJet 3 at roughly $437–$498 on sale will cool your side of the bed without a subscription, and a good cooling topper costs less still — see our best cooling mattress topper for night sweats roundup. What those options cannot do is Menopause Mode's automatic pre-cooling and stage-aware adjustments, so the decision is really about how much automation you want, not whether cooling itself is available cheaper. It is, by a wide margin.

What Menopause Mode Means for Sleep Tech

Menopause Mode matters less as a single feature than as a sign of where sleep tech is heading. For years, temperature-controlled beds were sold as a generic comfort upgrade: pick a number, sleep cooler, wake up better. Eight Sleep is now slicing that same hardware into life-stage experiences, starting with Pregnancy Mode earlier in 2026 and now Menopause Mode, each tuned on data from the people it is built for. The 57,000 nights behind Menopause Mode come from 450 peri- and postmenopausal women, a dataset no rival cooling brand can match, because no rival has that many sensor-equipped beds in bedrooms collecting nightly data.

That data advantage is also the honest limit on the claims. The 56% figure comes from Eight Sleep's own research, and the company has not published a peer-reviewed study of Menopause Mode itself or a clear account of how that figure was measured.

The competitive picture is straightforward. No other bed-cooling brand offers a menopause-specific mode, because no other brand pairs water cooling with sleep-stage sensing and a subscription app at this scale. BedJet and ChiliSleep compete on price and simplicity, and for many sleepers that is the right call. Eight Sleep competes on automation and data, and charges accordingly. Menopause Mode sharpens that divide: it is the clearest example yet of the Pod as a software platform that keeps gaining features after purchase — provided you keep paying for Autopilot.

Frequently asked questions

When does Eight Sleep Menopause Mode launch?

Menopause Mode begins rolling out globally on October 29, 2026, through the Eight Sleep app on iOS and Android. Eight Sleep announced it on October 28, 2026. The rollout goes to members with an active Autopilot membership, so it may take some days to reach every account after the 29th.

Do I need a new Pod to use Menopause Mode?

No new hardware is required. Menopause Mode is a software setting in the Eight Sleep app, and Eight Sleep describes it as rolling out to members with an Autopilot membership and a Pod. The current model is the Pod 6, launched September 23, 2026, and reporting at launch described the feature as working on any Pod through the app. Eight Sleep has not published a specific model cutoff, so check the app on your own Pod to confirm it appears.

How much does Menopause Mode cost?

Eight Sleep has not disclosed any extra charge for Menopause Mode itself, beyond the Autopilot membership it requires. That means the cost is the Pod plus Autopilot: Pod 6 Solo from $1,999 or standard Pod 6 from $2,699 to $2,999, plus Autopilot at $199 a year for Standard, $299 for Enhanced, or $399 for Elite. Autopilot is required for the first 12 months of Pod ownership.

How does Menopause Mode cool the bed during a hot flash?

Two ways. First, it works ahead of time: the Pod lowers your side's temperature before the hours when hot flashes are most common, and cools further when light sleep is detected, since most hot flashes occur during light sleep. Second, it works on demand: press the button on the side of the bed and the Pod cools immediately, for a duration you choose, then returns to your normal temperature on its own. Only your side changes, so a partner is not affected.

Is Menopause Mode a medical treatment for menopause?

No. Menopause Mode is a temperature-comfort feature: it adjusts bed temperature for cooling comfort and shows sleep insights about disrupted nights. It does not diagnose or treat menopause or any health condition, and this article is not medical advice. If hot flashes or sleep disruption are affecting your health, talk to your clinician about options.
