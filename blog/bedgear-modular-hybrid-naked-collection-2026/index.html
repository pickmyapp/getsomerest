BEDGEAR Modular Hybrid Naked Collection: Split-Firmness Mattresses Explained

BEDGEAR's 2026 Las Vegas Market launch pairs all-natural latex with its Independent Suspension units, so each side of the bed gets its own firmness — explained.

Share

Couples rarely agree on a mattress, and the industry's answer has usually been to sell them a compromise neither loves. BEDGEAR's answer, unveiled at the 2026 Las Vegas Winter Market, is to stop selling one firmness per bed: its Modular Hybrid Naked Collection pairs all-natural latex comfort layers with Independent Suspension units, so each side of the bed gets its own firmness — and the parts can be swapped, cleaned, and replaced instead of throwing the whole mattress away. Here is what the collection is, how split-firmness actually works, what it costs in context, and who it suits.

Quick answer

The short answer: the Modular Hybrid Naked Collection is BEDGEAR's two-model, natural-latex take on its modular mattress line, launched at the 2026 Las Vegas Market. Each model pairs all-natural latex comfort layers with Independent Suspension units, so each sleeper picks their own side's firmness, with airflow-first construction throughout. BEDGEAR did not publish Naked pricing in its launch release; its modular siblings run roughly $2,499.99 to $3,499.99 queen at retail. The standout idea is not just personalization — it is replaceability: worn or wrong components swap out without replacing the mattress.

At a Glance: The Naked Collection

BEDGEAR has sold personalized sleep since 2009, but the 2026 Las Vegas Winter Market launch sharpened the pitch: one size does not fit all, so stop selling one firmness per bed. The Modular Hybrid Naked Collection is the natural-materials expression of that idea.

  • What it is: a two-model mattress line combining BEDGEAR's modular construction with all-natural latex comfort layers and a cleaner, more minimal aesthetic.
  • The core technology: Independent Suspension units that let each sleeper personalize firmness on their side of the bed, with airflow-first construction through every layer.
  • The sustainability angle: modular components can be removed, cleaned, and replaced over time, extending product life and reducing waste — BEDGEAR's version of the argument that a mattress that fits lasts longer.
  • The ecosystem: the launch also refined BEDGEAR's pillow fitting (by body type, sleep position, temperature preference) and organized its Performance Sheets into Naked and Athleisure collections.
  • The gap: no published pricing for the Naked models themselves; the collection is sold through BEDGEAR and retail partners with retailer-set prices.

Context matters here: BEDGEAR is the official sleep partner of the Dallas Mavericks and sells in more than 6,500 stores worldwide, so this is not a startup experiment — it is a major brand betting that personalization, not price, is the next mattress battleground.

How Split-Firmness Actually Works (and How to Buy It Right)

Split-firmness sounds simple — two firmnesses, one bed — but buying it well takes more thought than buying a fixed mattress. Here is the process.

Start with a fitting, not a firmness label. BEDGEAR's pitch is that mattresses should be fit like shoes: by body type, sleep position, and temperature preference. That fitting matters more for a modular mattress than for a fixed one, because the whole point is that you choose components rather than accepting a factory default. If you buy online without a fitting, use the retailer's sleep-position and body-weight guidance carefully, and lean on the trial period.

Choose each side independently — and honestly. The classic mistake is matching your partner's side out of politeness. A back sleeper generally needs firmer support to keep hips level; a side sleeper needs more give at shoulders and hips. BEDGEAR's Independent Suspension units let each side be set without affecting the other, so there is no reason to compromise — and every reason not to, since a wrong firmness on one side is the reason couples end up mattress shopping again in two years.

Think about the long game. Modular components can be removed, cleaned, and replaced. That means a firmness change after a back injury, a weight change, or a new sleep position does not require a new mattress — just a component swap. Ask the retailer what a replacement component costs and how the swap works before you buy, because that is where the long-term value lives.

Check the airflow story against your own heat. The Naked collection is designed for maximum airflow through every layer, with natural latex (which sleeps cooler than dense memory foam) over breathable construction. If you sleep hot, that is a genuine advantage over all-foam builds. If you sleep cold, it is a reason to pair the mattress with warmer bedding rather than a reason to avoid it.

Our Verdict: Who the Naked Collection Suits

Here is the most defensible read on the Naked launch — what it gets right, where the honest gaps are, and who should actually consider it.

Buy it if: you share a bed with someone whose firmness needs differ from yours, you like natural materials, and you plan to keep a mattress for a decade rather than replace it every few years. The combination of per-side firmness and replaceable components is genuinely different from a conventional mattress, and it solves the two most common reasons people replace mattresses early: wrong firmness and worn comfort layers.

Think twice if: you want a simple online purchase with a single published price and a bed-in-a-box delivery. BEDGEAR's modular line is a retail-fitted product with retailer-dependent pricing, and the Naked collection's price is not published in the launch materials. If you want transparent online pricing and a straightforward trial, the direct-to-consumer brands in our best-mattress guide are an easier buy.

The sustainability question. BEDGEAR's claim — that personalization and sustainability go hand in hand because a mattress that fits lasts longer — is one of the more honest green claims in the industry, because it is testable: if components really are replaceable at reasonable cost, the mattress really does last longer. The test buyers should apply is practical: ask the retailer the price of a replacement comfort component before you buy. If that number is reasonable, the sustainability story holds; if it is not, the modular design is mostly marketing.

How the Naked Collection Compares Within BEDGEAR's Line

BEDGEAR now sells three distinct mattress families, and the Naked collection only makes sense in context. Here is how they stack up.

Modular Hybrid (M-series): the flagship line. The M3 Performance mattress (queen around $2,499.99 to $2,999.99 at retail) and the M3 Night Ice (queen $3,499.99) offer the full modular system with Ver-Tex instant-cooling covers, Air-X mesh, and integrated air vents. The M5 and M5 Night Ice extend the line upward. The Naked collection is this line's natural-materials sibling: same modular philosophy, latex comfort layers, minimal aesthetic, two models instead of four.

Hybrid Performance (H-series): the simpler, cheaper line. The H2 (queen $1,499.99) is the firm option with a streamlined comfort profile; the H4 and H6 (queen $1,999.99 each) add the React Pillow-Top Pro cover for medium and plush feels. These are fixed-comfort hybrids, not modular ones — you pick a model, not a per-side firmness. They are the right choice if you sleep alone or agree with your partner, and the price gap to the modular line is the cost of personalization.

Modular Hybrid Naked: the middle path with a twist. You get the modular line's per-side personalization and replaceable components, but with all-natural latex comfort layers and a sustainability-first story. The trade-off is transparency: no published price, retail fitting required, and a two-model range that may not cover every firmness preference the four-model M-series does.

Frequently asked questions

What is the BEDGEAR Modular Hybrid Naked Collection?

It is a two-model mattress line BEDGEAR introduced at the 2026 Las Vegas Winter Market under its Sleep Just Got Personal showroom theme. The collection combines the brand's modular hybrid construction with all-natural latex comfort layers and a cleaner, more minimal aesthetic. Like BEDGEAR's other modular mattresses, each model uses Independent Suspension units that let each sleeper personalize the firmness on their side of the bed, and the whole build is designed for airflow through every layer.

How does split-firmness actually work?

Instead of one fixed comfort layer, BEDGEAR's modular design uses Independent Suspension units — swappable support components on each side of the bed. You (or the retailer fitting you) choose the firmness for the left side and the right side independently, so a back sleeper who wants firm and a side sleeper who wants plush can share one mattress without compromise. In the broader M-series line, BEDGEAR labels firmness from 0.0 (firm) to 3.0 (soft); the Naked collection uses the same per-side personalization principle with its natural latex comfort layers.

How much does the Naked Collection cost?

BEDGEAR did not publish Naked collection pricing in its launch release, and the collection is sold through BEDGEAR's site and retail partners rather than as a single online price. The honest price context comes from its modular siblings: the M3 Performance mattress lists around $2,499.99 to $2,999.99 for a queen depending on retailer, the M3 Night Ice lists at $3,499.99 queen, and the H-series hybrids run $1,499.99 (H2) to $1,999.99 (H4/H6) queen. Expect the Naked collection, with its natural latex build, to sit in the upper part of that range — and confirm the live price with a retailer before budgeting.

What makes the collection 'Naked'?

Two things: materials and aesthetic. The comfort layers use all-natural latex rather than synthetic foams, and the design is deliberately minimal — fewer layers, cleaner styling, and a sustainability story built on longevity. BEDGEAR also organizes its Performance Sheets program into two collections, Naked and Athleisure, using the same naming logic: natural fabrics and breathable builds on the Naked side. The name is a brand line, not a product category — but the natural-latex construction behind it is real.

Can you replace parts instead of the whole mattress?

Yes, and that is the modular line's most underrated feature. BEDGEAR's modular components can be removed, cleaned, and replaced over time, so a worn comfort layer or a firmness change does not mean discarding the whole mattress. That extends product life, cuts waste, and — for couples whose needs change — lets you re-fit one side without touching the other. It is also the practical side of BEDGEAR's sustainability pitch: getting the fit right the first time, and keeping it right, means fewer mattresses in landfill.
