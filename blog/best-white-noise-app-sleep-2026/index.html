The Best White Noise App for Sleep in 2026: myNoise vs Dark Noise vs BetterSleep

Comparing the three best white noise apps for sleep in 2026: myNoise (free, donation-run), Dark Noise (polished Apple app) and BetterSleep (sounds + sleep tracker). Real prices, real specs, honest winner.

The best white noise app for sleep in 2026 depends on what you value: myNoise is the free, donation-run library with 200+ endlessly tunable sound generators, Dark Noise is the most polished noise app on iPhone and iPad with a rare lifetime purchase option, and BetterSleep is the all-in-one sleep program with sounds, stories, meditations and a tracker. Below: real 2026 prices, real specs and an honest recommendation for each type of sleeper.

The short answer: for pure white noise with total control, myNoise’s free website is unbeatable. For the slickest iPhone experience, Dark Noise wins — and its $49.99 lifetime purchase beats renting. If you want sounds plus bedtime stories, meditations and a sleep tracker in one place, BetterSleep is the most complete program, at $59.99/year.

Top Picks at a Glance

These three apps could not be more different in philosophy, which is exactly why the comparison works. One is a one-man passion project kept free by donations, one is an indie Apple-platform specialist with the polish of a flagship app, and one is a full sleep program that happens to include white noise. Here is the two-minute version before we go deep.

  • myNoise — best free white noise, period. The website (mynoise.net) is free and ad-free with 200+ sound generators you can shape with 10-band sliders. The mobile apps are a one-time $10 lifetime purchase. Nothing here is rented.
  • Dark Noise — best for iPhone and iPad owners who want polish. Free with 8 sounds; the full 50+ sound library, custom mixes and Siri Shortcuts unlock at $2.99/month, $19.99/year, or a $49.99 one-time purchase. Beautiful, privacy-respecting, and deeply integrated into iOS.
  • BetterSleep — best all-in-one sleep app. The artist formerly known as Relax Melodies: 300+ sounds plus bedtime stories, guided meditations and a sleep tracker. Free tier is genuinely limited; Premium is listed at $59.99/year on the App Store.

If you stopped reading here: try myNoise’s website tonight for free while you decide whether an app is worth paying for at all.

How to Choose a White Noise App (What Actually Matters)

Most white noise apps look the same on the surface — a grid of icons, a play button, a timer. The differences that matter at 2 a.m. are less obvious, so weigh these before you pay.

Price model over price tag. A $5 app you keep forever beats a $3/month subscription that quietly costs you $36 a year. Check whether the full version is a one-time purchase, a subscription, or (best of all) free. Subscriptions make sense only if the app keeps adding content you will actually use.

Sound quality and variety. There is a real difference between a cleanly recorded rain loop and a hissy MP3 stretched into “white noise.” Look for calibrated noise colors (white, pink, brown, grey), natural recordings rather than synthesized loops, and seamless looping — a click at the loop point will keep light sleepers awake.

Offline mode. If you travel, fly, or sleep with your phone in airplane mode, streaming-only apps are dead weight. Offline downloads matter more than most people realize until the hotel Wi-Fi dies.

Sleep timer and fade-out. A timer that cuts the sound off abruptly is worse than none; a gradual fade-out over 10–30 minutes lets you drift off without a jolt. Customizable timer lengths and fade curves are the gold standard.

Mixing and customization. Some sleepers want rain; others want rain plus distant thunder minus the high hiss. Apps with per-frequency sliders or multi-sound mixes let you build exactly the sound that works for you instead of accepting a preset.

Platform fit. An iPhone-only app is a non-starter on Android, and a web-only tool needs a browser tab open all night. Match the app to every device you might play sound from — phone, tablet, speaker, or browser.

Ads and data. Free tiers often pay their bills with ads or upsell screens. At bedtime, a full-screen ad is not a minor annoyance — it is a sleep interruption. Check the privacy labels too: some sleep apps track purchases, usage data and identifiers across apps.

Our Winner: Dark Noise (With Two Honorable Mentions)

If you own an iPhone or iPad and want the best dedicated white noise app, pick Dark Noise. It is the rare app that treats a simple utility with craft: 50+ genuinely good sounds, seamless infinite looping, custom mixes, a sleep timer that fades instead of cutting, Siri Shortcuts (“play brown noise for 45 minutes” from bed), widgets, and a universal purchase across iPhone, iPad and Mac. The $49.99 lifetime option is the honest middle ground between a subscription treadmill and a gutted free tier — and the fact that pre-3.0 buyers were grandfathered in forever says something good about the developer, indie creator Charlie Chapman. Its RevenueCat-verified stats (about $4,700/month from 2,600 subscribers as of mid-2026) show an indie app thriving on merit, not dark patterns.

Honorable mention 1: myNoise, the free champion. If money is tight — or if you simply love precision — nothing touches the website. Ten color-coded sliders per generator let you sculpt sound to your hearing and your room in a way no $5 app can. The $10 lifetime app unlock is the best value in the category. The interface is utilitarian and proudly nerdy; if you want beautiful, this is not it.

Honorable mention 2: BetterSleep, the program. If white noise alone has never quite done it for you, BetterSleep’s bedtime stories, guided meditations and sleep tracker give you more levers to pull. The tradeoff is real: the free tier is thin (roughly ten sounds, per long-term users), the app nudges toward Premium, and its privacy label is the most data-hungry of the three. At $59.99/year it is a commitment — try the 7-day trial seriously before paying.

One more option worth knowing: Apple’s built-in Background Sounds (Settings > Accessibility > Audio/Visual on iPhone and Mac) plays white noise, rain and ocean for free with no app at all. It is basic, but for pure masking it removes every excuse.

In-Depth Reviews

myNoise — 9/10 for value, 7/10 for looks. The first thing to understand is that myNoise is not really competing on app-store polish; it is a sound engineer’s life’s work. Every generator is a multi-layered recording you can dissect with 10 sliders covering the full audible range, with calibrated presets for white, pink, brown and grey noise plus the unusual Speech Blocker. Animate mode slowly evolves the soundscape so it never feels like a loop, and donors can order custom-length audio files — up to 12 hours — generated from their exact slider settings. The website asks for nothing: no account, no ads, no paywall on any generator. The mobile apps ($10, one-time, per platform) add offline downloads, which is what you want for flights and hotels. Weaknesses: the interface looks like a lab instrument, discovery across 200+ generators takes effort, and iOS/Android unlocks are separate purchases. Still, for the price of zero dollars, it is the benchmark everything else is judged against.

Dark Noise — 9/10 overall, the Apple pick. Developer Charlie Chapman built the app Apple forgot to ship: fast, animated, and woven into the OS. Sounds are high quality, including stereo remasters of rain, thunderstorm, beach and airplane interior. Custom mixes let you stack sounds (rain + fireplace is the canonical combo), favorites and iCloud sync keep everything consistent across devices, and Shortcuts integration means your bedtime routine can start the noise, set the timer and dim the lights in one tap. The free tier’s 8 sounds are a fair trial, not a tease. The subscription pricing ($2.99/month or $19.99/year) is reasonable by app-store standards, and the $49.99 lifetime purchase is the move if you know you will use it for years. The real limitation is platform: this is an Apple-ecosystem app, full stop. Android users and desktop-browser users should look at myNoise instead.

BetterSleep — 8/10 as a program, 6/10 as a pure noise app. This is the heavyweight: 300+ sounds and music tracks spanning nature, water, ASMR, binaural beats and isochronic tones, plus 250+ guided meditations, hypnosis sessions and 100+ narrated sleep stories. The sleep tracker and recorder (it can flag snoring and sleep talking) turn it from a noise machine into a nightly program, and 91% of surveyed users in the app’s own materials report sleeping better after a week — take that self-reported figure with a grain of salt, but the 4.3-star average across 372,000+ Play Store reviews is real. The catch is the funnel: the free tier has been pared back to roughly ten sounds, most mixes show locks, and the path to the good stuff runs through $59.99/year. It is also the heaviest of the three (over 300 MB) and the most data-hungry. If you want one app that covers sound, story and tracking, it is genuinely good — just know you are buying a program, not a noise machine.

Frequently asked questions

Which white noise app is completely free?

myNoise’s website (mynoise.net) is free and ad-free, kept alive entirely by donations — you get all 200+ sound generators at no cost. Its iOS and Android apps cost a one-time $10 for lifetime access to every sound. Dark Noise offers 8 sounds free and BetterSleep’s free tier is limited to a handful of sounds, with the full libraries behind a purchase or subscription.

Does Dark Noise have a one-time purchase or is it subscription-only?

Both. Since version 3.0, new users get a free tier with 8 sounds, and Dark Noise Pro costs $2.99/month, $19.99/year, or a $49.99 one-time lifetime purchase. If you bought Dark Noise before the 3.0 update (when it was a $9.99 one-time purchase), you keep every current Pro feature free — existing users were grandfathered in.

What happened to the Relax Melodies app?

Relax Melodies was renamed BetterSleep by the same developer (BetterSleep Health, LLC, formerly Ipnos Software). It is the same app with a new layout, plus a built-in sleep tracker and sleep recorder. The library now spans 300+ sleep sounds and music tracks, 250+ guided meditations and hypnosis sessions, and 100+ bedtime stories.

Is myNoise really run by one person?

Yes. myNoise is the project of Stéphane Pigeon, a sound engineer with a Ph.D. in applied sciences, who records, designs and maintains the site himself. The web version runs entirely on donations — any donation, even a few dollars, grants lifetime privileged access on the website.

Can a white noise app mask a snoring partner or street noise?

White noise helps many people fall asleep by smoothing over sudden environmental sounds — it masks the interruptions rather than silencing the world. For comfort, keep the volume modest: about as loud as a quiet conversation is a good rule of thumb, and avoid earbuds at high volume all night. If noise consistently ruins your sleep, a phone app is a comfort tool, not a treatment — talk to a professional about persistent problems.

The Takeaway

  • Best free: myNoise’s website — 200+ tunable generators, no ads, no account, forever free. The $10 lifetime mobile unlock is the best money in the category.
  • Best for iPhone/iPad: Dark Noise — polished, private, deeply integrated into iOS, with a $49.99 lifetime option that beats the subscription.
  • Best all-in-one: BetterSleep — sounds, stories, meditations and a tracker, but the free tier is thin and Premium is $59.99/year.
  • Check the price model, not just the price: a one-time purchase you keep beats a cheap subscription you forget to cancel.
  • Whatever you choose, keep the volume modest — about the level of a quiet conversation — and give any new sound a few nights before judging it.
