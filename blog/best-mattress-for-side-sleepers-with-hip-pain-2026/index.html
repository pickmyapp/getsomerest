Best Mattress for Side Sleepers With Hip Pain (2026)

Why Side Sleeping Hurts Your Hips

The hip pain problem is a geometry problem. Lying on your side concentrates your weight on the greater trochanter — the bony knob on the outside of the hip — which presses into the mattress through a thin layer of muscle and the trochanteric bursa, the fluid-filled sac that cushions the joint. Too firm, and that point becomes a pressure hotspot for eight hours. Too soft, and the hip drops until the spine curves sideways. The right mattress does both jobs at once: it lets the hip sink just enough, and it holds the waist up.

1. Zoned support matters most

Zoned construction is the single most useful feature for hip pain: the comfort layer is softer under the shoulders and hips and firmer under the lower back and legs. The Amerisleep AS5 builds this in with five HIVE zones, and the Helix Midnight Luxe zones its coils the same way. A uniformly soft mattress cannot do this — it gives everywhere, including where you need support.

2. Firmness by body weight

Weight decides how far you sink, so the right firmness moves with it. Under 130 lb, medium-soft (about 4 out of 10) gives enough hip sinkage without losing support. From 130 to 230 lb, medium (5 to 6) is the most reliable range for cushioning the hip while keeping the spine level. Over 230 lb, medium-firm (6 to 7) keeps the hip from bottoming out into the support core, which creates a different alignment problem.

3. The comfort layer needs real depth

For side sleepers with hip pain, 1.5 to 2 inches of memory foam at medium firmness is the safe range for the top layer, over a denser transition layer of 1.5 to 2 inches. Thin comfort layers bottom out under the hip; very thick soft layers feel good for ten minutes and saggy by morning. All-foam beds like the Nectar Premier and the AS5 get this right by pairing a thick comfort layer with a dense base.

4. Give the trial time

A mattress decision made in a showroom is a guess. Every pick here ships with a long at-home trial — 365 nights on four of the five — and the honest test is a month of real sleep, not one afternoon lying down. If the hip still aches after 30 nights on a new bed, the firmness is wrong, and the trial is there to let you swap it.

The Mattresses in Detail

Amerisleep AS5 — $2,349 queen

The AS5 is the softest bed in this lineup and the most deliberate about hip pressure. It is a 14-inch all-foam mattress built on Bio-Pur foam, with HIVE zoning cut into five zones: softer under the shoulders and hips, firmer under the head, lower back and legs. For a side sleeper whose hip aches by morning, that zoning is the point — the hip sinks into a softer pocket while the waist stays supported, so the spine stays closer to level. The trade-off is the softness itself: at 3 out of 10 it is softer than the medium range most guides suggest, so it fits lighter sleepers and anyone who wants the hip to sink further. Heavier sleepers may prefer the AS5 Hybrid, which adds a coil base for $2,649.

Saatva Classic in Plush Soft — $2,229 queen

The Saatva Classic is the traditionalist's pick: a hand-built innerspring hybrid with a plush Euro pillow top over individually wrapped coils, available in Plush Soft, Luxury Firm and Firm. For hip pain, Plush Soft is the one — the soft top cushions the greater trochanter while the coils hold the rest of the body up, and the innerspring build sleeps cooler and feels bouncier than any foam bed here. Independent testing gives the Plush Soft the highest side-sleeper score we have on file, and the 365-night trial plus lifetime warranty make it the pick if you expect to keep the bed for a decade. The catch is the price — and it is heavy to move, though white-glove delivery is included.

Helix Midnight Luxe — $1,899 queen

The Midnight Luxe is the all-rounder. It is a zoned hybrid with a medium feel, a quilted pillow top and individually wrapped coils that are firmer in the middle and softer at the shoulders and hips — the same zoning idea as the AS5, executed with coils instead of cut foam. It sleeps cooler than the all-foam picks, isolates motion well for couples, and suits the widest weight range of the five. At $1,899 for a queen it sits between the budget and luxury picks, and the 365-night trial and lifetime warranty match the best here. If you are choosing one bed for two people with different needs, this is the one to start with.

Nectar Premier — $999 queen

The Nectar Premier is the value shock of this list: a $999 queen with a pressure-relief score of 9.0 from Sleep Foundation testing, the highest we found for any bed here, and a specific call-out for hip pain from Sleepopolis. It is an all-foam memory foam bed at medium firmness, with a cooling cover that handles the usual memory-foam heat complaint better than the entry-level Nectar Classic at $699. The trade-off is the feel: it is a classic slow-sinking memory foam, so combination sleepers who change position a lot may find it less responsive than a hybrid, and it sleeps warmer than the Helix. For a side sleeper on a budget whose main problem is hip pressure, it is the best money-to-relief ratio here.

DreamCloud Premier Hybrid — $1,099 queen

The DreamCloud Premier Hybrid is the quiet overachiever: the highest pressure-relief sub-score in one independent database we checked, strong motion isolation and edge support, and a queen price of $1,099 that undercuts every pick here except the Nectar. Frequently asked questions

What firmness is best for side sleepers with hip pain?

Medium to medium-soft — about 4 to 6 on a 10-point firmness scale — suits most side sleepers with hip pain. Lighter sleepers under 130 lb usually do best at the softer end (around 4), average-weight sleepers at medium (5 to 6), and sleepers over 230 lb at medium-firm (6 to 7) so the hip does not bottom out into the support core.

Is a soft or a medium mattress better for hip pain?

A medium mattress is the safer default for most people, because it cushions the hip without letting the pelvis sink too far. A soft mattress suits lighter sleepers and anyone who wants deeper hip sinkage, but heavier sleepers on a very soft bed often trade hip pressure for a curved spine — a different problem. Zoned construction, softer at the hip and firmer at the waist, beats a uniformly soft surface either way.

How long does it take for a new mattress to help hip pain?

Give any new mattress at least 30 nights. Your body adjusts to a new surface over the first few weeks, and the foams themselves soften slightly as they break in. All five mattresses here come with long trials — 365 nights on the Amerisleep, Saatva, Helix and Nectar picks — so a month of real sleep is the honest test, not one night in a showroom.

Should I get a mattress topper instead of a new mattress?

A topper can help if your current mattress is supportive but too firm at the surface — a 2 to 3 inch memory foam topper adds the hip cushioning you are missing for a fraction of a new bed. If the mattress itself sags, or the support core has gone soft, a topper sits on a bad foundation and will not fix it. Our best mattress topper for back pain under $100 covers the budget options.

When should hip pain send you to a doctor?

This guide is about comfort and sleep setup, not diagnosis. If hip pain is sharp, wakes you every night, comes with numbness or tingling down the leg, does not change when you switch sleep positions, or has lasted more than a few weeks, talk to a clinician — a mattress can ease pressure, but it cannot treat an injury or a joint condition.
