Best Sleep Tracker for Couples Who Share a Bed (2026)

Five sleep trackers that work when two people share one bed — Eight Sleep, Beautyrest, Oura, Withings and Galaxy Ring, prices checked Nov 26, 2026.

Share

By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Nov 26, 2026 · Last updated Nov 26, 2026

Most sleep trackers assume one person in the bed. Share it with a partner and the numbers get messy: an under-mattress sensor picks up two heartbeats, a bedside radar cannot tell you apart, and a single wearable only tracks the person wearing it. The fix depends on what you want — per-person data with nothing to wear, the cheapest way to cover two sleepers, or an end to the nightly temperature argument. We checked prices on November 26, 2026, and picked five trackers that actually work for couples, from about $149 to $2,899.

Quick answer

Buy the Eight Sleep Pod 6 if you want both partners tracked independently with their own temperatures — Queen $2,899 plus $199 a year for Autopilot. On a budget, the Beautyrest Sleeptracker Monitor covers two sleepers for $149–$199 with no subscription. If you prefer wearables, buy two rings: the Oura Ring 4 ($349) or the subscription-free Samsung Galaxy Ring ($299.99, Android only).

The Short Answer: 5 Sleep Trackers for Couples

Three designs work for couples, and they solve different problems. A dual-zone bed system tracks both partners and controls each side's temperature. Two wearables give perfectly separated data. Two under-mattress mats give the same result with nothing to wear. The five picks below cover all three designs.

Our five picks: the Eight Sleep Pod 6 (Queen $2,899) is the best overall — the only tracker that also gives each side its own temperature; the Beautyrest Sleeptracker Monitor ($149–$199) is the best budget pick, covering two sleepers in one box with no subscription; the Oura Ring 4 ($349) is the best wearable, with the cleanest per-person data; the Withings Sleep Analyzer ($199.95) is the best no-wearable, no-subscription pick, one mat per sleeper; and the Samsung Galaxy Ring (from $299.99) is the best no-subscription wearable for Android couples.

If you only remember one thing: always price the pair, not the single. Two rings, two mats or one dual-zone cover are three very different purchases, and the cheapest sticker price is rarely the cheapest couples price once subscriptions are counted.

How Couples Tracking Actually Works

There are three ways to track two sleepers, and they are not equal. A wearable tracks the person wearing it — a ring or wristband only ever measures your finger or wrist, so two wearables give two perfectly separated reports with zero cross-talk. An under-mattress sensor tracks the sleeper directly above it, so couples need two mats, one per side, on separate accounts. And a bed system like the Eight Sleep Pod 6 builds two sleeper profiles into one cover, tracking both partners from the same device. Bedside radar is the odd one out: it watches the whole bed and struggles to split two people.

Per-person data: rings win, mats are close

If clean data is your priority, buy two wearables. A ring's optical sensor sits on your finger and cannot feel your partner at all, which is why the Oura Ring 4 and Samsung Galaxy Ring are the most reliable couples options for data purity. Under-mattress sensors are nearly as good when you sleep on your own side — Withings says the Sleep Analyzer focuses exclusively on the person directly above it — but a partner who sleeps close to the centre can still confuse the readings, and our own review found exactly that. Beautyrest solves it with two separate sensor strips, one per sleeper, in a single box.

The temperature argument is a tracking problem too

The most common couples complaint is not data — it is temperature. One partner runs hot, the other runs cold, and the thermostat cannot please both. Only two products in this guide fix it: the Eight Sleep Pod 6, which circulates water from 55 to 110 degrees Fahrenheit independently per side, and the ChiliSleep Dock Pro, which cools or warms each side separately but does no sleep tracking at all. If temperature is your nightly argument, a tracker alone will not solve it; you need a dual-zone system.

Count the cost for two, not one

Couples pricing is where these products diverge most. The Pod 6 looks expensive at $2,899 for a Queen, but one Autopilot membership ($199 a year) covers both sleepers. The Beautyrest monitor is the cheapest true dual-sleeper system at $149–$199 for both partners, with no subscription. Rings and mats are priced per person: two Oura Ring 4s cost $698 plus two memberships ($69.99 a year each), while two Galaxy Rings cost about $600 with no subscription ever. Two Withings mats cost $299.90–$399.90. Always double the sticker price before comparing.

What to skip for a shared bed

Three products look plausible and are not. The Google Nest Hub tracks one person only — Google's own FAQ says a second person in the same spot will affect your data. The SleepScore Max is effectively discontinued: its iOS app was removed from the App Store in October 2024 and no current official shop exists. And the Ultrahuman Ring Air, attractive on paper at $349 with no subscription, is under a US import ban since October 2025 over an Oura patent case; the US store now sells the Ring Pro ($479) instead. For a US couple, the Air is not a safe buy.

Our Pick: Eight Sleep Pod 6 — The Only True Dual System

If you share a bed, buy the Eight Sleep Pod 6. It is the only product here that tracks both partners independently and fixes the number-one couples complaint at the same time: temperature. Each side gets its own water-circulated temperature profile from 55 to 110 degrees Fahrenheit, its own sleep data — heart rate, heart-rate variability, respiratory rate, snoring, sleep stages — and its own sleeper account, all from one cover with no wearable to charge. Nine times more sensors than the Pod 5, with temperature changes 20 percent faster, per the launch announcement.

The catch is the total cost, and it is real: a Queen is $2,899, and the Autopilot membership is mandatory — $199 a year for the Standard tier, which covers both sleepers. The 5 Sleep Trackers, One by One

1. Eight Sleep Pod 6 — Queen $2,899 + $199 a year

The best sleep tracker for couples, and the only one that also ends the temperature argument. The Pod 6 is a water-circulating mattress cover with a Hub, tracking both partners independently with separate temperature profiles from 55 to 110 degrees per side. Sensors were designed to tell sleepers apart and ignore pets and children. The cost is the highest here by far — Queen $2,899 plus a mandatory $199-a-year Autopilot membership — and the warranty length depends on the tier you pay for.

2. Beautyrest Sleeptracker Monitor — $149–$199, no subscription

The cheapest true dual-sleeper system, and the only sub-$200 product built for two sleepers in one box. Two thin under-mattress sensor strips, one per sleeper, plug into a wall outlet — nothing to wear, nothing to charge — and each partner gets their own report and coaching in the app. Core tracking costs nothing per year. The caveats: it launched in 2017, so the app feels dated; availability is inconsistent; and the sensors can bleed together if partners sleep close to the centre of the bed.

3. Oura Ring 4 — $349 list, $249 on Amazon

The best wearable for couples, because a ring can never confuse two sleepers. Each partner wears their own Oura Ring 4 and gets perfectly separated data: sleep stages, heart-rate variability, resting heart rate and temperature trends, with overnight HRV within about 6 percent of an ECG strap in independent checks. Battery lasts 5–8 days and a free sizing kit is included. The cost adds up, though: two rings at $349 list plus two memberships at $69.99 a year each is about $838 in year one. Note that the Ring 5 is now the flagship at $399 and up; the Ring 4 is the discounted previous generation running the same software.

4. Withings Sleep Analyzer — $199.95 list, $149.95 on sale

The best no-wearable, no-subscription pick — with one catch for couples: one mat tracks one person, so a couple needs two. The mat slides under the mattress, plugs in and never needs charging, tracking sleep stages, heart rate, respiratory rate, snoring and breathing disturbances with a sensor clinically validated against polysomnography. Withings says it focuses exclusively on the person directly above it, but our own review found a partner's movements can still confuse readings. Two mats cost $299.90–$399.90, still far cheaper than a Pod and with no subscription for core features.

5. Samsung Galaxy Ring — from $299.99, no subscription

The best no-subscription wearable for Android couples. Two Galaxy Rings cost about $600 with zero dollars a year in ongoing fees — the cheapest long-term cost of any premium ring for two people — and each partner's data stays perfectly separate. Sleep stages, sleep score, snore detection and Energy Score are all included free, and the battery lasts up to 7 days (14 with the charging case). The hard limit is platform: it needs Android 11 or later and a Samsung Galaxy phone for setup, and iPhone owners are completely excluded. The Galaxy Ring 2 is reportedly delayed to early 2027.

Frequently asked questions

Can one sleep tracker track two people in the same bed?

It depends on the sensor. A wearable tracks the person wearing it, so two rings or two wristbands always give two clean reports. An under-mattress sensor tracks the sleeper directly above it — one mat, one person — so couples need two mats, one per side. The Eight Sleep Pod 6 is the exception: a dual-zone cover with two sleeper profiles on one Pod. A bedside radar like the Google Nest Hub tracks one person only.

Do both partners need a subscription?

Not always, and the maths differ by product. The Eight Sleep Pod 6 needs one Autopilot membership ($199 a year for the Standard tier) and it covers both sleepers' accounts. Oura charges per ring — $5.99 a month or $69.99 a year each — so two partners pay twice. The Samsung Galaxy Ring, Withings Sleep Analyzer and Beautyrest Sleeptracker Monitor have no subscription for their core tracking.

Is a smart ring or an under-mattress sensor better for couples?

Rings give the cleanest per-person data: the sensor is on your finger, so your partner's movement can never contaminate the reading. Under-mattress sensors are the most comfortable option — nothing to wear, nothing to charge — but they can bleed together if you sleep close to the centre of the bed, and couples need two mats. The Pod 6 gives both: per-person data with nothing to wear, plus separate temperature control per side.

Will my partner's movement ruin my sleep score?

It can, on under-mattress sensors. A mat measures tiny movements through the mattress, and a partner tossing and turning nearby adds noise the algorithm has to filter out. Withings says its Sleep Analyzer focuses exclusively on the person directly above it, but our own review found a partner's movements can still confuse readings, especially if you sleep close together. Wearables do not have this problem at all.

What about the Google Nest Hub for couples?

Do not buy it for couples. The Nest Hub's Soli radar tracks one person only, and Google's own FAQ says another person or pet in the same spot will affect your data. The official workaround is two Hubs, one per side — $200 for two single-person reports. A pair of rings or two Withings mats gives cleaner data for similar money, and the Beautyrest monitor covers both sleepers in one box for less.

Key Takeaways

  • Best overall for couples: Eight Sleep Pod 6 — tracks both partners independently and gives each side its own temperature, Queen $2,899 plus $199 a year.
  • Best budget: Beautyrest Sleeptracker Monitor — two sleepers in one box for $149–$199, no subscription.
  • Best wearable: Oura Ring 4 — perfectly separated data, $349 per ring plus $69.99 a year each.
  • Best no-wearable, no-subscription: Withings Sleep Analyzer — two mats for a couple, $299.90–$399.90, no subscription for core features.
  • Best for Android couples: Samsung Galaxy Ring — two rings for about $600 with no subscription ever, but Android only.
  • Count the cost for two: always double the sticker price and add the subscription before comparing.
  • Skip for a shared bed: Google Nest Hub (one person only), SleepScore Max (discontinued) and Ultrahuman Ring Air (US import ban).
