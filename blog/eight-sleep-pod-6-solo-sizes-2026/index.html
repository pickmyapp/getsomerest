Eight Sleep Pod 6 Solo Sizes Explained: Who the $1,999 Single-Side Cover Suits

Eight Sleep's Pod 6 Solo covers one side of the bed for $1,999 in every size. Who it suits, who should buy Standard instead, and the $199-$399/year Autopilot catch.

Share
9 min read
By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Oct 26, 2026 · Last updated Oct 26, 2026

For five generations, Eight Sleep's Pod was a couples' product: one cover, two zones, one very large price tag. The Pod 6, launched September 23, 2026, breaks that pattern with Solo sizes — a single-side cover for one sleeper at $1,999 in every size, from Twin to California King. It is the cheapest way ever to get Pod technology, and the first Pod that makes sense in a studio apartment or a teenager's room. But Solo is not simply a cheaper Standard: it drops compatibility with the Base and Blanket add-ons, and the mandatory Autopilot subscription still adds $199 a year. Here is who Solo genuinely suits, who should spend more on Standard, and how the real first-year cost stacks up.

Quick answer

The short answer: Pod 6 Solo is Eight Sleep's $1,999 single-side cover for one sleeper — Twin, Twin XL, or one side of a Queen, King, or California King. It suits solo sleepers and couples where only one partner wants smart-bed tech, but it cannot use the Pod Base or Blanket add-ons, and an Autopilot membership ($199-$399/year) is required for the first 12 months, making the lowest first-year cost $2,198.

At a Glance: Solo in One Minute

Eight Sleep launched the Pod 6 on September 23, 2026, in over 37 countries, and the headline was not the hardware — it was the sizing. For the first time, the Pod comes in Solo sizes built for one sleeper, and the starting price drops to $1,999.

  • What it is: a single-side smart mattress cover that heats, cools, and tracks sleep for one person, on a Twin, Twin XL, or one side of a Queen, King, or California King.
  • What it costs: $1,999 in every size, plus a mandatory Autopilot membership for the first 12 months ($199 Standard, $299 Enhanced, $399 Elite per year). Lowest first-year total: $2,198.
  • What you get: the full Pod 6 stack — 550W of heating and cooling per side, 5-minute priming, 18 biometric sensors, and app-based sleep data with no wearable required.
  • What you lose vs Standard: whole-bed coverage, a second zone, and compatibility with the Pod Base and Pod Blanket add-ons.
  • Who it is for: solo sleepers, and couples where only one partner wants smart-bed tech — Eight Sleep itself names teenagers as a target audience.

The Solo launch matters beyond Eight Sleep. It is the first time a premium smart-bed brand has admitted that half its market sleeps alone — and priced accordingly. The subscription catch is real, but the $1,999 entry point changes who can even consider a Pod.

How to Choose Between Solo and Standard

The decision is simpler than Eight Sleep's pricing page makes it look. Work through three questions in order, and the right cover picks itself.

First: how many sleepers want the technology? If the answer is one — you sleep alone, or your partner actively does not want a smart cover — Solo is the natural fit. On a Queen, King, or California King, Solo covers your side and leaves your partner's side as an ordinary mattress. That is a feature, not a compromise, for couples with mismatched temperature needs: one partner gets active cooling, the other gets left alone.

Second: do you want the add-ons? This is the question that kills Solo for a lot of buyers. Solo is not compatible with the Pod Base (the adjustable, vibrating base) or the Pod Blanket (the temperature-controlled blanket). Standard works with every add-on. If you already own a Base or Blanket, or you plan to buy one, Solo is out — no exceptions, according to Eight Sleep's own compatibility notes.

Third: might a second sleeper join later? Eight Sleep says a Secondary Solo Cover can be zipped alongside a Solo if a partner moves in. That rescues Solo from being a dead end, but the pair still will not support the Base or Blanket. If there is a realistic chance the bed becomes a two-Pod bed with full add-ons, buy Standard now and avoid paying twice.

A note on bed sizes: Solo's flat $1,999 pricing is unusually generous at the top end. A California King Solo costs the same as a Twin Solo, while Standard jumps from $2,699 (Full) to $2,999 (King/California King). For a solo sleeper in a King bed, Solo saves a full $1,000 against Standard — the biggest saving in the lineup.

Our Verdict: Who Should Buy Solo

Solo is the best Pod 6 value — for a narrower audience than Eight Sleep's marketing suggests. Here is the honest split.

Buy Solo if: you sleep alone in any size bed; you share a bed but only one of you wants smart-bed technology; you want Pod cooling and tracking at the lowest possible entry price; or you are buying for a teenager's room or a guest setup where one side is all that matters. The $1,999 flat price, the smaller hub, and the 5-minute priming (down from 20 minutes on Pod 5) make this the most accessible Pod ever.

Buy Standard if: both sleepers want the technology; you want the Base or Blanket now or later; or you want the option to add a second zone without replacing the cover. The $700-$1,000 premium buys full-bed coverage and full add-on compatibility — and for a shared bed, that is money well spent.

Buy neither if: the subscription is a dealbreaker. Autopilot is mandatory for the first 12 months at $199-$399 a year, and the 5-year warranty on Enhanced and Elite plans lapses if you stop paying. If you want active bed cooling without a subscription, the ChiliSleep Dock Pro (from $1,299, no required subscription) remains the honest alternative — it cools, but it does not heat, track, or auto-adjust the way the Pod does.

What the Launch Coverage Got Right

Launch coverage of the Pod 6 largely agreed on the headline: Solo is the story, not the incremental hardware gains. The smaller hub, the 550W of heating and cooling per side (up from 400W on Pod 5), and the 18 sensors (up from one per side on Pod 5) all got mentions, but every outlet led with the same fact — $1,999 is the lowest starting price in Pod history, and it arrives because of a single-sleeper option, not a cheaper product.

Coverage also correctly flagged the subscription as the asterisk. Eight Sleep's own launch materials are upfront that Autopilot is required for the first 12 months, and reviewers did the math the company did not headline: $2,198 is the lowest realistic first-year cost, not $1,999. The three Autopilot tiers — Standard at $199/year with a 2-year warranty, Enhanced at $299/year and Elite at $399/year with 5-year warranties — were reported consistently, as was the detail that Elite adds Health Check cardiovascular and respiratory monitoring.

One point of genuine confusion in early coverage was add-on compatibility: some summaries said Solo works with all add-ons, following the launch release's general line that Pod 6 works with the Blanket, Pillow Cover, and Base. Eight Sleep's size-specific materials are clearer — Solo is not compatible with the Base or the Blanket — and that is the version we have used here. If you are buying Solo specifically to pair with a Base, verify compatibility with Eight Sleep before ordering, because this is the one spec where sources still disagree.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Eight Sleep Pod 6 Solo?

Solo is a single-sleeper version of Eight Sleep's Pod 6 smart mattress cover. It covers one side of a Queen, King, or California King bed, or fits a standalone Twin or Twin XL mattress. It delivers the same core Pod 6 technology as the Standard cover — active heating and cooling, sleep tracking through 18 biometric sensors, and Autopilot temperature adjustments — but only for one sleeper. Eight Sleep launched it on September 23, 2026, alongside the Standard cover, as the first Pod generation designed for solo sleepers.

How much does Pod 6 Solo cost, including the subscription?

Solo costs $1,999 in every size: Twin, Twin XL, Queen (one side), King (one side), and California King (one side). An Autopilot membership is required for the first 12 months and costs $199 a year for Standard, $299 for Enhanced, or $399 for Elite. So the lowest realistic first-year cost is $2,198 ($1,999 + $199), and the membership covers two user profiles per Pod even though Solo serves one sleeper. After year one, you can keep or drop the membership, but Eight Sleep says the 5-year warranty on Enhanced and Elite plans only stays active while the plan is paid.

What is the difference between Pod 6 Solo and Pod 6 Standard?

Coverage and add-ons. Standard covers the whole bed with a zone per side and costs $2,699 for Full, $2,899 for Queen, and $2,999 for King or California King. Solo covers one side for one sleeper at $1,999 in every size. Solo is not compatible with the Pod Base or the Pod Blanket add-ons, while Standard works with all add-ons. Eight Sleep says a Secondary Solo Cover can be zipped alongside a Solo later if a partner moves in, which softens the upgrade path.

Does Pod 6 Solo work without a wearable?

Yes. Like the rest of the Pod 6 line, Solo tracks sleep through sensors built into the cover itself — Eight Sleep says 18 biometric sensors map position and body metrics across the mattress, tracking heart rate, breathing, and sleep stages without a watch or ring. You manage settings and review sleep data in the Eight Sleep app on your phone. The Hub, which powers the heating and cooling, is 50% smaller than the Pod 5 hub and fits under most bed frames.

Should I buy Solo or wait for a sale on Standard?

Buy Solo if you sleep alone, or if only one side of a shared bed needs the technology — for example, a hot sleeper whose partner sleeps cool and wants nothing to do with a smart cover. The $700-$1,000 saving over Standard is real, but only if you will never want the Base or Blanket add-ons, which Solo cannot use. If there is any chance a partner will want their own zone later, Standard is the safer buy: adding a Secondary Solo Cover later is possible, but it will not give you Base or Blanket compatibility. Couples who both want the tech should skip Solo entirely.

Key Takeaways

  • Pod 6 Solo costs $1,999 in every size — Twin, Twin XL, or one side of a Queen, King, or California King — making it the cheapest Pod ever and the first designed for a single sleeper.
  • The technology matches Standard exactly: 550W heating and cooling, 18 biometric sensors, no wearable needed, and a hub 50% smaller than Pod 5's.
  • Solo is NOT compatible with the Pod Base or Pod Blanket add-ons; Standard works with all add-ons. This is the deciding factor for many buyers.
  • Autopilot is required for the first 12 months at $199-$399/year, so the lowest real first-year cost is $2,198, not $1,999.
  • Best for solo sleepers and mixed-temperature couples where only one partner wants the tech; couples who both want it, or who want add-ons, should buy Standard.
