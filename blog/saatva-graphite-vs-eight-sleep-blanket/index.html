Saatva Graphite vs Eight Sleep Pod Blanket: Cooling Toppers Compared

Two cooling toppers, two universes: the Saatva Graphite Memory Foam Topper ($305) pulls heat away passively with graphite-infused foam, while the Eight Sleep Pod Blanket actively holds your temperature at 55–110°F — as part of a $4,099+ system with a $199/year subscription.

By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Oct 13, 2026 · Last updated Oct 13, 2026

Hot sleepers have two very different answers on the market right now. One is passive: memory foam infused with graphite that conducts heat away from your body. The other is active: a hydro-powered blanket that holds your side of the bed at an exact temperature all night. We compare them on cooling power, feel, price and who each one actually suits.

Quick answer

They are barely in the same category. The Saatva Graphite Memory Foam Topper ($305 queen) is a 3-inch memory foam topper that cools passively — simple, no power, no subscription, with a 180-night trial. The Eight Sleep Pod Blanket is a hydro-powered blanket (55–110°F, dual-zone) that actively holds your temperature all night, but only as part of the Pod 5 Plus system starting around $4,099, plus a $199/year subscription required for full features. Pick Saatva for value and simplicity; Eight Sleep only if you are a committed hot sleeper with the budget. Prices checked October 2026.

How each one cools

Understanding the two cooling methods is the whole comparison. Everything else — price, feel, subscription — flows from this one difference.

Saatva: passive conduction. The 3-inch topper blends graphite directly into its memory foam. Graphite is one of the best heat conductors used in bedding, so it pulls body heat away from you and spreads it through the foam instead of letting it pool under your hips and shoulders. There is no power, no water, no app. The trade-off is built in: passive cooling can only narrow the gap between you and room temperature, never push you below it. Reviewers consistently note it stays reasonably cool but lacks the cold-to-the-touch feel hot sleepers dream about. If your bedroom runs warm, see our guide to the best cooling mattress toppers for night sweats for more mid-range options, and our ideal bedroom temperature research for why room climate still matters.

Eight Sleep: active regulation. The Pod Blanket circulates water through channels in the blanket and the matching mattress cover, holding each side of the bed anywhere from 55°F to 110°F — including heating, which matters more than you would think for deep sleep and gentle wake-ups. The app’s Autopilot feature adjusts your temperature through the night based on your sleep stages, and the system tracks sleep stages, heart rate, HRV and breathing with no wearable. It is a genuine thermal cocoon: every inch of your body held at your target temperature. Nothing passive comes close.

Your core body temperature needs to drop by about 1–2°F for sleep onset — which is why warm bedrooms and hot sleepers collide, and why active temperature control beats passive cooling for people who run hot.

The honest cost math

Do the five-year math before you fall in love with the technology. The Saatva costs $305 once, plus nothing ever again. The Pod 5 Plus starts around $4,099 and the Standard subscription is $17/month or $199/year — mandatory the first year — so year one runs roughly $4,298 and five years lands around $5,095. That is nearly seventeen Saatva toppers. The subscription alone pays for a new premium topper every eighteen months. For that math to work, you have to be a genuinely hot sleeper — night sweats, a partner with a different temperature, a bedroom that never cools down — not just someone who likes the idea of a gadget.

Feel matters too

The two products change the bed in opposite ways. The Saatva adds 3 inches of body-hugging memory foam — softer, more contouring, excellent for side sleepers on a firm mattress, and it slightly changes how your mattress feels. The Eight Sleep system barely changes feel at all: the hydro cover is thin, the blanket lies on top like normal bedding, and your mattress keeps its character. If you love your mattress but hate its temperature, that is a quiet point in Eight Sleep’s favor. Who should buy which

Buy the Saatva Graphite Topper if you want a meaningful cooling upgrade for a firm or warm mattress without buying into a tech ecosystem. At $305 with a 180-night trial and no subscription, it is the sane default for most hot sleepers — better than plain foam, genuinely comfortable for side sleepers, and cheap enough to be a no-regrets experiment.

Buy the Eight Sleep Pod Blanket system if you are a serious hot sleeper for whom temperature is the single biggest sleep problem — night sweats, menopause-related heat, or a partner who wants the opposite climate. The active cooling, dual zones and per-stage temperature adjustments are unmatched, and the sleep tracking is a real bonus. Just go in with eyes open: the first-year cost is roughly fourteen times the Saatva, the subscription is required for the features you are paying for, and the hub is another box in your bedroom.

One last honest note for anyone on the fence: no cooling product fixes a hot room. Both the Saatva and the Eight Sleep perform best when the bedroom itself is reasonably cool — the graphite topper because it can only pull heat toward room temperature, and the Pod system because its hub exhausts heat into the room while it cools you. If your bedroom bakes at night, pairing either product with a cooler room — see our ideal bedroom temperature research — will do more for your sleep than any topper upgrade alone.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Eight Sleep Pod Blanket work without the subscription?

Only partially. The blanket’s basic manual temperature control keeps working, but Eight Sleep says the subscription is required for full features — Autopilot temperature adjustments, sleep tracking, health reports and the warming wake-up. The Standard plan costs $17 per month or $199 per year and is mandatory for the first year.

Is the Saatva Graphite Topper really cooling, or is it just foam?

It is cooler than standard memory foam, but it is still passive. Graphite conducts heat away from your body better than plain foam, and reviewers note it stays reasonably cool — though it lacks the cold-to-the-touch feel that hot sleepers often want. It cannot match active water cooling for serious night sweats.

Will the Saatva Graphite Topper make my mattress firmer or softer?

Softer. Three inches of memory foam adds contouring plushness, which suits firm mattresses and side sleepers best. If your mattress already sleeps hot and soft, adding more foam can trap more heat — which is exactly the case where active cooling earns its price.

Can I use the Eight Sleep Pod Blanket on my own mattress?

Yes. The system is designed to fit over your existing mattress — the hydro-powered cover wraps it and the blanket lies on top like normal bedding. It fits Full through Cal King mattresses between 10 and 16 inches thick. You do need floor space near the bed for the water hub.

Which is better for night sweats specifically?

The Eight Sleep system, honestly. Active cooling that holds your side at a set temperature beats any passive foam for night sweats — that is the one thing the $4,099 price is really buying.
