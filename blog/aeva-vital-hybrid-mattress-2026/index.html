Aeva Vital Hybrid Mattress Explained: The Recovery-Stack Mattress Sold With Saunas and Cold Plunges (2026)

Aeva Vital's hybrid mattress — the BedStory SpineAlign in three builds from $699 — anchors a recovery stack of saunas, tubs and cold plunges. What is verified, what is brand PR, and the trial gap that matters.

By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Nov 9, 2026 · Last updated Nov 9, 2026

Aeva Vital is a home-wellness storefront with a clear pitch: one place to buy the whole recovery routine — a hybrid mattress, infrared saunas, freestanding soaking tubs and a portable cold plunge, all under one brand. The mattress at the centre of it is the BedStory SpineAlign, sold in three builds from $699 for a Queen, and the 'recovery stack' idea is genuinely how the store is organised. But the source base behind the brand is thin: nearly everything written about Aeva traces to its own press releases, one of which ranks Aeva first in a list it compiled itself, and no independent review or hands-on test of the mattress exists. Here is what is actually verified about the mattress, the stack and the price — and the trial gap that should decide where you buy it.

Quick answer

The short answer: Aeva Vital sells the BedStory SpineAlign hybrid mattress — Premier 12-inch from $699, Pro 13-inch from $899.99 and Luxe 14-inch from $999.99 per Queen — alongside infrared saunas ($2,099–$3,000), freestanding tubs and a portable cold plunge. The mattress itself is a verified, well-specced firm hybrid. But Aeva states no sleep trial (BedStory direct offers 365 nights on the same bed), there are no bundle prices, and the 'No. 1 ranking' is Aeva's own press release, not independent testing.

At a Glance: The Aeva Vital Mattress and the Recovery Stack

Aeva Vital's homepage tagline is 'Better Living Starts at Home,' and the store is organised around a single idea: buy the whole recovery routine from one place. The catalogue is a hybrid mattress, five infrared saunas, two freestanding bathtubs and a portable ice bath, all sold direct-to-consumer online with home delivery. The mattress is the anchor product — the homepage calls it 'the hybrid mattress the rest of the catalogue is measured against' — and it is the only item in the stack with fully documented specs.

  • The mattress is a BedStory SpineAlign: an 8-layer hybrid with a TENCEL cover, gel-infused memory foam, cashmere cotton wool padding and 8.7-inch individually pocketed coils with 5-zone support. BedStory launched the SpineAlign collection in North America on June 23, 2026, and Aeva's page is transparent that the layer names and firmness ratings are BedStory's own.
  • Three builds, one price ladder: Premier 12-inch (Extra Firm, 8.5/10) from $699, Pro 13-inch (Firm, 8.0/10) from $899.99 and Luxe 14-inch (Medium Firm, 7.5/10) from $999.99 per Queen. Kings run $899.99 to $1,399.99. Only Queen and King are sold.
  • The stack around it: five infrared saunas at $2,099–$3,000, two freestanding tubs, and a portable cold plunge listed as 'Contact for pricing.' No bundle pricing has been published.

Two facts should frame everything else in this article. First, the mattress is genuinely well documented and cheap for its build — under $1,000 for a 14-inch certified hybrid. Second, the brand behind it has no independent track record: its 'rankings' are its own press releases, it states no sleep trial, and no reviewer has tested the mattress. This article separates the verified specs from the brand's own claims, so you can judge the bed on its own merits.

How to Choose: Which SpineAlign Build Fits You?

The three builds are the same mattress in three heights, and the choice is simpler than it looks: taller means softer, not firmer. The difference is the comfort layer above the coils — 8 cm of foam on the 12-inch, 11 cm on the 13-inch, 14 cm on the 14-inch — so each step up adds cushioning and drops the firmness by half a point on BedStory's 10-point scale.

Pick the 12-inch Premier (Extra Firm, 8.5/10) if you sleep on your back or stomach, or you have found plush mattresses sink too far under you. This is the firmest build and the cheapest at $699 for a Queen, and it is the one Aeva's own fit guidance points back and stomach sleepers toward. Couples also benefit here: the individually wrapped coils reduce motion transfer.

Pick the 13-inch Pro (Firm, 8.0/10) if you want a firm mattress with a little more give — the middle ground, and the one most buyers will land on. At $899.99 for a Queen it is $200 more than the Premier for 3 cm more comfort foam.

Pick the 14-inch Luxe (Medium Firm, 7.5/10) if you are a strict side sleeper who prefers a deeper, cradling surface. It is the softest of the three, and Aeva's own guidance flags side sleepers as the group that should think twice about the firmer builds. At $999.99 it is still under $1,000 for a Queen, which is cheap for a 14-inch hybrid with a latex-free but well-layered build.

The more important choice is where you buy it. Aeva and BedStory direct sell the same mattress at the same price — $699 for the 12-inch Queen — but BedStory direct offers a 365-night home trial while Aeva states only a 30-day return in the original packaging. Unless you are buying the sauna or tub at the same time, there is no reason to take the shorter return window for the same bed at the same price.

Our Verdict: Who Should Buy It

The mattress is a legitimate budget firm hybrid — buy the 13-inch Pro, and buy it from BedStory direct. At $899.99 for a Queen, the Pro is a 13-inch, 8-layer hybrid with TENCEL cover, gel memory foam, zoned pocketed coils and CertiPUR-US and OEKO-TEX certifications, and it undercuts most name-brand firm hybrids by hundreds of dollars. The construction is documented layer by layer, the firmness-by-height logic is transparent, and the price is the same at Aeva and at BedStory.

Buy from BedStory direct, not Aeva, unless you are also buying the sauna or tub. The deciding fact is the trial: 365 nights at BedStory direct versus a 30-day return in the original packaging at Aeva, for the same mattress at the same price. A mattress you cannot properly try is a mattress you should not buy at a premium — and here there is no premium to pay for the longer trial, so there is no trade-off at all.

The recovery stack is a real idea with an unproven seller. Buying a mattress, a sauna, a tub and a cold plunge from one storefront is convenient, and Aeva's catalogue is genuinely organised that way. But no bundle pricing exists, no independent testing exists, and the brand's own releases rank it first in lists it compiled itself. If the stack appeals to you, judge each product on its own specs and terms — and start with the mattress, where the verifiable facts are strongest.

What Early Coverage Says

There is no independent coverage — only the brand's own releases. No review of the Aeva Vital mattress appears on Sleep Foundation, Mattress Clarity, Sleepopolis, Tom's Guide, Wirecutter, Reddit, Trustpilot or YouTube as of November 9, 2026. The only blog-style coverage found is a generic October 28, 2026 SEO post that mentions aevavital.com in passing with no testing, and Aeva's own advice blog, which is brand content.

What does exist is a pattern worth naming. Four Aeva press releases — saunas (September 8, 2026), ice bath (October 31, 2026), mattress ranking and tubs (both November 5, 2026) — each present an Aeva product line as leading its category, and each was syndicated verbatim across small news domains. The mattress release ranks Aeva first of seven against Saatva, WinkBeds, Helix, DreamCloud, Brooklyn Bedding and Tempur-Pedic, while its own comparison table lists Aeva's price as 'Contact for pricing' and its sleep trial as 'Unknown' — the release does not even document the two facts a buyer needs most. Its methodology note says it draws on 'published brand specifications, company history and founding data, and search-demand patterns.' No testing, no reviewer, no sample size.

One more caution: most 'Vital mattress' search results are the Ecosa Vital, an Australian mattress (around $700–$1,175 AUD, 100-night trial, 15-year warranty) that has nothing to do with Aeva. Do not use Ecosa Vital specs, prices or reviews for this mattress.

What to watch: the first independent review, and whether Aeva publishes a sleep trial. Either would change this article's verdict materially. We will update it when they appear.
