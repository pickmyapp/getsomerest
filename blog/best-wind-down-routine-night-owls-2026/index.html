The Best Wind-Down Routine for Night Owls: A 90-Minute Schedule That Works With Your Chronotype

A 90-minute wind-down routine built for night owls: dim-light rules, screen curfews and a minute-by-minute schedule that works with a late chronotype — plus three tools that make lights-out easier.

Share
9 min read
By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Oct 15, 2026 · Last updated Oct 15, 2026

Night owls don't have broken sleep — they have late sleep. If your mind is sharpest at midnight, forcing a 10 PM bedtime just adds frustration to the tiredness. The fix isn't willpower; it's a routine built for a delayed clock: dim the light early, give your brain a real 90-minute runway, and protect your wake-up time like it matters. Here's the routine that works.

Quick answer

The best wind-down routine for night owls runs 90 minutes and works with — not against — a late chronotype: dim all lights 90 minutes before your target bedtime, cut screens 60 minutes out, take a warm shower, then spend 30 minutes on calm, low-stimulation wind-down (reading, stretching, journaling) before lights-out. The non-negotiable: keep the same wake-up time every day, weekends included.

The routine at a glance

Every effective night-owl wind-down has the same four phases: cool the light, slow the stimulation, power down the mind, then give your body identical lights-out cues. Here is the whole routine on one table — the minute-by-minute version comes further down.

PhaseWhenWhatWhy
Cool down 90–60 min before bed Dim every light; close work apps; last caffeine was hours ago Bright light and stimulants push a late clock even later — remove them first
Slow down 60–30 min before bed Screens off; warm shower or bath; water or herbal tea Warmth followed by cooling mimics the body's natural pre-sleep temperature drop
Power down 30–10 min before bed Paper book, gentle stretching, or journaling in dim light Low-stimulation, single-focus activity lets your rising melatonin do its work
Lights out Final 10 min Cool, dark, quiet room; slow breathing; same order nightly Repeated cues train your body to recognize the sequence as "sleep is next"

Example times assume a 1 AM target bedtime. Slide the whole schedule earlier or later to match yours — the spacing is what matters, not the clock.

Why night owls need a different wind-down

Roughly one in four people is an evening chronotype: your melatonin — the hormone that signals bedtime to the brain — simply rises later than it does for morning people. That is biology, not a character flaw. And it is exactly why generic advice like "be in bed by 10:30" fails night owls: you are being asked to fall asleep before your body has even started its bedtime signal.

The fix is not willpower. It is a routine that cooperates with a delayed clock: aggressive evening light management, a genuine 90-minute runway, and one non-negotiable anchor — the same wake-up time every day. Five principles make the whole thing work.

  • Light is the strongest signal. Bright evening light — especially overhead LEDs — suppresses melatonin. Night owls, whose melatonin already runs late, pay the steepest price. Dimming is the single highest-leverage move in this routine.
  • Anchor the morning, and the night follows. A fixed wake-up time (yes, weekends too) sets your circadian anchor more powerfully than any bedtime trick. Our guide on how to fix your sleep schedule in 3 days runs on the same principle.
  • Caffeine has a long tail. Its half-life is roughly 5–6 hours, so a 4 PM coffee is still at quarter strength by 10 PM. If you are a night owl, set a noon caffeine cutoff — your late clock cannot afford the extra delay.
  • Screens are a double hit. Alerting light plus engaging content: one pushes your clock later, the other keeps your brain revving. Phones off the nightstand is the cheapest upgrade in this article.
  • The routine is the point, not the products. No gadget replaces dimming, consistency and a real runway. The three tools below only make the routine easier to keep.

Start with light, not willpower

Ninety minutes before your target bedtime, change the light in your home like you mean it: overheads off, lamps on low and warm, hallway dim. If you work late, bias everything toward dim, warm task lighting. This one change does more for a night owl than any supplement or app, because it lets your already-late melatonin rise happen at all.

Caffeine, screens and late meals

The three saboteurs of a late clock are caffeine, screens and heavy late meals. Caffeine: done by noon. Screens: off 60 minutes before bed — phone charging outside the bedroom. Meals: a big dinner at 10 PM keeps digestion busy for hours; keep it light after 8 PM and save the heavy meal for earlier. A small snack or herbal tea closer to bed is fine.

Keep it even on weekends

Social jetlag — the Monday-to-Friday schedule plus a weekend free-for-all — is hardest on night owls. Keep your wake-up time within an hour of your weekday time, even on weekends. Out late? A sample night: 11:30 PM to 1 AM

Here is what the routine looks like in practice. At 11:30 you flip the apartment from stadium to campfire: overheads off, one warm lamp on, phone plugged in on the kitchen counter — not the nightstand. The shower at 11:40 is deliberately boring: warm water, no phone, just the feeling of the day washing off. By midnight the screens are dark, and the house goes quiet in a way that feels intentional rather than empty.

The last hour is the payoff. Tea, five minutes of slow breathing or stretching, then a book under the lamp. Around 12:45 you scribble tomorrow's chaos onto paper — work deadlines, errands, the thing you forgot to reply to — and close the notebook on it. Clothes laid out, alarm checked, lights out at 1 AM. Nothing heroic happened. That is the whole point: a routine so undemanding you will actually repeat it, which is the only way a routine works.

Frequently asked questions

What time should a night owl start winding down?

Count back 90 minutes from your realistic target bedtime — not an aspirational one. If you usually land in bed around 1 AM, start dimming the lights at 11:30 PM. The routine works because of the spacing between steps, so slide the whole schedule earlier or later to match your own target; what matters is that you protect the full 90-minute runway.

Can night owls train themselves to become morning people?

Only modestly. Chronotype is largely genetic — your melatonin rise simply runs later than a morning person's. Consistent bright morning light and a fixed wake-up time can shift your clock earlier by an hour or so, but a full conversion into a 6 AM riser is rarely realistic. The better play is to work with your clock: a real wind-down runway plus an anchored wake-up time.

Is it really that bad to scroll on my phone in bed?

It's a double hit: alerting screen light plus engaging content, both of which push sleep later. If you must use a screen, dim it to minimum, stick to something passive like an audiobook or calm video, and set a hard stop 30 minutes before bed. But a paper book in lamplight will always be the safer wind-down bet.

Does a warm shower before bed actually help you fall asleep?

Yes, and the mechanism is well understood: warm water raises your skin temperature, and the cooldown afterward drops your core body temperature — which mimics the body's natural pre-sleep signal. A 10 to 15 minute warm shower about 60 to 90 minutes before bed is the sweet spot.

What if I still can't fall asleep after 30 minutes in bed?

Don't lie there forcing it. Get up, go to a dim room, and read or stretch until you feel drowsy, then return to bed. This is the classic stimulus-control approach: it stops your brain from associating your bed with tossing and turning. If sleepless nights are frequent, that's worth a conversation with your doctor.

Key takeaways

  • The best wind-down for night owls runs 90 minutes: dim the light, kill the screens, warm up then cool down, and finish with calm low-stimulation activity.
  • Dimming is the highest-leverage move — night owls have the most to lose from bright evening light because their melatonin already runs late.
  • Anchor a fixed wake-up time (weekends included) and set a noon caffeine cutoff; the routine only works if the anchors hold.
  • The schedule adapts to your target bedtime — protect the spacing between steps, not the exact clock times.
