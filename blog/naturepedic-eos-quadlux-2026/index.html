Naturepedic EOS Quadlux Explained (2026)

Naturepedic's EOS Quadlux, announced November 4, 2026, is a 13-inch all-latex organic mattress with four split layers you can rearrange per side — priced $3,599 to $7,099 with no coils at all.

Share
9 min read
By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Nov 7, 2026 · Last updated Nov 7, 2026

Couples rarely agree on mattress firmness, and most mattresses force a compromise: one side too soft, the other too firm, or a middle ground nobody loves. Naturepedic's answer, announced November 4, 2026, is the EOS Quadlux — a 13-inch mattress built from four separate layers of certified organic latex, each split down the middle so partners can tune their own side. There are no coils, no polyurethane foam and no glues anywhere in the build. It is also the most expensive mattress Naturepedic has ever sold, starting at $3,599 and running to $7,099. Here is what the Quadlux actually is, how its four-layer system works, and who it makes sense for.

Quick answer

The short answer: the EOS Quadlux is a $3,599–$7,099, 13-inch all-latex organic mattress with four individually split layers that let each partner choose their own firmness. It has no coils, carries GOTS, GOLS and GREENGUARD Gold certifications, and ships roll-packed for at-home assembly. It suits couples who want organic materials and per-side customization — not budget shoppers.

At a Glance: The EOS Quadlux

Naturepedic has sold customizable organic mattresses for years through its EOS line, but every model until now topped out at three latex layers or mixed latex with coils. The Quadlux, announced November 4, 2026, is the first in the line to go all-latex with four layers — and the first to make all four layers individually split and swappable. The pitch is simple: a mattress you can tune like a piece of furniture, and re-tune later, instead of replacing.

  • Price: $3,599–$7,099 depending on size, sold direct at naturepedic.com, in 20-plus Naturepedic flagship stores and through 150-plus retailers. No per-size pricing has been published yet. You can also browse organic latex mattresses on Amazon to compare the category.
  • Build: 13 inches tall, four layers of certified organic latex, each zipped into its own organic cotton encasement. Quilted organic cotton and wool top with a brass-zippered cover. No coils, no polyurethane foam, no flame retardants, no fiberglass and no glues or adhesives.
  • Customization: the top two layers come in Plush, Medium, Cushion-Firm, Firm or Extra Firm, set independently per side. The bottom two layers are firm by default and provide the support coils normally would. Layers can be rearranged or replaced individually as comfort needs change.
  • Cooling: perforated latex plus the cotton-and-wool cover are designed to move air and wick moisture, rather than relying on a cooling cover or phase-change layer. Latex sleeps cooler than memory foam by nature, though it is not an actively cooled bed.
  • Certifications: GOTS, GOLS, EWG Verified, MADE SAFE, GREENGUARD Gold and UL Formaldehyde Free — one of the most certified builds in the mattress market.

The trade press picked it up fast: Home Textiles Today covered the launch on November 6, calling out the four-layer design and per-side customization as the story. No hands-on review exists yet — the mattress is three days old — so everything below is based on the announcement, trade coverage and what reviewers have already established about the EOS line it belongs to.

How to Choose: Is a Four-Layer Latex Mattress Right for You?

A four-layer organic latex mattress is a specific purchase. Start with the question it exists to answer: do you and your partner actually disagree about firmness? If you both sleep comfortably on a medium mattress, the Quadlux's headline feature buys you nothing — a three-layer Trilux or even a conventional latex hybrid would do the same job for less. The Quadlux earns its price when one of you wants plush and the other wants firm, or when your needs have changed before and you expect them to change again.

Next, weigh the organic build honestly. The certifications are real and extensive, and for shoppers who care about what is inside their mattress — no polyurethane foam, no flame retardants, no fiberglass, no glues — the Quadlux is one of the cleanest builds available at any price. But certifications do not change how a mattress feels. Latex has a distinct buoyant, responsive feel: it pushes back more than memory foam and does not cradle in the same sinking way. If you have never slept on latex, try one in a store before committing $3,599-plus to a material you might not like.

Practical points matter too. The Quadlux ships roll-packed and needs at-home assembly, and an all-latex queen is heavy — plan for two people and a clear afternoon. Measure your foundation: latex mattresses want solid support, so check the recommended slat spacing if you use a slatted base. And because the announcement gives no per-size prices and no Quadlux-specific trial or warranty, get those two numbers in writing from the product page or a store before ordering. The EOS line standard is a 100-night trial and a 25-year limited warranty, but confirm it applies to this model.

Finally, think about longevity. The replace-a-layer design is the Quadlux's quiet advantage: if the top layer softens after years of use, you swap one layer rather than the whole bed. That only pays off if you keep the mattress long enough — and at this price, you probably should.

Our Verdict: Who Should Buy It

The Quadlux is the best version of Naturepedic's core idea: a mattress that adapts to two different sleepers and keeps adapting. If you and your partner have spent years compromising on firmness, if you want certified organic materials without exceptions, and if you like the idea of replacing a single layer instead of a whole mattress down the road, this is the most complete expression of that idea on the market right now.

It is not the right pick for everyone. The price puts it firmly in luxury territory, and the fourth layer is a refinement, not a revolution — the Trilux does most of the same job for about $1,000 less. Shoppers who have never tried latex should test the feel first, because no amount of customization fixes a material you dislike. And anyone who wants a mattress delivered ready to sleep on, or a confirmed trial period in writing, should wait until Naturepedic publishes per-size pricing and Quadlux-specific terms.

Our take: buy it for the split-layer system and the organic build, not for the novelty of a fourth layer. If those two things are worth $3,599-plus to you, the Quadlux is the purest version of them Naturepedic has made. If they are not, the Trilux or the Classic will serve you just as well — and leave a meaningful amount of money in your pocket.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the Naturepedic EOS Quadlux cost?

Naturepedic lists the Quadlux at $3,599 to $7,099, depending on size. No per-size price breakdown has been published yet, so there is no confirmed queen price at the time of writing — check the product page on naturepedic.com before budgeting. For comparison, the three-layer EOS Trilux runs $2,599 to $4,999, so the fourth layer and the extra customization carry a real premium.

What makes the Quadlux different from the EOS Trilux?

The Trilux has three latex layers; the Quadlux has four, and all four are individually split down the middle. The top two layers are customizable in Plush, Medium, Cushion-Firm, Firm or Extra Firm on each side, while the bottom two layers are firm by default and take over the support job coils normally do. The extra layer gives finer control over feel, and any single layer can be swapped or replaced later instead of replacing the whole mattress.

Can partners really sleep at different firmness levels?

Yes — that is the point of the design. Each of the four latex layers is split down the middle, so one side can be set Plush while the other is Firm, and the layers can be rearranged as needs change. Latex also absorbs motion well, so movement on one side is less likely to disturb the other. It is the same split-layer idea Naturepedic uses across the EOS line, taken to four layers for the first time.

Does the Quadlux need a special bed base?

Naturepedic ships the Quadlux roll-packed in recyclable kraft paper for at-home assembly, and it works on standard foundations — a solid platform, slatted base or adjustable base. Because it is all latex with no coils, it is heavy for its size, so plan for two people to move and assemble it, and check the product page for the recommended slat spacing before ordering.

What trial and warranty does the Quadlux come with?

The November 4 announcement does not state a trial or warranty for the Quadlux specifically. Across the EOS line, independent reviewers report a 100-night trial (with a 30-night minimum before a return) and a 25-year limited warranty — treat those as the line standard, not a Quadlux promise, and confirm the exact terms on the product page before you buy.

  • The EOS Quadlux is Naturepedic's first four-layer all-latex organic mattress, announced November 4, 2026, priced $3,599–$7,099.
  • All four latex layers are individually split, so partners can set different firmness on each side — Plush to Extra Firm on the top two layers.
  • There are no coils, no polyurethane foam, no flame retardants and no glues; certifications include GOTS, GOLS and GREENGUARD Gold.
  • Layers can be rearranged or replaced individually, so a softened top layer does not mean a new mattress.
