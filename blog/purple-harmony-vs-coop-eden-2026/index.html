Purple Harmony vs Coop Eden: Which Cooling Pillow Is Actually Worth Your Money?

Purple Harmony ($229-$269) vs Coop Eden ($96-$116): which cooling pillow is better for hot sleepers? Cooling tech, loft, adjustability and real prices compared.

Share

By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Oct 16, 2026 · Last updated Oct 16, 2026

If you wake up flipping your pillow to the cool side, you don't need another cotton pillow — you need one engineered to move heat. The Purple Harmony ($229-$269) and the Coop Eden ($96-$116) are the two cooling pillows hot sleepers ask about most, and they attack the same problem from opposite directions: the Harmony uses a hyper-elastic gel grid core that sleeps cool by construction, while the Coop Eden pairs ventilated gel-infused fill with a bamboo-blend cover and full adjustability. One is a technology pillow; the other is a customizable one. Here is how they compare, price by price and night by night.

Quick answer

The Purple Harmony ($229 Standard / $269 King) is the better outright cooling pillow thanks to its gel grid core that keeps air moving all night, while the Coop Eden ($96 Queen / $116 King) is the better value: fully adjustable loft, a soft down-like feel, and a breathable Lulltra bamboo-blend cover. Choose the Harmony if you sleep extremely hot and want cooling without fiddling; choose the Eden if you want to dial in your exact loft for about half the price.

How cooling pillows actually work (and what to check)

Foam pillows sleep hot for a simple reason: dense foam traps the heat your head produces, and there's nowhere for it to go. Cooling pillows attack that problem with two different strategies. The Purple Harmony is a conduction design — its gel grid doesn't retain heat the way solid foam does, and the open grid structure gives warm air somewhere to escape. The Coop Eden is an airflow design — shredded, ventilated fill creates thousands of tiny air channels, and a breathable bamboo-blend cover wicks surface warmth.

Neither one is a refrigerator. Set your expectations on cooling, then judge everything else — loft, feel, maintenance — because that's what you'll live with every night.

  • Cooling method. Grid (Harmony) is the stronger passive cooler and stays consistent all night. Ventilated shredded fill (Eden) breathes well at first and fades toward neutral as the fill compresses under your head.
  • Loft and sleep position. Side sleepers need enough height to keep the neck aligned — roughly 5 to 7 inches for most adults. The Eden lets you build that height yourself; the Harmony sells it in three fixed sizes (low 5.5", standard 6.5", tall 7.5").
  • Adjustability. The Eden's extra fill bag is the real deal: you can go from stomach-sleeper thin to side-sleeper thick in minutes. The Harmony is what it is.
  • Weight and care. The Harmony is heavy — around five pounds — and its grid core is spot-clean only. The entire Eden (cover and core) goes in the washing machine, which matters more than you'd think over a year.
  • Trial and warranty. Both offer a 100-night sleep trial. The Harmony backs it with a 10-year warranty; the Eden carries a 5-year warranty. Sleep on your pick for a full month before deciding.

The breathable Lulltra fabric contains bamboo fibers, which makes it breathable and less heat-trapping — and gel memory foam draws heat away from the body.

Grid vs fill: two cooling philosophies

Purple's GelFlex grid is the same idea as their mattresses: a collapsible honeycomb that bends under pressure points and stays open everywhere else. Open space means airflow, and airflow means the pillow never builds up the heat pocket that makes you flip a foam pillow at 2 AM. The Eden goes the shredded-fill route: gel-infused memory foam cut into cross-cut pieces, mixed with microfiber so it doesn't clump, inside a gusseted Lulltra cover that holds its shape. It breathes well and feels luxuriously soft — but by design it eventually reaches equilibrium with your body heat.

Loft, position and the shoulder-gap problem

Most pillow discomfort for side sleepers isn't firmness — it's geometry. Your pillow has to fill the gap between your shoulder and your head, or your neck bends all night. That's the Eden's home turf: pull fill out or stuff it in until the height matches your build. Back sleepers generally want medium loft; stomach sleepers want thin. With the Harmony, you're matching your body to three preset heights, which works fine for most people but offers no fine-tuning. Pair either pillow with a cool room — our ideal bedroom temperature research explains why the room matters as much as the pillow.

Care, weight and the long game

The Eden's washability is underrated: pillows collect sweat, skin oils and dust mites, and a fully machine-washable pillow stays fresher years longer. The Harmony's spot-clean-only grid is the price of its technology — and at five pounds, it's noticeably heavy to maneuver and not a travel candidate. Both should last the length of their warranties if you follow care instructions, but expect the Eden's fill to need a fluff-and-adjust session every few months.

Our verdict: which pillow should you buy?

Buy the Purple Harmony if you are a genuinely hot sleeper who has cycled through cooling sheets, breathable toppers and still wakes up warm. Nothing in its price class sleeps cooler — the gel grid is the real deal, and the 100-night trial plus 10-year warranty make the $229-$269 an experiment you can unwind. It's also the pick if you want zero maintenance: no fluffing, no refilling, no loft math. The price is the obvious pain point, and the spot-clean-only care plus the weight are real everyday trade-offs.

Buy the Coop Eden if you want 80% of the cooling at 40% of the price, plus a pillow you can shape to your exact sleep position. The adjustable fill is the Eden's superpower — side sleepers who need a precise loft, combination sleepers whose needs change, and anyone who has ever bought a pillow that was "almost right." The bamboo-blend cover breathes well, the whole thing is machine washable, and the 5-year warranty is generous at $96-$116. Just know it's temperature-neutral rather than actively cold, so pair it with the other half of the heat equation: a cool room and breathable bedding, like our cooling mattress topper guide covers.

Frequently asked questions

Are heated mattress pads safe to leave on all night?

Yes, with a modern pad that has an auto shut-off timer and overheat protection, such as the ETL- or UL-certified models we compare here. Set the auto-off timer for the time you actually need — most sleepers use one or two hours after falling asleep rather than the full 12-hour window — and never use a pad that is damp or folded over itself.

Heated mattress pad vs electric blanket: which is better for sleep?

A heated mattress pad usually wins for sleep. It warms the bed from underneath so heat rises around you, it never slides off, and it can preheat the bed before you get in. Electric blankets trap heat on top and often make sleepers overheat; a pad plus your normal blanket lets you fine-tune warmth on each side.

How much does it cost to run a heated mattress pad?

Very little. A pad like the Bwarm HeatSync draws about 65 watts per side — roughly the same as an incandescent light bulb. Running one side for eight hours costs only a few cents of electricity, far less than running a 1,500-watt space heater to warm the whole bedroom.

Will a heated mattress pad fit my mattress?

Most fit mattresses up to 18 inches deep, which covers the majority of pillow-top and hybrid mattresses. If your mattress is thicker than 18 inches, pick a deep-pocket model — the Beautyrest Cotton pad here fits up to 21 inches, and all three pads use all-around elastic skirts so they stay put.

Can I wash a heated mattress pad?

Yes — all three pads we compare are machine washable. Detach the controller and cords first, wash on a gentle cycle, and make sure the pad is completely dry before plugging it back in. Never dry clean or bleach it, and never operate a pad that is still damp.
