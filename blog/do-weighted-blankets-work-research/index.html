Do Weighted Blankets Actually Work? What the Latest Research Says

Weighted blankets are claimed to ease anxiety and improve sleep. Here's what randomized trials and reviews through 2025 actually found — plus Bearaby vs Gravity vs YnM compared on price, fill and washability. Prices checked October 2026.

Share

By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Oct 15, 2026 · Last updated Oct 15, 2026

Walk any bedding aisle and you'll find weighted blankets promising calmer nights. The idea — gentle, even pressure that feels like a hug — is simple enough. But does it hold up in actual research? Over the past few years, randomized trials and systematic reviews have finally put the claims to the test. Here's what they found, and which blanket is worth your money.

Quick answer

The research on weighted blankets is promising but limited. Randomized trials and reviews through 2025 report that adults using weighted blankets often sleep better and feel calmer — one 2024 trial of 102 adults with insomnia found better sleep quality, less daytime sleepiness and lower anxiety after a month. But study sizes are small and results vary, so treat them as a comfort aid, not a treatment. This is a research summary, not medical advice.

What the research says, at a glance

The weighted blanket literature is still young, but it is no longer just marketing. These are the studies researchers keep citing — what was tested, who was tested, and what came out. The detailed verdict is further down.

StudyWhoWhat they found
2024 pilot randomized trial (Yu et al.) 102 adults with insomnia, one month of nightly use Better sleep quality scores, less daytime sleepiness, fatigue, anxiety, stress and bodily pain than a normal blanket; fewer nighttime awakenings on wrist actigraphy
2024 systematic review (Dawson, Flinders University) 18 studies of overnight use "Significant evidence" for better sleep in adults, reduced sleep-medication use, and better mood and pain reports. Results were mixed for children with ADHD or autism
2025 hospital study (Saint Luke's, Kansas City) 54 acutely ill adults 20 minutes under a 15-pound blanket lowered anxiety — and the drop persisted even after the blanket was removed
2025 randomized crossover trial (Scientific Reports) 40 adults, two 14-day periods Better sleep efficiency, fewer awakenings, and less anxiety with the weighted blanket versus an identical-looking placebo blanket
2025 mapping review (Lönn & Odéus) 38 studies across populations Positive signals but a small, heterogeneous evidence base — promising, not settled science

How to read weighted blanket research — and buy one that fits

The theory behind it is deep pressure stimulation — the same reason a firm hug feels calming. The research asks a narrower question than the marketing: in controlled studies, do people actually sleep better or feel calmer under the weight? Mostly, and modestly, yes — for adults.

Whether a specific blanket works for you depends less on the brand story and more on five practical details. Get these right and you will enjoy whatever blanket you buy; get them wrong and even the best-reviewed blanket becomes an expensive laundry problem.

  • Weight: the 10% rule. Researchers and brands converge on the same guidance: choose a blanket around 10% of your body weight. Too light and the pressure effect vanishes; too heavy and you will fight the blanket all night instead of relaxing under it.
  • Fill matters: glass beads vs knit. Most blankets use glass micro-beads sewn into a quilted grid; premium knit blankets like the Bearaby get their weight from the yarn itself, with no fill at all. The difference shows up in heat: quilts trap it, open knits breathe.
  • Size: cover yourself, not the bed. Weighted blankets are sized to drape over your body, not to cover the whole mattress like a comforter. If you share a bed, each sleeper wants their own weight — one blanket for two people doubles the load unevenly.
  • Heat: the honest trade-off. Extra layers plus extra weight equals extra warmth. Hot sleepers should look at open-knit builds first, and everyone should keep the room cool — our guide to the ideal bedroom temperature for sleep explains why.
  • Washability. A 20-pound blanket is a workout. Knit blankets like Bearaby's are machine washable on cold; quilted blankets usually rely on a removable, washable cover over a spot-clean inner.

Weighted blankets seem to offer a tangible, non-drug intervention for improving sleep quality.

Source: Dr. Suzanne Dawson, Flinders University — review of 18 studies in the American Journal of Occupational Therapy (2024)

The 10% body-weight rule

Choosing a weight is simple arithmetic: a 150-pound sleeper lands on a 15-pound blanket; 200 pounds lands on 20. Brands sell 10, 15, 20 and 25 pound versions because those four cover most adults. First-time buyers should stay on the lighter side of the rule — you can always size up, but a blanket that feels oppressive on night one rarely gets a night two.

Glass beads vs knit: the heat question

This is the real fork in the road. Glass-bead quilts (Gravity, YnM and most of the market) deliver even, clinical pressure and cost less — but the dense quilted construction holds heat. The Bearaby approach — a chunky hand-knit with no fill — distributes the weight through open yarn loops, so air moves through the blanket freely. It costs three to five times more, and the premium buys you exactly one thing: breathability. For a deeper look at sleeping cool in general, see our best cooling mattress toppers for night sweats.

What the research can't promise you

The honest reading of the literature: small trials, short durations, positive but modest signals — mostly in adults, mostly for perceived sleep quality and calm rather than objectively measured sleep architecture. Nobody has run the large, long trial that would settle this. So buy a weighted blanket the way you'd buy a very good pillow: for comfort, with realistic expectations, and from a brand with a sane return policy.

Our verdict — and who should buy which

The research does not crown a brand; it supports the concept. So the buying decision comes down to comfort priorities. The Bearaby Cotton Napper is the one we recommend for hot sleepers and anyone who wants the pressure without the heat trap: the open hand-knit is genuinely the most breathable weighted blanket design on the market, it uses no plastic beads, and it is machine washable. The price — from $199 — is the obvious trade-off, and at 20+ pounds it is a genuine workout to launder.

Buy the Gravity Weighted Blanket if you want the classic quilted experience from the brand that defined the category: even glass-bead pressure and a removable duvet cover at around $250 — though like most quilts, it sleeps warm. Buy the YnM Weighted Blanket if you are trying the concept for the first time and refuse to spend $200 to do it: at around $50 it delivers the same deep-pressure idea, with a wide weight range and removable covers. The build is less refined, but it is the cheapest honest entry point in the category.

Frequently asked questions

Do weighted blankets help with anxiety?

Small published studies report calmer feelings: a 2024 randomized trial found lower anxiety scores after a month of weighted blanket use, and a 2025 hospital study found that 20 minutes under a 15-pound blanket reduced anxiety, with the effect persisting after the blanket was removed. But these studies are small, so treat a weighted blanket as a comfort aid — not as a treatment for anxiety or any condition.

How heavy should a weighted blanket be?

The standard guidance used by researchers and brands alike is roughly 10% of your body weight: a 150-pound sleeper would choose a 15-pound blanket, and a 200-pound sleeper a 20-pound one. Going much heavier doesn't add benefit in the research — it mostly adds laundry-day regret.

Are weighted blankets safe?

Published studies report weighted blankets as well-tolerated by adults. They are not recommended for very young children, or for anyone who could not push the blanket off themselves. If you have breathing, circulation or sleep-related health concerns, talk to your doctor before using one — this article reports on research and comfort, not medical advice.

Will a weighted blanket make me sleep hot?

It depends on the build. Quilted glass-bead blankets can trap heat, while open-knit styles like the Bearaby breathe noticeably better — breathability is the main reason the Knit-style blanket earns its premium. If you sleep hot, a knit blanket plus a cool room is the safer combination; see our guide to the best cooling mattress toppers for night sweats for the rest of the heat picture.

Do weighted blankets work for children?

The evidence is mixed. A 2024 review of the research found limited sleep improvements for children with ADHD or autism spectrum disorder, although some parents reported better daytime functioning. Published guidance is cautious for young children — weighted blankets are best approached as an adult comfort product unless a clinician suggests otherwise.

Key takeaways

  • Randomized trials and reviews through 2025 report that adults under weighted blankets often sleep better and feel calmer — but studies are small and the evidence base is still preliminary.
  • Results are strongest for perceived sleep quality and calm in adults; evidence is mixed for children, and nobody has run the large long-term trial that would settle the science.
