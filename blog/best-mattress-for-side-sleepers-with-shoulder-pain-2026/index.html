Why Side Sleeping Hurts Your Shoulders

The shoulder problem is the mirror image of the hip problem, with one difference: the shoulder sticks out further. The ball-and-socket joint protrudes several inches beyond the torso, so when you lie on your side it carries a large share of your weight on a small contact area. Too firm, and pressure restricts blood flow, the arm goes numb, and you wake up shifting position every hour. A mattress that is too soft lets the shoulder drop until the neck bends sideways, which trades shoulder ache for neck ache. The right bed does two things at once: it lets the shoulder sink enough to decompress the joint, and it stops the rest of the body from sinking with it.

1. The comfort layer needs depth

Three to four inches of comfort material is the target for shoulder pain — a thick comfort layer that the shoulder can sink into before it meets the firmer transition layer underneath. Thin comfort layers bottom out under the shoulder. The Amerisleep AS3 pairs a 3-inch memory foam comfort layer with a dense base; the Helix Midnight Luxe uses a quilted pillow top over zoned coils to the same end. If a mattress spec sheet does not say how thick the comfort layer is, that is a warning sign.

2. Firmness by body weight

Weight decides how far you sink, so the right firmness moves with it. Under 130 lb, medium-soft (about 4 out of 10) gives enough shoulder sinkage without losing support. From 130 to 230 lb, medium to medium-plush (5 to 6) is the most reliable range. Over 230 lb, a medium bed with zoned support works better than a uniformly soft one — the shoulder still needs give, but the body needs the coils to hold it up.

3. Zoned support helps here too

Zoning is not just a hip feature. A softer zone under the shoulder and a firmer zone under the lower back lets the shoulder drop while the waist stays level, which is what keeps the spine straight. The Helix Midnight Luxe zones its coils this way, and the Amerisleep AS5's five HIVE zones do the same in foam. A uniformly medium bed can work, but a zoned one solves the geometry more directly.

4. The pillow is half the system

A side sleeper's pillow has to fill the gap between the shoulder and the head — usually 4 to 6 inches of loft, depending on shoulder width. A pillow that is too flat lets the head drop toward the mattress and bends the neck, which feels like shoulder pain by morning. If the mattress is right and the shoulder still aches, change the pillow before you change the bed.

Frequently asked questions

What firmness is best for side sleepers with shoulder pain?

Medium to medium-plush — about 4 to 6 on a 10-point scale — suits most side sleepers with shoulder pain. Under 130 lb, medium-soft works best; 130 to 230 lb, medium to medium-plush; over 230 lb, medium with zoned support, because a very soft bed lets the shoulder drop too far and twists the neck.

Can a mattress cause shoulder pain?

A mattress that is too firm for a side sleeper is a common cause of morning shoulder ache: the shoulder cannot sink in, so pressure builds on the joint all night and blood flow is restricted, which can cause numbness or tingling in the arm. A mattress that is too soft causes a different problem — the shoulder drops too far and the neck bends. The fix is a bed that cushions the shoulder while holding the rest of the body level.

Does a pillow matter for shoulder pain too?

Yes — the pillow and the mattress work as a pair. A side sleeper needs a pillow with enough loft to fill the gap between the shoulder and the head, or the neck bends down toward the mattress all night. If the mattress is right but the shoulder still aches, the pillow is the next thing to check — our best pillows for side sleepers covers loft and fill for exactly this.

How long should I test a mattress for shoulder pain?

Give it at least 30 nights. The foams soften slightly as they break in, and your body adjusts to a new surface over the first few weeks. All five mattresses here come with long trials — 365 nights on four of them — so a month of real sleep is the honest test.

When should shoulder pain send you to a doctor?

This guide is about comfort and sleep setup, not diagnosis. If shoulder pain is sharp, comes with weakness or numbness that does not fade, follows an injury, or has lasted more than a few weeks despite a better sleep setup, talk to a clinician — a mattress can ease pressure, but it cannot treat an injury or a joint condition.
