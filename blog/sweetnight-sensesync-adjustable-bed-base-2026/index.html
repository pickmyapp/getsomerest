At a Glance: The SenseSync Base and the CoolNest System

SweetNight's first smart sleep product is deliberately unglamorous in the best way: no sensors, no subscription, no app you have to keep updated. The SenseSync is an adjustable bed base built around positioning, and it anchors a four-product Cooling Sleep System the company has been assembling since September 2026.

  • The base: SenseSync Adjustable Bed Base, from $599.99 (queen $749.99), with independent head and foot adjustment, back and leg massage, Zero Gravity, Anti-Snore, and TV modes, USB charging ports, and under-bed lights.
  • The system: CoolNest Ultra Memory Foam Mattress (queen from $729.99, ice-silk cover, PCMflux and AirMesh foams, 7-zone support), CoolNest Cooling Comforter (CoolGlide fabric, breathable fill), and CoolNest Ergonomic Cooling Pillow (CoolGlide fabric, gel layer, memory foam core).
  • The strategy: SweetNight is moving from selling mattresses to selling a sleep environment — cooling from the mattress up through the bedding, with positioning underneath it all.
  • The catch: the base does not track sleep, cool actively, or connect to an app. If you want a data-driven sleep system, this is not it.

Launch coverage also notes SweetNight's usual sale pattern: sitewide discounts plus an extra 10% at checkout during launch periods, which can move the real price meaningfully below list. The 100-night trial and 10-year limited warranty framework SweetNight applies to its mattresses is the standard to compare the base's own terms against.

How to Choose an Adjustable Base (and Avoid the Usual Mistakes)

Adjustable bases fail buyers in predictable ways, and almost none of them are about the motor. Run through these before you pay.

Size and split. A single queen or king base moves both sleepers together. If you and a partner keep different hours or positions, a split configuration — two independent bases under one mattress setup — is the feature that actually matters, and it is the one buyers most often skip to save money, then regret. Confirm the split option exists for your bed size before ordering.

Mattress compatibility. Foam and most hybrids flex fine. Rigid innersprings, very thick pillow-tops, and some latex builds do not, and using an incompatible mattress can void its warranty. Check your mattress maker's adjustable-base policy in writing, not just the base maker's compatibility claim.

What you will actually use. Massage sounds great in the showroom and gets used for a week in most homes; presets (Zero Gravity, Anti-Snore, TV) get used nightly. Under-bed lighting is genuinely useful for middle-of-the-night trips; USB ports are convenient but rarely decisive. Weight capacity, wall-hugging design (so the base stays near the nightstand as the head rises), and the warranty on the motor matter more than any feature list suggests.

Assembly and returns. Bases are heavy, and returning one is a project. Check whether delivery includes setup, how long the trial lasts, and who pays return shipping. SweetNight offers a 100-night trial and a 10-year limited warranty on its mattresses; confirm the base carries comparable terms before checkout, since base warranties often differ from mattress warranties.

Our Verdict: Who Should Buy It

Here is the most defensible read on the SenseSync launch — what it gets right, where it is ordinary, and who it is actually for.

Buy it if: you want adjustable positioning — reading, TV, leg elevation, snore-reducing head lift — at a mid-range price, and you like the idea of building a cooling setup piece by piece. At $599.99 to $749.99 for the common sizes, SenseSync undercuts most name-brand adjustable bases, which typically start around $800 to $1,000, while including the full preset and massage set. For a first adjustable base, that is a sensible price to experiment at.

Skip it if: you want sleep tracking, automatic firmness adjustment, or active temperature control. SenseSync is a positioning base, not a smart mattress system: it has no sensors, no sleep data, and no cooling or heating of its own. The cooling in the CoolNest system comes from the mattress, comforter, and pillow, not the base. If active cooling is the goal, an Eight Sleep Pod or ChiliSleep Dock Pro is the honest comparison — at several times the price.

The system question. SweetNight's pitch is that cooling should extend across the whole sleep environment, not just the mattress. That is reasonable as far as it goes — a cooling mattress under a heat-trapping comforter is a half-measure — but each piece should earn its place. The base earns its place on positioning alone. The mattress earns its place if you sleep hot. The comforter and pillow are optional extras, not requirements, and the system works fine if you buy only the pieces that solve your actual problems.

How SenseSync Compares to Other Adjustable Bases

Adjustable bases cluster into three price bands, and SenseSync sits at the top of the budget band — which is exactly where the value is.

Budget bases ($300–$600) typically offer head and foot adjustment, a basic remote, and little else: no massage, no presets, no USB, no lighting. SenseSync's starting price of $599.99 is just above this band, but it brings the full feature set — massage, presets, USB, under-bed lights — that budget bases skip. That makes it a value outlier rather than a budget pick.

Mid-range bases ($800–$1,500) from established brands add wall-hugging design, quieter motors, better remotes or apps, and longer motor warranties. SenseSync matches them on features but not necessarily on refinement: its motor noise, app support, and long-term reliability are unproven, since the product is weeks old. The 10-year limited warranty framework SweetNight uses for mattresses is reassuring, but base-specific warranty terms deserve a check before purchase.

Premium bases ($1,500+) add app control, sleep tracking integrations, and luxury finishes. SenseSync does not compete here and does not pretend to — there is no app, no sensor, no data. Buyers who want a base that talks to a sleep app should look elsewhere; buyers who want a remote and presets will not miss the app.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the SenseSync adjustable bed base cost?

SweetNight lists the SenseSync base from $599.99, with the queen size listed at $749.99 in launch coverage, and a top variant at $1,199.99. SweetNight runs frequent sitewide sales — launch-period coverage noted an extra 10% off applied at checkout — so the price you actually pay depends on timing. As with all prices on this site, check the live listing before buying, since sale prices move.

What sizes does the SenseSync base come in?

SweetNight lists the base in multiple sizes with four price points ($599.99, $699.99, $749.99, and $1,199.99); launch coverage confirms the queen at $749.99. SweetNight's own product page carries the full size list and current pricing, and split sizes for couples are the configuration to look for if you and a partner want independent adjustment on each side.

Does the SenseSync base work with any mattress?

Adjustable bases work best with mattresses designed to flex — most foam and hybrid mattresses are built for them, while traditional rigid innersprings and some very thick pillow-tops are not. SweetNight designed SenseSync as part of its Cooling Sleep System around the CoolNest Ultra mattress, but the base is sold on its own too. Before pairing it with a mattress you already own, check that mattress's warranty: some brands void coverage if the mattress is used on an adjustable base it was not designed for.

What are Zero Gravity, Anti-Snore, and TV modes?

These are one-touch presets in the SenseSync Smart Sleep Control System. Zero Gravity raises head and legs into the position NASA popularised, distributing weight to take pressure off the lower back. Anti-Snore lifts the head slightly, which can reduce the airway narrowing behind some snoring — it is a comfort feature, not a treatment for sleep apnea. TV mode raises the head into a reading and watching position while keeping the legs lower. Independent head and foot adjustment lets you fine-tune beyond the presets.

Do I need the whole CoolNest system, or can I buy the base alone?

The base is sold on its own, and SweetNight says each of the four Cooling Sleep System products works independently. The system is a bundle strategy, not a lock-in: the CoolNest Ultra mattress (queen from $729.99) handles cooling with an ice-silk cover, PCMflux foam, AirMesh foam, and 7-zone support; the CoolNest cooling comforter adds CoolGlide fabric and breathable fill; and the CoolNest ergonomic cooling pillow pairs CoolGlide fabric with a gel layer and memory foam core. Buy the base alone if positioning is your problem; add the cooling pieces only if heat is also your problem.
