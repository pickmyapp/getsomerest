Garmin CIRQA Screenless Sleep Band Explained (2026)

Garmin's CIRQA Smart Band, announced July 21, 2026, is a $199.99 screenless band with up to 10 days of battery, full sleep staging, HRV, Body Battery and Training Readiness — and no subscription required.

By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Nov 5, 2026 · Last updated Nov 5, 2026

The most interesting sleep wearable of 2026 has no screen at all. Garmin's CIRQA Smart Band, announced July 21, 2026, is the company's first screen-free band, and its pitch is aimed squarely at the membership-band market: a $199.99 one-time purchase, up to 10 days of battery, and Garmin's full health and training software — sleep stages and score, heart rate variability, Body Battery, and Training Readiness — with no subscription required for any of it. For sleepers who have been paying Whoop $199 to $359 every year, or who simply cannot sleep comfortably in a watch, the CIRQA is the first band that removes both the screen and the bill at once.

Quick answer

The short answer: the Garmin CIRQA is a $199.99 screenless band with up to 10 days of battery that tracks sleep stages, sleep score, HRV, Body Battery, stress, Pulse Ox, and Training Readiness in the free Garmin Connect app — no subscription required. It has no display and no onboard GPS, so it suits sleepers and recovery-focused athletes, not runners who want maps on their wrist. Buy it if you want Whoop-style data without a yearly fee.

At a Glance: The CIRQA

Garmin announced the CIRQA Smart Band on July 21, 2026, as its first screen-free smart band, with all of its features available without a subscription. The hardware is deliberately minimal: a small sensor pod that snaps into a woven ComfortFit nylon band, worn on the wrist or, with an optional accessory band, on the upper arm.

  • Price: $199.99 once, in the US (£179.99 in the UK, AU$299.99 in Australia). No subscription required for core health, sleep, and training data.
  • Battery: up to 10 days per charge, with about 90 minutes to refill. Independent testing has measured closer to 5–7 days in real use — still a full week, but not the full claim.
  • Weight and size: the pod weighs about 14.8 grams and measures roughly 27 × 47 × 9 mm; with the band, the whole thing is about 20 grams — lighter than any Garmin watch.
  • Sleep tracking: sleep stages, sleep score, sleep coach guidance, nap detection, smart wake alarm, heart rate variability, respiration, overnight skin temperature, and Pulse Ox, all in the free Garmin Connect app.
  • Training data: Body Battery, Training Readiness, HRV status, VO2 max, training load, training status, stress tracking, and workout benefit — the same wellness engine Garmin puts in its mid-range watches.
  • What it lacks: no display, no onboard GPS, no floor counting, and no heart-rhythm check. One side button starts and stops activities.

You can find it via Amazon search, or direct from Garmin.

How to Choose: CIRQA vs the Alternatives

The CIRQA's real competition is not other watches — it is the membership bands and rings that ask you to keep paying. Choose by the bill first, then by the shape.

Choose the CIRQA over Whoop if you hate subscriptions. Whoop costs $199 to $359 every year and stops working when you stop paying; the CIRQA costs $199.99 once and keeps working. Over three years, that is a saving of roughly $400 to $880, and the CIRQA gives you comparable sleep and recovery data — stages, score, HRV, Body Battery — without a monthly charge. Whoop still wins on community features and its app experience, but on value the CIRQA is not close.

Choose the CIRQA over a smart ring if you want training data. Rings like the RingConn Gen 2 and Samsung Galaxy Ring are excellent sleep trackers with no subscription, but they do not offer Garmin's Training Readiness, training load, or VO2 max. If recovery for sport matters as much as sleep, the band's training stack is the deciding factor. If you simply cannot tolerate anything on your wrist at night, a ring remains the better shape — see our no-subscription ring comparison.

Choose a Garmin watch instead if you need a screen or GPS. The CIRQA has no display and no onboard GPS, so runners who want pace and maps on their wrist, and anyone who checks notifications mid-workout, should look at a Forerunner or Venu instead. The CIRQA also pairs well as a second device: wear a watch by day and the band at night, or use Garmin's cheaper Index Sleep Monitor if sleep-only tracking is all you need.

Note the optional Connect+ tier. Garmin's Connect+ plan costs $6.99 a month or $69.99 a year and adds coaching features, but Garmin says existing Connect features remain free, and none of the metrics above sit behind the paywall. Live heart-rate broadcasting was briefly paywalled at launch and rolled back after user complaints — a reminder to check exactly which features are free before you buy.

The sensor is Garmin's Elevate Gen 4 optical heart-rate sensor, paired with skin temperature, Pulse Ox, and an accelerometer. It comes in Black, Captain Blue, French Gray, and Mauve, and the optional upper-arm band costs $59.99. Garmin's sync to Apple Health and Health Connect is one-way and does not include HRV, which matters if you keep your health history in Apple's app — check that limitation before switching ecosystems.

Our Verdict: Who Should Buy It

Buy the CIRQA if you want deep sleep and recovery data without a subscription. At $199.99 once, it is the best-value screenless tracker of 2026 for most people: full sleep staging, HRV, Body Battery, and Training Readiness, in a 20-gram band you can actually sleep in, with a week of battery even on conservative real-world estimates.

Buy it especially if you already trust Garmin's numbers. If you own a Garmin watch, the CIRQA slots into the same Connect app and the same metrics, so there is no new system to learn — and as a night-only band it solves the oldest Garmin complaint, that watches are uncomfortable to sleep in.

Skip it if you need GPS, a screen, or heart-rhythm checks. Runners, triathletes who train by the watch, and anyone who wants ECG-style rhythm data should buy a screened Garmin watch instead. Also note availability: Garmin's US store listed an 8-to-12-week shipping estimate in some checks during 2026, so confirm stock before ordering for a gift or a trip.

What Garmin Is Really Betting On

Garmin did not build the CIRQA to win the watch market — it built it to stop losing the subscription-band market. Whoop proved that millions of people will pay yearly for recovery data on a screenless band, and Garmin, which already owned the underlying sensor and software, simply had to package it without a watch case. The CIRQA is that package, and its pricing is the strategy: one $199.99 payment against Whoop's perpetual bill makes the comparison almost unfair, and Garmin's training stack — Training Readiness, training load, Body Battery — is deeper than anything else at this price without a subscription.

The compromises are honest ones. An older Gen 4 sensor, no GPS, and no display keep the price and weight down, and the optional Connect+ tier is Garmin's way of monetizing coaching without locking core data behind it. The early stumble over paywalling live heart-rate data, quickly reversed, shows the tension in that model — but the reversal also shows Garmin listening. For sleepers, the verdict from reviewers has been consistent: this is 2026's best screenless band, and the screenlessness is the feature, not the sacrifice.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Garmin CIRQA need a subscription?

No. Heart rate, HRV status, sleep stages and score, Body Battery, stress, Pulse Ox, skin temperature, VO2 max, training load, and Training Readiness all land in the free Garmin Connect app. Connect+ is an optional paid tier ($6.99/month or $69.99/year) that adds coaching features, but none of the core metrics sit behind it.

How long does the CIRQA battery last?

Garmin claims up to 10 days per charge, with about 90 minutes to refill. Independent testing has measured roughly 5 to 7 days in real use, which is still a full week between charges and far longer than any screened smartwatch.

Can you sleep comfortably in the CIRQA?

Yes — that is its main design goal. At about 20 grams with its woven nylon band and with no screen to glow or catch on bedding, it is easier to sleep in than any Garmin watch, and it tracks sleep stages, sleep score, naps, and a smart wake alarm.

Does the CIRQA have GPS or a screen?

No. It has no display — one side button starts and stops activities — and no onboard GPS, so it cannot show pace, maps, or notifications on your wrist. Runners who need those features should buy a Garmin watch instead.

Is the CIRQA better than Whoop?

On value, yes: $199.99 once versus $199–$359 every year for Whoop, with comparable sleep and recovery data and no device lockout if you stop paying. Whoop still leads on community features and app polish, but for sleep and recovery tracking without a bill, the CIRQA is the stronger buy.

Key Takeaways

  • Garmin's CIRQA Smart Band, announced July 21, 2026, costs $199.99 once with no subscription required for core health, sleep, and training data.
  • It tracks sleep stages, sleep score, nap detection, smart wake, HRV, Body Battery, stress, Pulse Ox, respiration, and Training Readiness in the free Garmin Connect app.
  • Battery is rated up to 10 days; independent testing suggests 5–7 days in real use.
  • The pod weighs about 14.8 grams (~20 grams with band), has no display and no onboard GPS, and can be worn on the wrist or upper arm.
  • Over three years it saves roughly $400–$880 versus Whoop's annual membership — the strongest value case of any screenless tracker in 2026.
