TEMPUR-ActiveBreeze Smart Bed Explained (2026)

Tempur-Pedic's TEMPUR-ActiveBreeze Smart Bed pairs a 13-inch hybrid mattress with the ProSmart Air Base to cool or warm each side by up to 30 degrees — Twin XL from $9,398, Queen $10,098, with Sleeptracker-AI coaching and snore response built in.

Couples rarely agree on bedroom temperature — one sleeps hot, the other reaches for an extra blanket. Tempur-Pedic's answer is the TEMPUR-ActiveBreeze Smart Bed: a 13-inch hybrid mattress on the TEMPUR-Ergo ProSmart Air Base, which uses fans to push cool or warm air up through the mattress, separately on each side, across a claimed 30-degree range. It starts at $9,398 for a Twin XL — $10,098 for a Queen after a $500-off offer running on Tempur-Pedic's site — and it bundles Sleeptracker-AI sleep coaching, automatic snore response and relaxation programs into the same purchase. Here is what the bed does, how it compares with the water-cooled competition, and who should actually spend five figures on it.

Quick answer

The short answer: the TEMPUR-ActiveBreeze Smart Bed is a 13-inch hybrid mattress plus the ProSmart Air Base that actively cools or warms each side of the bed by up to 30 degrees, with three cooling levels, a 60-minute bed-warming feature, Sleeptracker-AI coaching and automatic snore response. Twin XL starts at $9,398 and Queen at $10,098 after Tempur-Pedic's $500-off offer — far more than a cooling cover, but the only system that heats and cools, adjusts, tracks and answers snoring in one purchase.

At a Glance: The TEMPUR-ActiveBreeze Smart Bed

Tempur-Pedic's ActiveBreeze is the brand's second-generation active-climate bed, and the first fully integrated with Sleeptracker-AI. Instead of relying on breathable foam or a cooling cover, it moves conditioned air through the bed itself: fans in the base push air up through a dual-chamber layer in the mattress, on each side independently.

  • Price: Twin XL $9,398 after a $500-off offer (list $9,898); Queen $10,098 (list $10,598); King $14,097; Split King $18,796. Mattress and ProSmart Air Base sold as a set. Prices as listed November 8, 2026.
  • Mattress: 13-inch hybrid, medium feel, tight top, with a patented dual-chamber air-distribution layer that spreads cool or warm air evenly across your body.
  • Climate: up to 30 degrees of cooling or warming range, per Tempur-Pedic. Three cooling levels (low, medium, high), two programs — Temp-Curve, which follows your body's natural temperature rhythm, and Constant mode for up to 10 hours — plus active bed warming for up to 60 minutes on one or both sides.
  • Base: TEMPUR-Ergo ProSmart Air Base with specially designed fans, head and foot adjustment, and automatic snore response that raises the headrest about 12 degrees when snoring is detected.
  • Tracking: Sleeptracker-AI built in — daily sleep quality reports, personalized insights and coaching, and relaxation programs. No subscription fee stated.
  • Controls: included remote or the Sleeptracker-AI app, with blue LEDs for cooling levels and red for warming.

The headline number is the 30-degree range: Tempur-Pedic rates the bed as delivering a sleep temperature range of up to 30 degrees from warm to cooling, per side. That is far beyond the 5–10 degrees of passive cooling its own Breeze foam mattresses claim — and it is a manufacturer rating, which is why the rest of this article keeps the claim and the evidence for it separate.

How to Choose: Is an Active-Climate Bed Worth It?

Start with the problem you are actually solving. If you and your partner fight over the thermostat, or one of you wakes up sweaty while the other is cold, dual-zone active climate is the only bedding product that fixes both sides at once — passive cooling covers and breathable sheets can only move you toward room temperature. The ActiveBreeze is built for exactly that household: two people, two different sleep temperatures, one bed.

Next, check what you already own. The ActiveBreeze is a full replacement, not an add-on. The mattress is sold as a set with the ProSmart Air Base, and retailer listings state the base must be purchased with the mattress. If your current mattress is new and you like it, a cover system such as the Eight Sleep Pod 6 or the ChiliSleep Dock Pro gives you active cooling for a fraction of the price, on the bed you already sleep on. The ActiveBreeze only makes financial sense when you are already shopping for a new premium mattress and base.

Then weigh the subscription math. The ActiveBreeze charges no monthly fee for its Sleeptracker-AI features, while the Pod 6 needs Autopilot at $199 to $399 a year. Over five years that is $1,000 to $2,000 the Pod 6 costs that the ActiveBreeze does not — which narrows, but does not close, a price gap of roughly $7,000 on a Queen. The no-subscription design is a genuine long-term saving, not a reason on its own to spend $10,098.

Finally, be honest about the unknowns. The 30-degree range is Tempur-Pedic's own rating, and no independent lab has published a test of how much the surface actually cools in a real bedroom. The snore response — raising the head about 12 degrees when snoring is detected — is a comfort feature, not a treatment. If you need the cooling number verified before spending five figures, wait for independent reviews. If you trust the brand's engineering and want the most complete climate bed you can buy in one box, this is it.

Our Verdict: Who Should Buy It

The ActiveBreeze is the most complete climate-control bed you can buy in a single purchase. No cover system heats and cools, no cover includes an adjustable base, and none answers snoring by raising the head for you. The air-based design also avoids the water reservoir, tubes and potential leaks of water-cooled systems, and the lack of a subscription means the price you pay is the price you pay.

The case against it is the price, and it is a strong one. A Queen at $10,098 costs about five times a Pod 6 cover and roughly eight times a ChiliSleep Dock Pro, before you count the Autopilot saving. The cooling claim is manufacturer-rated, not independently verified, and the mattress itself is a single medium feel — if you need soft or firm, this bed does not offer it. And because the mattress and base are sold together, you cannot try the climate system on a mattress you already love.

Our take: if you are a couple with opposite sleep temperatures, you are already budgeting for a premium mattress and adjustable base, and you want heating, cooling, tracking and snore response in one purchase with no monthly fee, the ActiveBreeze is the right bed and the price is the price of that completeness. If you only need cooling, or you like your current mattress, buy a Pod 6 or a Dock Pro and keep the $7,000. And if the 30-degree claim is what would sell you, wait for an independent test — a claim that big deserves a second source.

What Early Coverage Says

Coverage so far is announcement- and listing-level. Tempur Sealy International announced the second-generation ActiveBreeze as the first of the line fully integrated with Sleeptracker-AI, with customizable climate programs tied to each sleeper's schedule. Retailer listings repeat the same core claims: a sleep temperature range of up to 30 degrees, patented dual-zone climate control, ActiveAir Technology with a dual-chamber air-distribution layer, three cooling levels, and active bed warming.

Tempur-Pedic's own help center adds the operational detail the listings skip. Cooling runs in two programs — Temp-Curve, which gradually varies airflow to mimic the body's natural temperature rhythm, and Constant mode, which holds one level for up to 10 hours. Bed warming runs for up to 60 minutes on one or both sides. The user manual confirms the snore response raises the head about 12 degrees, and notes the TEMPUR-ProAir sheet set made for the bed.

What nobody has published yet is an independent test: no reviewer has measured surface temperatures overnight, compared the two sides, or checked the snore response against a reference. That is normal for a bed at this price — few outlets buy a $10,000 review unit — but it means every performance number in this article is the manufacturer's, and we have marked it as such.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the TEMPUR-ActiveBreeze Smart Bed cost?

A Twin XL starts at $9,398 after Tempur-Pedic's $500-off offer (list price $9,898), and a Queen is $10,098 (list $10,598). Retailers list a King at $14,097 and a Split King at $18,796 after the same $500 discount. The price is for the mattress and the ProSmart Air Base as a set — the base is required, and the mattress is not sold on its own. Prices are as listed on November 8, 2026, and the $500-off offer applies automatically in the cart on Tempur-Pedic's site.

How does the ActiveBreeze cool and warm the bed?

Fans inside the TEMPUR-Ergo ProSmart Air Base quietly circulate cool or warm air up through the mattress, which has a patented dual-chamber air-distribution layer that spreads the airflow evenly across your body. You choose from three cooling levels — low, medium or high — and two cooling programs: Temp-Curve, which gradually varies airflow to mimic your body's natural temperature rhythm through the night, and Constant mode, which holds one level steady for up to 10 hours. An active bed-warming feature warms one or both sides for up to 60 minutes, for example before bed or as you wake. Each side is controlled independently, so one partner can cool while the other warms.

Does the ActiveBreeze need a subscription?

No. The Sleeptracker-AI features — sleep tracking, daily sleep quality reports, personalized coaching and insights, automatic snore response and relaxation programs — are included with the bed, with no monthly fee stated at purchase. That is a real difference from the Eight Sleep Pod 6, whose Autopilot membership costs $199 to $399 a year on top of the cover. As with any smart bed, check the current terms on the product page before ordering, since app features can change.

What does Sleeptracker-AI do?

Sleeptracker-AI is the bed's built-in sleep system. It monitors your sleep and reports daily information about sleep quality, with personalized insights and coaching meant to help you improve it over time. It also powers the automatic snore response: when it detects snoring, it raises the headrest by about 12 degrees to open the airway and reduce the interruption. A suite of relaxation programs is included to help you fall asleep and stay asleep. You control everything from the included remote or the Sleeptracker-AI app.

Is the ActiveBreeze better than the Eight Sleep Pod 6?

They solve the same problem in different ways. The ActiveBreeze is an air-based, whole-bed system: it heats as well as cools, includes a full adjustable base with snore response, and charges no subscription — but it costs roughly three to four times a Pod 6 cover and you must buy the mattress and base together. The Pod 6 is a water-based cover that fits the mattress you already own, starts at $1,999 for a Solo cover, and cools with water rather than air, but it needs the $199–$399 a year Autopilot membership for its full features. If you want one purchase that replaces the bed, heats and cools, and answers snoring, the ActiveBreeze is the more complete system. If you like your current mattress and want the cheapest route into active cooling, the Pod 6 makes more sense.
