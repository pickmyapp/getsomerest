The Short Answer: 5 Apps That Are Actually Free

Most meditation apps are free to download and then charge $70 to $80 a year for everything worth using. The five apps below are different: they are free all the way through, with no trial, no paywall and no card — four of them run by non-profits, and the fifth giving away a library so large it is the main product. All five have sleep content for anxious nights; they differ on choice and structure.

Our five picks: Insight Timer is free with more than 300,000 tracks and is the best all-round buy — which is to say, it costs nothing; Medito is completely free with no ads and no account needed; Smiling Mind is completely free and the best choice for families; UCLA Mindful is completely free and the best structured course; and the Healthy Minds Program is completely free and the best science-based training program.

If you only remember one thing: download Insight Timer tonight, start its free seven-day course, and decide in two weeks whether you need anything else. You probably will not.

What “Actually Free” Means — and What to Check

App stores make it hard to tell what free means. An app can be free to download, free for seven days, free for a few starter sessions, or free forever — and the listing rarely says which. Here is how to read the tiers, and what matters for a sleep practice specifically.

Free forever vs free trial vs free starter pack

Four apps in this guide are free all the way through, with no subscription to unlock: Medito, the Healthy Minds Program, UCLA Mindful and Smiling Mind — all run by non-profits, all funded by donations. Insight Timer is different: it is free with a huge library, and an optional $60-a-year subscription adds extras like offline listening. Calm and Headspace are the opposite shape — a small free starter pack, then $70 to $80 a year for the real library. Balance sits in between: a free year is often offered to new sign-ups, then $69.99 a year.

Library size vs structure

Insight Timer's 300,000-plus tracks are the biggest free library in the category, but size is also its weakness: with that much choice, beginners stall. A structured course is the easy way in — Insight Timer's free seven-day course is one, UCLA Mindful's is another. Medito and the Healthy Minds Program are structured by design: courses and 30-day challenges that tell you what to do next. If you browse and freeze, pick structure over size.

Sleep content is a separate question

Not every meditation app is a sleep app. For bedtime you want guided sleep meditations, sleep music, bedtime stories and a timer with ambient sounds — not just daytime stress courses. Insight Timer and Calm are the strongest here; Medito has sleep stories and a sleep timer too; the Healthy Minds Program and UCLA Mindful are more about daytime practice, and their sleep content is thinner. If sleep is your main goal and you are willing to pay, Calm is worth it. Our white noise app guide covers the sound-only alternative if voices keep you awake.

Ads, accounts and offline listening

Free apps pay for themselves somehow, and the how matters at bedtime. Medito has no ads and needs no account. Insight Timer's free tier is ad-supported in places, and offline listening is a paid extra. Smiling Mind and UCLA Mindful are free with no ads.

What the research actually says

Keep expectations honest. A 2024 meta-analysis of 45 trials found that mindfulness apps reduced symptoms of depression and anxiety by a small amount compared with control groups (Linardon, Clinical Psychology Review, 2024). Small is still real — and the app you will actually open matters more than the one with the best study. Start free, pay only once you know you will use it, and treat the app as a wind-down habit, not a treatment. If anxiety is interfering with your days or your sleep most nights, talk to a doctor or a therapist. Our Pick: Insight Timer — Free, With the Biggest Library

If you download one app, download Insight Timer. It is free, and its free tier is the main product, not a teaser: more than 300,000 guided meditations, talks and music tracks from about 20,000 teachers, hundreds of free courses, live meditation and yoga classes, and a customizable meditation timer. For sleep it has thousands of sleep tracks — guided meditations, sleep music and bedtime stories.

The honest drawback is the size of the library. With 300,000 tracks, choosing one can take longer than the meditation. The fix is to start with a course, not the search bar: the free Learn How to Meditate in Seven Days gives you one session a day and no decisions. If you want structure without the choice problem, Medito is the better first app — and it is the pick we would download second. The optional $60-a-year subscription adds offline listening; you do not need it to build a practice.

One practical tip: try the app for two weeks before you decide anything. Most people stop using an app within weeks — so the one you will actually open at 10 p.m. is the right one, and only two weeks of use answers that.

The 5 Free Apps, One by One

1. Insight Timer — free, optional $60 a year

The best free meditation app for most people, simply because of volume. More than 300,000 free guided meditations, talks and music tracks from about 20,000 teachers, plus a timer for unguided practice, ambient sounds for sleep or focus, live sessions and short courses. The library spans secular, spiritual, religious and philosophical traditions. The trade-offs: the sheer choice can overwhelm beginners, and offline listening is a paid extra.

2. Medito — completely free

The best app if you want no paywall at all. Medito is made by the Medito Foundation, a registered non-profit in the Netherlands. It is free with no trial, no premium tier and no ads, and you do not even need to create an account. It includes guided meditations, 30-day challenges, sleep stories, breathing exercises and a timer with ambient sounds. The trade-off is size: hundreds of sessions rather than hundreds of thousands, and it runs on donations, so it will not match Insight Timer's range. If you want a calm, ad-free app with clear courses and nothing to buy, Medito is the one to try first.

3. Smiling Mind — completely free

The best free app for families, and the most quietly generous one in this guide. Smiling Mind is an Australian not-for-profit, free for individuals and families, with programs for adults, children and teenagers — including sleep programs for kids, which none of the paid apps give away. It is structured around short daily programs rather than a giant library. The trade-off is depth for adults: the catalog is smaller and less varied than Insight Timer's, and the sleep content for adults is thinner than its kids' sleep content.

4. UCLA Mindful — completely free

The best free structured course. UCLA Mindful comes from the UCLA Mindful Awareness Research Center, and the app is built around a free mindfulness course with a clear beginning and end — the closest thing to a class in this guide, and one that costs nothing. It includes guided meditations in English and Spanish, a timer, and short practices. The trade-off is scope: it is a course, not a library, so once you finish it you will want a second app for variety.

5. Healthy Minds Program — completely free

The best free science-based program. The Healthy Minds Program comes from Humin, a nonprofit formerly called Healthy Minds Innovations, founded by neuroscientist Richard Davidson at the University of Wisconsin. The app trains four pillars — awareness, connection, insight and purpose — as skills, with short daily practices. It is free, with optional donations, on iPhone and Android. The trade-off is the same as UCLA Mindful's: it is a training program, not a library, and its sleep content is thinner than Insight Timer's or Medito's.

Frequently asked questions

Can a meditation app actually help with anxiety?

A little, for most people. A 2024 meta-analysis of 45 trials found that mindfulness apps reduced symptoms of depression and anxiety by a small amount compared with control groups. That is a real effect, but it is small — an app is a wind-down tool, not treatment. If anxiety interferes with daily life or sleep most nights, talk to a doctor or therapist; an app sits alongside that, not instead of it.

Which free app is best for falling asleep?

Insight Timer, because its free library includes thousands of sleep tracks — guided sleep meditations, sleep music and bedtime stories — alongside its daytime content. Medito also has free sleep stories and a timer with ambient sounds. If you fall asleep to a voice, Insight Timer's range wins; for a short structured wind-down, Medito is simpler.

Does Insight Timer really have 300,000 free tracks?

That is the number Insight Timer itself publishes: more than 300,000 free guided meditations, talks and music tracks from about 20,000 teachers, plus a free timer and live classes. The optional $60-a-year subscription adds offline listening, but the free library is the main product, not a sample.

Do I need an account for Medito?

No. Medito is run by a registered non-profit foundation in the Netherlands, it is free with no trial, no premium tier and no ads, and you do not even need to create an account. It includes guided meditations, courses including 30-day challenges, sleep stories, breathing exercises and an unguided timer with ambient sounds, with offline downloads.

What does Balance's free year actually cost later?

Balance has long offered new users the full app free for a year, then $69.99 a year or $11.99 a month, with a $399.99 lifetime option. The catch is that not everyone gets the free year: the maker's page just says “Try for free” without naming a length, and some sign-ups report getting a week or less. Check what your sign-up offers, and note the renewal date.

Key Takeaways

  • Best overall free app: Insight Timer — 300,000+ free tracks, sleep content included, and the free tier is the main product.
  • Best truly free app: Medito — no trial, no premium tier, no ads, no account needed.
  • Best for families: Smiling Mind — free, with sleep programs for kids as well as adults.
  • Best free course: UCLA Mindful — a structured mindfulness course that costs nothing.
  • Best science-based program: Healthy Minds Program — four-pillar skill training from a nonprofit.
  • Read the tier, not the listing: “free to download” can mean a 7-day trial or a starter pack — the five apps above are free forever.
  • Start with a course, not the search bar: 300,000 tracks is a stall, not a start.
  • Keep expectations honest: trials show small benefits for stress and anxiety — a real effect, and an app is a wind-down tool, not treatment.
