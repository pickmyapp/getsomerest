At a Glance: The Incline Sleep 100

Incline Sleep is a small company with one idea: sleep on a slope, with the whole bed tilted instead of bent. The Incline Sleep 100 is its tilt-only base — no head hinge, no foot hinge, just a flat platform that rises at the head end on a remote command.

  • Price: not published yet. Announced November 4, 2026; check InclineSleep.com for pricing when it goes on sale.
  • Tilt: remote-controlled elevation from 0 to 12 inches, anywhere in between — the whole sleeping surface tilts as one flat plane, so the mattress never folds.
  • Built for: latex and luxury mattresses, and side and back sleepers. Latex mattresses are heavy and do not bend well on hinged bases; the flat tilt avoids that problem entirely.
  • Support: 18 slats spaced 1.5 inches apart for airflow and stability, with a 1,200-pound weight capacity.
  • Warranty: 10 years.
  • Siblings: the Incline Sleep 500 adds head and foot adjustment, and a foam incline wedge fits under an existing mattress for people who want to try the slope first.

The design difference is easy to picture. A hinged base folds your mattress at the waist; the 100 lifts the whole bed like a ramp. For a latex mattress that weighs a lot and resists bending, that is not a minor detail — it is the difference between a base you can use and one you cannot.

How to Choose: Tilt, Hinge or Wedge?

Start with your mattress. If you sleep on latex — natural or blended — or on a luxury mattress with a rigid build, a hinged adjustable base is a bad match: the fold in the middle stresses the materials and can void the warranty. The Incline Sleep 100 exists for exactly that mattress. If your mattress is a standard foam or hybrid that bends happily, a hinged base gives you more positions for less money, and the tilt-only design buys you nothing.

Next, think about how you sleep. Side sleepers need a flat surface under their hips and shoulders, and a hinged base takes that away the moment the head rises — the bend lands right where your hips are. The Incline Sleep 100 keeps the surface flat at every height, which is why the announcement calls out side sleepers alongside back sleepers. If you mostly sleep on your back and want head-up reading and TV positions, a hinged base is the more flexible tool.

Then consider the experiment. Inclined sleeping is a real adjustment: some people love it from night one, others find the slope strange and slide down the bed. Incline Sleep's own foam wedge is the cheap way to find out — it slides under your existing mattress and gives you the slope without the purchase. If a week on the wedge feels good, the 100 is the permanent, adjustable version. If it does not, you have spent wedge money, not base money.

Finally, wait for the price. No price has been published, and a base with a motor, a remote and a 10-year warranty will not be cheap. Until Incline Sleep lists it, the honest comparison is against hinged bases you can buy today — and on value alone, a good hinged base wins for most sleepers. The 100 wins only where the flat tilt matters: latex, luxury builds, and side sleepers who want elevation without a bend.

Our Verdict: Who Should Buy It

The Incline Sleep 100 solves a narrow problem very cleanly. If you own a latex or luxury mattress that cannot bend, and you want the head of your bed raised without folding the mattress, there is almost nothing else that does this: hinged bases fold, wedges are not adjustable, and stacking pillows is not a system. A flat 0-to-12-inch tilt, a remote, 18 slats for airflow and support, and a 1,200-pound capacity is a thoughtful spec sheet for that job.

The case against buying it now is the missing price and the narrow use. A tilt-only base gives up the head-and-foot positions that make adjustable bases useful for reading, watching TV and relieving pressure — and if your mattress bends fine, a hinged base does more for less. The 10-year warranty is reassuring, but it covers a first-generation product from a small company, and there are no reviews yet to say whether the motor is quiet or the remote is reliable.

Our take: if you sleep on latex, sleep on your side, and want elevation without a bend, put the Incline Sleep 100 on your shortlist and wait for the price — then compare it against a good hinged base before you commit. If you are unsure inclined sleeping will suit you at all, buy the foam wedge first and try the slope for a week. And if your mattress bends, skip the tilt entirely: a hinged base is the better buy.

What is incline bed therapy?

Incline bed therapy means sleeping with the whole bed raised at the head end, so your body rests on a gentle slope rather than flat. The announcement notes that bed elevation is a common recommendation for people with conditions like nighttime acid reflux or sleep apnea — that is the manufacturer's framing, not medical advice. If you are considering inclined sleeping for a health reason, talk to your clinician first. As a comfort choice, the appeal is simpler: some people find a slight incline more comfortable for breathing, reading and relaxing before sleep.

How is the Incline Sleep 100 different from a normal adjustable base?

A normal adjustable base hinges in the middle: the head rises and the mattress folds at the bend, which latex and some luxury mattresses do not tolerate well. The Incline Sleep 100 tilts the entire sleeping surface as one flat plane, from 0 to 12 inches, so the mattress never bends. That is why it is aimed at latex and luxury mattresses — and at side sleepers, who need a flat surface under their hips and shoulders even when the bed is raised.

Will the Incline Sleep 100 work with my mattress?

It is designed for latex and luxury mattresses, which are usually the ones that cannot bend on a hinged base. The base has 18 slats spaced 1.5 inches apart, which support the mattress evenly and leave gaps for airflow, and it supports up to 1,200 pounds including sleepers. If your mattress is a standard hinged-base-compatible model, a regular adjustable base with head and foot movement will give you more positions for less money — the Incline Sleep 100's advantage is specifically the flat tilt.

And a foam incline wedge fits under an existing mattress, for people who want to try inclined sleeping first without buying a base.
