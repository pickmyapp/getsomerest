Eight Sleep Pod 6 vs Pod 5: What's New and Should You Upgrade? (2026)

Eight Sleep's Pod 6 (from $1,999) is smaller, faster and far more sensor-dense than the Pod 5 — and cheaper to get into. We compare the hardware, the prices and the subscription math so you know whether to upgrade.

Share
8 min read
By GetSome Rest editorial team · Independent sleep product guides Published Oct 15, 2026 · Last updated Oct 15, 2026

Eight Sleep launched the Pod 6 on September 23, 2026, calling it the biggest hardware launch in the company's history. The new system keeps the core formula — a water-circulating mattress cover driven by a bedside hub — but rebuilds nearly everything around it: a hub half the size that slides under the bed, nine times the biometric sensors, five-minute priming, and a new Solo configuration that finally brings the price of entry down to $1,999.

Quick answer

New buyers should get the Pod 6: it starts at $1,999 (Solo) or $2,699 (Standard), versus about $4,099 for the Pod 5 Plus system, and improves on the Pod 5 in every measurable way — a 50%-smaller hub, 550W vs 400W thermal engine, 18 sensors vs 2, and 5-minute priming vs 20. Pod 5 owners should only upgrade if they want the smaller hub, faster temperature response or Solo sizing; your existing Autopilot membership carries across. Prices checked October 2026.

The short version: should you upgrade?

This is really two different questions, and they have different answers.

If you don't own a Pod yet: the Pod 6 is the one to buy, full stop. The entry price dropped from roughly $4,099 for the cheapest Pod 5 Plus system to $1,999 for the new Pod 6 Solo — and the Solo isn't a stripped-down toy, it keeps the same cover technology and sensor array. Even the full-bed Pod 6 Standard at $2,699 is cheaper than the Pod 5 cover at every size, while being faster and more sensor-dense.

If you already own a Pod 5: the honest answer is that your system is still excellent. You already have dual-zone water-based cooling and heating plus Autopilot, and your Autopilot membership carries across to the Pod 6, so nothing you're paying for goes to waste. The upgrade buys you a hub that fits under the bed instead of beside it, priming that drops from 20 minutes to 5, temperature relief about 20% faster, and much more precise sleep tracking from 9 sensors per side instead of 1. Worth it for the convenience and the speed; not essential for the sleep itself.

What's new in the Pod 6

Eight Sleep says the Pod 6 was rebuilt from the ground up. Four changes matter most.

A hub that finally fits under the bed

The Hub — the box that pumps temperature-controlled water through the cover — is 50% shorter than the Pod 5's unit. That's not a spec-sheet nicety; it means the hub can slide under most standard bed frames instead of sitting beside the bed taking up floor space and visual real estate. Eight Sleep also redesigned the thermal engine inside it: redesigned thermoelectric coolers (TECs), optimized airflow, and a jump from 400W to 550W of thermal power, which delivers temperature adjustments about 20% faster than any previous Pod.

Nine times the biometric sensors

The Pod 5 cover carried 2 biometric sensors (one per side); the Pod 6 cover packs 18 (nine per side). That density buys spatial resolution the system never had: it can distinguish the signals of two people sleeping close together, and separate a sleeper's signal from a pet or child on the bed. Heart rate, heart rate variability, breathing rate, snoring and sleep-stage tracking all improve, with Eight Sleep claiming near parity with medical-grade references. The denser data also feeds Autopilot, the AI engine that adjusts temperature automatically based on sleep stage, environment and activity — no manual input needed.

Five-minute priming — and 20% faster relief

Priming — the time the system takes to bring the cover to your target temperature before bed — drops from 20 minutes on the Pod 5 to 5 minutes on the Pod 6. Overnight, the thermal engine relieves night sweats and chills up to 20% quicker. For genuinely hot sleepers, this is the most meaningful upgrade: the system reacts to a temperature spike before you wake up, instead of catching up afterward.

Solo sizes — the Pod for one sleeper

For the first time, you can buy a Pod that doesn't assume a couple. Solo sizes fit Twin and Twin XL beds, and one side of a Queen, King or California King — ideal for a teenager, a single sleeper in a big bed, or a split-mattress setup. The Solo drops the dual-zone controls (one sleeper needs one zone) and starts at $1,999 — the lowest starting price in Eight Sleep's history. Note that every Pod 6, Solo included, still requires an Autopilot subscription, priced at $199–$399/year depending on tier.

Price and subscription math

The Pod 6 is cheaper to buy and similar to run. Here's the honest five-year math.

A Pod 6 Standard at $2,699 plus the base Autopilot tier at $199/year costs about $2,898 in year one and roughly $3,694 over five years. The Pod 6 Solo at $1,999 plus $199/year lands around $2,198 in year one and about $2,794 over five — undercutting the Pod 5 Plus system ($4,099 + $199/year = ~$4,298 in year one, ~$5,095 over five) by more than two thousand dollars over five years.

Two things to be honest about: the subscription is not optional — every Pod 6 requires an Autopilot membership for full features — and the higher Autopilot tiers run up to $399/year, which changes the math meaningfully if you want them. Frequently asked questions

How much does the Eight Sleep Pod 6 cost?

The Pod 6 Solo starts at $1,999 and the full-bed Pod 6 Standard starts at $2,699 (up to about $2,999 depending on mattress size). Every Pod 6 also requires an Autopilot subscription at $199–$399/year. Prices last checked October 15, 2026.

Should I upgrade from the Pod 5 to the Pod 6?

Only if you'll feel the differences: a hub that fits under the bed, priming that drops from 20 minutes to 5, temperature relief about 20% faster, and far more precise sleep tracking from 18 sensors instead of 2. Your Pod 5 hardware and Autopilot membership still work — nothing is being switched off.

Does my Pod 5 Autopilot subscription work on the Pod 6?

Yes. Eight Sleep has confirmed that an existing Autopilot membership carries across to the Pod 6, so current Pod 5 owners don't lose their subscription when they upgrade.

Is the Pod 6 a medical device?

No. Eight Sleep states that the Pod is not a cleared medical device; its sleep and biometric tracking is presented for wellness and personal insight. The company has said it is pursuing regulatory clearance for sleep apnea in some markets, but as of October 2026 the Pod diagnoses and treats nothing.

Pod 6 Solo vs Standard: which should I buy?

Buy the Solo if you sleep alone — it's one-zone, fits Twin/Twin XL or one side of a larger bed, and starts at $1,999. Buy the Standard if you share a bed, because it's the only configuration with dual-zone per-side temperature and tracking.

Key takeaways

  • The Pod 6's biggest changes: a hub 50% smaller (fits under the bed), 550W vs 400W thermal power, 18 sensors vs 2, and 5-minute priming vs 20.
  • New buyers should choose the Pod 6 — Solo from $1,999, Standard from $2,699 — since it's cheaper and better than the ~$4,099 Pod 5 Plus system in every way.
  • Pod 5 owners can sit this one out unless they want the smaller hub, faster response or Solo sizing; their Autopilot membership carries across.
  • Every Pod 6 requires an Autopilot subscription ($199–$399/year) — factor it into the five-year cost before you buy.
