The Short Answer: 5 Cooling Weighted Blankets

A cooling weighted blanket has to do two things that fight each other: press down with even weight, and let heat out. Sealed bead blankets do the first and fail the second. The five picks below are the ones that manage both — one by removing the beads entirely, the others with bamboo fabrics and even bead distribution.

Our five picks: the Bearaby Tree Napper at $249 to $379 is the best all-round buy for genuinely hot sleepers; the YnM Cooling Bamboo Weighted Blanket at about $70 is the best budget pick; the Aricove Cooling Weighted Blanket at $129.95 is the best mid-price pick for night sweats; the Mosaic Weighted Blanket with bamboo cover at $206.99 to $298.99 is the best exact-weight build; and the Luxome Cooling Weighted Blanket at $156 on sale has the softest fabric in the category.

If you only remember one thing: buy construction, not fabric. An open knit or a well-vented build stays cool all night; a bamboo cover on a sealed blanket does not.

What Actually Keeps a Weighted Blanket Cool?

Most weighted blankets are a cotton shell divided into small pockets, each filled with glass micro-beads. The pockets keep the weight even; the sealed shell keeps the heat in. Cooling versions change one of three things — the fabric, the fill, or the whole construction — and only the third one changes the night.

Construction beats fabric

A bamboo viscose cover feels cool to the touch and wicks moisture, which is why nearly every cooling blanket in this guide uses one. But a cool cover on a sealed bead blanket is a first-hour fix: once your body heat has soaked into the beads, the blanket holds it. The Bearaby Tree Napper is the exception in this guide because it has no beads and no sealed shell — it is a chunky open knit, and air moves through the gaps in the knit all night.

Fill: glass beads, and the weight they carry

Glass micro-beads are the standard fill because they are dense, quiet and washable. They also hold heat. More, smaller pockets distribute beads evenly and feel less lumpy. The Bearaby avoids the problem entirely by using the knit itself as the weight: no fill to shift, no fill to trap heat.

Weight: the 10% rule of thumb

The usual guidance is about 10% of your body weight — 15 lb for a 150 lb sleeper, 20 lb for 200 lb. All five picks come in 15, 20 and 25 lb, and the Mosaic can be built to any exact pound from 5 to 25 lb in 1-lb steps. If you are between sizes, size down: a blanket that is too heavy feels hot and restrictive, and you can always add a light sheet.

Washing: the question people forget to ask

Hot sleepers sweat, and a blanket you cannot wash becomes a problem within months. The Bearaby knits, the YnM and the Aricove are machine washable; the Luxome has a removable cover you wash while the weighted inner stays dry; the Mosaic is hand-wash on most configurations. Check the care label before you buy.

One honesty note: nobody in this category publishes lab-measured surface-temperature data, so what follows is construction fact plus verified pricing. If the rest of the bed sleeps hot too, our cooling sheets guide and cooling pillows under $50 cover the other layers — and our weighted blanket research roundup covers what the studies actually found about the weight itself.

Frequently asked questions

Are cooling weighted blankets actually cooler than regular ones?

Yes, but only if the construction changes — not just the fabric. A bamboo cover on a sealed bead blanket feels cool for the first hour, then warms up. The blankets that stay cool all night are the ones built to breathe: the Bearaby's open knit has no beads and no sealed shell at all, so air moves through it. Fabric helps; construction decides.

What weight weighted blanket should I choose?

The usual rule of thumb is about 10% of your body weight — 15 lb for a 150 lb sleeper, 20 lb for 200 lb. Most picks come in 15, 20 and 25 lb; the Mosaic can be built to any exact pound from 5 to 25 lb. If you are between sizes, size down: a blanket that is too heavy feels hot and restrictive, and you can always add a light sheet.

Can I wash a cooling weighted blanket?

It depends on the build. The Bearaby knits, the YnM and the Aricove are all machine washable, which is a real advantage if you sleep hot and sweat. The Mosaic is hand-wash on most configurations because of its custom construction, and the Luxome has a removable cover you can wash while the weighted inner stays dry.

Are weighted blankets safe for children?

Manufacturers generally say weighted blankets are for adults and older children only, and never for children under 3 or anyone who cannot remove the blanket themselves. If you are buying for a child, check the maker's age guidance on the product page and choose the lightest weight they recommend. This is a product safety note, not medical advice — ask your pediatrician if you are unsure.

Do cooling weighted blankets help with night sweats?

They help with the heat part, not the sweating itself. A breathable blanket moves warm air away from your body instead of trapping it, so you wake up less hot — and the Aricove is specifically recommended by reviewers for menopausal night sweats. If night sweats are new, severe, or come with other symptoms, that is a conversation for your doctor, not a blanket.
