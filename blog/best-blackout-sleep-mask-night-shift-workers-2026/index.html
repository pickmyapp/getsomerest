Best Blackout Sleep Mask for Night Shift Workers (2026)

The Short Answer: 5 Blackout Masks for Day Sleep

Night shift sleep fails on light first, and a mask is the fastest way to fix it. A contoured cup mask seals around your eyes and blocks the noon sun; a flat mask shades it. All five block enough light for day sleep — they differ on blackout, comfort over eight hours, and price.

Our five picks: the Manta Pro Sleep Mask at $68 on sale is the best all-round buy; the Manta Sleep Mask at $31.20 is the same idea for less; the Nidra Deep Rest Eye Mask at $28 is the best value contoured mask; the MZOO Sleep Mask at $19.99 is the cheapest cup design; and the Alaska Bear Silk Sleep Mask at $8.49 is the lightest, cheapest mask that still works.

If you only remember one thing: buy contoured cups for daily day sleep. Flat masks are for travel and naps.

What Makes a Mask Work at Noon?

A mask that works in a dark bedroom at midnight can fail completely at noon. Daylight is brighter, it comes from every angle, and you are trying to sleep when your body expects to be awake — so the seal has to be better and the fit has to survive eight hours of turning over. Here is what actually matters.

Contoured cups vs flat masks

Contoured masks have hollow cups that sit over your eyes without touching them. The cups seal against your brow and cheekbones, which is where flat masks leak. They also let you blink and open your eyes inside the mask, so there is no pressure on your eyelids and no waking up to peel the mask off. Flat masks drape across your face, press on your lashes, and leave gaps at the nose bridge. They are lighter and cooler, and they pack smaller — but for a full day sleep after a night shift, contoured wins.

The nose bridge is where blackout fails

Almost every light leak in a sleep mask happens at the nose. Look for a mask with a nose baffle — a small padded flap that fills the gap — or cups deep enough to bridge over it. The Manta Pro and the Nidra both seal here; the MZOO has a nose baffle too, though its seal is less forgiving if the strap is loose.

Strap design decides whether it stays on

A mask that slides off at 2 a.m. is a mask that does not work. Velcro straps adjust precisely but can catch hair; buckle straps are quieter and hold their setting for weeks. Dual straps — one above the ear, one below — stay put better than a single band, which is why the Nidra's dual sliding-buckle straps score well with side sleepers.

Side sleepers need cups that do not collapse

When you roll onto your side, your cheek presses the mask against the pillow. Shallow cups collapse and push into your eye; deep, stiff cups hold their shape. This is the biggest difference between the $68 Manta Pro and a $10 flat mask. If you are a strict back sleeper, the cheaper masks are fine.

Weight, heat and washing

A mask you wear for eight hours should weigh almost nothing — the Alaska Bear is 0.7 oz and disappears on your face. Silk and cotton breathe; foam cups trap a little warmth. Every mask in this guide is machine washable, Air-dry contoured masks so the foam keeps its shape.

One more piece of the setup: a mask fixes light, not sound. If daytime noise is also waking you, pair it with earplugs — our night shift sleep schedule guide covers the full routine, and blackout curtains for shift workers handle the room itself.

Frequently asked questions

Can a sleep mask really block daylight completely?

A contoured mask with deep eye cups can get very close — reviewers describe opening their eyes inside a Manta Pro and seeing nothing, even at noon. Flat masks cannot do the same: light leaks at the nose bridge and temples, so they shade rather than black out. For true day sleep after a night shift, buy a contoured cup design, not a flat one.

Is the Manta Pro worth $68 over the $31 Manta Sleep Mask?

For daily day sleep, yes. The Pro's C-shaped cups are deeper and seal better against the brow and cheekbones, and both the cups and the strap adjust to your face, so it holds its seal on side sleepers and people with long lashes. The original Manta at $31.20 is the same idea with shallower cups — fine for travel and occasional use, and the better buy if you only wear a mask a few nights a week.

Which sleep mask is best for side sleepers on night shift?

A contoured mask with adjustable cups, either the Manta Pro ($68 on sale) or the Nidra Deep Rest ($28). Both keep the fabric off your eyeballs, so rolling onto your side does not push the mask into your eyes. Flat silk masks like the Alaska Bear are comfortable on your back, but they tend to slip or gap at the nose when you turn sideways.

Do blackout masks get hot?

Flat silk and cotton masks breathe well and rarely feel warm. Contoured masks with foam cups trap a little more heat around the eyes, and the Manta Pro's sealed cups are the warmest of our picks. If you sleep hot, pair a contoured mask with a cool room and light bedding, or choose the Alaska Bear silk mask and accept a small amount of light leak in exchange for airflow.

How often should I wash a sleep mask?

Wash it weekly if you wear it every day — sweat and skin oils build up fast against your face. Most of our picks are machine washable on a gentle cycle. Air-dry them; a hot dryer warps the foam in contoured cups.
