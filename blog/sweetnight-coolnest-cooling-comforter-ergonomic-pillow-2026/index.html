Quick answer

For hot sleepers, the pillow is the better first buy; add the comforter if you also wake up sweating under your current bedding.

At a Glance: Comforter and Pillow

SweetNight expanded the CoolNest series in August 2026, adding four bedding products around its four CoolNest mattresses: the Ergonomic Cooling Pillow, the Cooling Sheet Set, the Cooling Comforter, and a Cooling Eye Mask. The comforter and pillow are the two pieces most hot sleepers will actually use every night, and both share the same CoolGlide cooling fabric.

  • CoolNest Cooling Comforter: $129 Queen, $149 King. A lightweight comforter with a CoolGlide cooling fabric surface rated Q-Max 0.4 — a higher cooling rating than the mattress cover — and a breathable fill blend of plant-based viscose, Sorona fiber, and airy polyester. OEKO-TEX certified.
  • CoolNest Ergonomic Cooling Pillow: $79, or $129 for two (comparable value listed at $152.99). Same CoolGlide cooling case, a 420-pod cooling matrix inside, and a dual-height memory foam core with 3.1-inch and 3.9-inch sides for back and side sleepers. Removable, washable cover.
  • The system idea: mattress cover, comforter, pillow, and sheets all using compatible cooling fabrics, so no single layer traps the heat the others shed.

If you are shopping for the mattress too, read our CoolNest Ultra mattress explainer first — the bedding makes the most sense as an add-on to a CoolNest bed, but it works on any mattress.

How to Choose: Pillow, Comforter, or Both

Buy for the place you actually feel hot. That sounds obvious, but cooling bedding is often bought as a set when only one layer is the problem, and the cheaper single purchase usually solves it.

Start with the pillow if your head and neck run hot. The Ergonomic Cooling Pillow is the more engineered of the two products: the CoolGlide case gives instant cool-to-the-touch feel, the 420-pod cooling matrix spreads heat inside the pillow, and the dual-height core lets back sleepers use the 3.1-inch side and side sleepers flip to the 3.9-inch side. At $79 — or $64.50 each in the two-pack — it is also the lower-risk experiment. The removable, washable cover matters here: a cooling pillow that cannot be washed will not stay fresh for long.

Add the comforter if you wake up sweating under your covers. A lightweight comforter with a Q-Max 0.4 surface is meaningfully cooler to the touch than standard cotton bedding, and the breathable viscose, Sorona, and polyester fill is designed to let air move rather than trap it. At $129 for a Queen, it undercuts most premium cooling comforters, and it is the piece that changes how the whole bed feels rather than just the pillow zone.

Buy both if you are a committed hot sleeper rebuilding your bedding. The two-pack pillow deal plus a Queen comforter comes to $258, which is still less than a single premium cooling mattress topper, and the shared CoolGlide fabric means the layers work together instead of fighting each other. For more hot-sleeper bedding, see our cooling sheets guide and cooling blanket comparison. You can also find the pillow via Amazon search.

The Q-Max number deserves a plain explanation: it measures how quickly a fabric pulls heat from skin on contact, so a higher Q-Max feels colder the moment you touch it. The comforter's 0.4 rating is a genuine cool-touch fabric, not just a marketing word — and SweetNight notes it feels colder than the mattress cover itself, which is the point of layering the system rather than relying on the mattress alone. The pillow's 420-pod matrix is the same idea in three dimensions: small cooling pods spread across the pillow so heat does not pool in one spot under your head.

Our Verdict: Who Should Buy What

Most hot sleepers should buy the pillow first. It is cheaper, its dual-height design serves both back and side sleepers, and a pillow is the layer your skin contacts most directly all night. The two-pack at $129 is the better value if you sleep with two pillows or share a bed.

Buy the comforter if your current comforter is the problem. Heavy, synthetic-filled comforters are the most common cause of waking up sweaty under otherwise fine bedding, and a lightweight $129 Queen replacement is a straightforward fix. Check the King price ($149) if you share a larger bed — the price step is small for the extra coverage.

Skip both if you sleep cold or already own cooling bedding you like. Cooling fabric only helps if heat is your problem; cold sleepers will find a Q-Max 0.4 surface unpleasantly chilly, and there is no reason to replace bedding that already works. Put the money toward a better pillow shape or a mattress topper instead.

How the Cooling System Fits Together

The comforter and pillow make more sense inside SweetNight's larger 2026 story. The CoolNest series now spans four mattresses — the Memory Foam, Hybrid, Pro, and Ultra models — and the August 2026 expansion added the bedding pieces that let the brand sell a complete cool bed rather than a single mattress. The Ultra, reviewed as the 2026 flagship with a 5-stage cooling system and 7-zone support, sits at the top of that range, while the standard CoolNest Memory Foam mattress has earned Sleep Foundation recognition as a best cooling mattress in 2026 lists.

That context is useful for buyers: SweetNight is not a one-product brand testing a cooling idea, but a bedding company building a full system around it, with prices that stay well below luxury cooling brands. The bedding pieces are also the lowest-commitment way into the system — $79 or $129 against $399.99 and up for a CoolNest mattress — which is exactly how a system should be sold: try the pillow, and if the cooling feel works for you, the mattress becomes an easier next purchase.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the SweetNight CoolNest Cooling Comforter cost?

The Cooling Comforter costs $129 for a Queen and $149 for a King, as reported in 2026 product coverage. Prices may vary during sales; SweetNight offered discounts of up to 45% sitewide during its Labor Day sale.

How much does the CoolNest Ergonomic Cooling Pillow cost?

The pillow costs $79 for one, or $129 for two. SweetNight lists a comparable value of $152.99 for the pillow, and the two-pack is the better per-pillow value.

What makes the CoolNest comforter cooling?

Its CoolGlide fabric surface is rated Q-Max 0.4, meaning it pulls heat from skin quickly on contact and feels colder to the touch than the mattress cover. The fill blends plant-based viscose, Sorona fiber, and airy polyester for breathability, and the comforter is OEKO-TEX certified.

Is the Ergonomic Cooling Pillow good for side sleepers?

Yes — its dual-height memory foam core has a 3.1-inch side for back sleepers and a 3.9-inch side for side sleepers, so you flip the pillow to the height that suits your position. The cover is removable and washable.

Do I need the CoolNest mattress for the bedding to work?

No. The comforter and pillow work on any mattress, and they are the cheapest way to try SweetNight's cooling fabric. They pair best with a CoolNest mattress as part of the full Cooling Sleep System, but they do not require one.
